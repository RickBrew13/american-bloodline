Life Story

Ransom Davis was born about 1840 in Muhlenberg County, Kentucky, the son of Gasham Davis and Permelia A. Whitaker — a link carried as supported, not proved: it rests on his place in his widowed mother's 1850 and 1860 census households and on his naming among her children in the Davis Family Bible record, transcribed in 1961–62. He must not be confused with his uncle Ransom Davis, born 1813, his father's brother. He grew up on Mud River, where his father had bought 150 acres in 1831. In July 1863 he registered for the Civil War draft in Muhlenberg County, aged 23, a farmer; no service followed, and no enlistment record has been found.

On 16 June 1861 Ransom married Sarah A. E. Pittman in Muhlenberg County; the minister's return survives and spells them "Ranson Davis & Sarah A Pitman," married at the house of J. Hunt by Abner Wood. They raised five children — Amanda, Jesse, William, Jerusha, and Balaam — first on Mud River, then, after 20 August 1875, on 83 acres of Bone Camp Creek in Morgan County, Tennessee, bought from Sarah's brother Ephraim Pitman for five dollars. Ransom sat as a Justice of the Peace for Morgan County's Sixth District from about 1881 until his last court appearance on 2 July 1888. His daughter Amanda married James Alexander McKinney in 1881, and his son William married Farsina Brewster in 1888 — the daughter of the Rev. John W. Brewster and Rebecca Farr — tying the Davis and Brewster lines a generation before Amanda's daughter Mary Ellen married William Alexander Brewster.

Ransom died after 2 July 1888 and by 1890, when the Morgan County tax list names the "Ransom Davis heirs"; the exact date, place, and burial are unproven. Sarah paid the tax on the 83 acres in 1891 and died between then and the 1896–97 division of the land among the heirs. No photograph of Ransom has been found. The Davis Family Bible transcription, the deed, the marriage return, and the census trail carry this family; the rest — his exact death, both burials, and the fates of Jesse and Jerusha after 1880 — remains open.

Research Notes and Open Questions

  • Grades on this page: DOCUMENTED means an original record was read (the minister's return, the 1875 deed, the draft registration, the Cox household image, the Balaam death certificate, the Duncan deed image); SUPPORTED means a claim attested by filed research — censuses, tax lists, compiled studies, the signed-in tree checks — but not by a register read here; UNPROVED means a claim carried from the research folder with no corroboration found.
  • Death: after 2 July 1888 and by 1890. The couple profile carried "by 1891"; the 1890 Morgan County tax list naming "Ransom Davis heirs" tightens it to 1890. Gough's statement that he died in Muhlenberg County is uncited and unproven — Morgan County was his home and office. Exact date, place, and burial unproven.
  • Marriage: 16 June 1861 is documented by the minister's return. The 18 June date in Gough, Anderson, and other compiled sources is an error, contradicted by the return.
  • Amanda's first marriage: Ben McCurran, divorced by 1880. Confirmed by the 1880 Charles Cox household image read (District 7, ED 132 — not ED 133 as the folder had it — sheet 551A: Amanda Davis, 18, marital status "D", Servant). This explains her absence from her parents' 1880 household. Gough's "Bunn McCurran" form is unverified. Her middle name "Luverna" is family tradition only and is not adopted.
  • Balaam Agy Davis: the birth is a genuine conflict. The censuses and the draft-card reconstruction give 1 May 1879, Sunbright, son of Ransom × Sarah; his Tennessee death certificate, read directly, gives 2 May 1871, parents "Erunce Davis" and "Eliza Spurling," and was filed 9 November 1946 — seven months after his death — by his widow Francis Davis. A delayed certificate filed by a widow can carry an informant's error, and the 1879 date is supported by his daughter Rachel Cora's 1926 birth record, which states her father was 47 — but neither date is settled here. His death, 8 April 1946, and burial at Union Grove Cemetery, Burrville, rest on the certificate.
  • William Davis: born September 1870 or 1871 — Gough's own pages disagree, and the 1900 census reportedly gives September 1871; the 1900 census image settles it and has not been read here. He married Farsina Brewster 5 October 1888, daughter of the Rev. John W. Brewster × Rebecca Farr. Lore, unbacked but with a named chain: his deathbed vision of yellow birds, about 1902 (1972 Wilma Crabtree Gibson interview with Andy Marion Brewster, transcribed 1997 to Gough).
