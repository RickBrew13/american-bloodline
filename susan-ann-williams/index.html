Life Story

Susan Ann Williams was born on 12 February 1838 at Clear Fork, the daughter of John Williams Sr. and Susanna Stover, and grew up in a household of seventeen children on Little Whitestick Creek near Shumate Dairy. She married Hughes Jefferson Warden on 4 April 1852, when she was fourteen, by Rev. Claiborne Curtis — the first minister of the Christian Church in the region. Eight Warden children were born to them between 1853 and about 1864, and the third, Letitia, carries the line.

Hughes was dead by 9 November 1865, and Susan remarried that day to Max Baldenburg, a German immigrant, the son of Martin and Regen Baldenburg. A ninth child, Martin, was born to them in 1869. The 1870 census finds her head of household at 33, with all seven children present indexed under her second husband’s surname, and the 1880 census finds her at 42, listed as “married,” keeping house with four sons — though Max himself appears in neither census, and whether he was dead by 1880 or simply absent is not settled. In 1899 she married a third time, to John J. Jordan of Lincoln County. No record of her after that marriage has been found, and her death, carried as about 1904, rests on the compilations alone.

Her life is best documented by the lawsuit she brought as Hugh’s widow — Baldenberg v. Warden, 14 W.Va. 397 (1878) — over the White Stick land her father-in-law Thomas had promised Hugh by word of mouth. She swore that Hugh had wanted to go to Indiana and she was unwilling, that Thomas had prevailed on him to stay and agreed to make him a title, and that she found the plat and certificate of survey among Hugh’s papers after his death. The circuit court ruled for her in 1875; the Supreme Court reversed and remanded. She is buried nowhere known, and no portrait of her has been found.

Research Notes and Open Questions

  • Grades: DOCUMENTED means an original record read directly says it; SUPPORTED means several records point to it; FAMILY ACCOUNT means the family’s own telling; UNPROVED means a lead, not proved. Every fact on this page carries its grade.
  • Birth and death (C7): born 12 February 1838, Clear Fork — supported (DePauk; FamilySearch, 4 sources; the 1850 census age 12). The birthplace is given as Clear Fork, Fayette County, Virginia (DePauk) against “Clear Fork, Wyoming, West Virginia” (FamilySearch; WikiTree) — the Wyoming County label is wrong, since Clear Fork of Coal River is in Raleigh/Fayette County, not Wyoming County — and “Clear Creek” (Geni). Died about 1904 — UNPROVED: the compilations agree, but no death record has been found under Jordan or Baldenburg, and her FamilySearch death has no sources. An older lore header’s “25 November 1837/38” birth and “9 November 1927, Charleston” death are unsourced and are not used.
  • Her mother (C8): Susanna Stover stands on Riffe, the 1885 account, DePauk, and the 23 July 1821 Giles County marriage index — and on the Each Generation page, which lists John Williams Sr. × Susanna Stover. An older compiled account carries a different maiden name for Susanna — no record anywhere supports it, and it is retired here. The name in question enters the Williams line one generation later, through the marriages of Susan’s nieces Jane and Susan Davis in 1874 and 1879.
  • John Williams Sr. (C9): born 10 September 1802 — Tennessee per the 1850 census and DePauk, Giles County, Virginia per WikiTree’s profile vitals (contradicted by its own census table) and per his death record (birthplace “Giles Co., Va.”, birth “1803” estimated). Died 24 July 1885, Raleigh County, age 82 (West Virginia Deaths, v.1 p.39) — the 1950 reprint’s “24 September 1885” is most likely a misreading of the account’s “departed this life the 24th ultimo.” Burial after 24 July 1885, Beckley. His FamilySearch portrait is a gravestone photograph; the Ancestry marker and death-record images are login-walled and unread.
  • John Sr.’s father (C10): David “Lonesome Dave” Williams × Mary Elizabeth McGriff is UNPROVED — the 1885 funeral account and the trees name him, but Turner met David in person in December 1857 and never called him John Sr.’s father. The chain past David — Jeremiah Williams, William Williams (1720 Wales – 1800) — is tree-only and is not decided here.
  • Max Baldenburg (C16): marriage documented; death UNPROVED. West and DePauk say he died before 1880, but the 1880 census lists Susan as “married” — either enumerator error or the compilations’ death date is wrong. Max is absent from the 1870 and 1880 censuses. His mother’s name in the register, “Regen,” is likely a transcription artifact (Regina?). No immigration or naturalization record has been found.
  • John J. Jordan (C17): marriage supported (marriage index and DePauk; Susan 60, Jordan 40, of Lincoln County, born about 1859 — not the compilations’ about 1856). FamilySearch does not carry the marriage and lists no John J. Jordan spouse for Susan. A John J. Jordan who served in Company A, 194th New York Infantry (Union) filed an invalid pension from West Virginia on 20 May 1913, and his widow Bernice D. Jordan filed on 3 March 1917 — he died between those dates — but identity with Susan’s husband is NOT established: the card names Bernice, not Susan, and no link to Raleigh County appears on it. No 1900 census record has been found for Susan and Jordan together.
  • Milton J. Warden’s paternity (C11): unresolved. He was born about 1863–1864, at the boundary of Hughes’s death and Susan’s remarriage; he is indexed “Baldenburg” in 1870 and “Warden” in 1880, and after 1880 he was an ore miner in Elizabeth Township, Lawrence County, Ohio, in a mining boarding household — no Warden family with him, and no later record has been found. He is listed here as the eighth child per the court opinion and DePauk, with this flag; he is not assigned to either man.
