Life Story

Thomas Benjamin Rich was born 14 July 1830 in Symmes Township, Hamilton County, Ohio, the son of John Rich and Sarah — a parentage supported at about 75–80% by the 1850 household image, which lists Thomas, 19, a teacher, in John and Sarah’s household of twelve children, but not proved. He grew up where Loveland straddles the county line: born in Hamilton County’s Symmes Township, he was living across the line in Clermont County’s Miami Township by 1870. He became a railroad conductor — the only occupation any record gives him — and on 8 January 1867 he married Barbara Goodpaster in Warren County. They had three sons: George, James Bernard, and Thomas Berry. He died 11 March 1892 at 4 a.m. at his residence, No. 174 Plum Street, Cincinnati, of consumption, aged 61 years and 8 months, and was buried from the vault at Miamiville on 17 April 1892. Five months later his eldest son George died at the same address, of peritonitis, aged 24. No portrait of Thomas has been found.

His name needs care. The records spell the surname both ways: his son James Bernard’s 1872 birth record calls the parents “Thomas B. Rick × Barbara Goodpaster”, while the 1870 and 1880 censuses spell it Rich, and Geni shows him as Thomas B. Rick. The middle initial B. is unresolved: FamilySearch calls him “Thomas Berry Rich”, but no record does — every record says Thomas Rich, T. B. Rich, Thomas B. Rich, Thomas B. Rick, or Thos. Rich. “Berry” reaches him only by conflation: his son, born 1874, is Thomas Berry Rich, and FamilySearch gives his mother as Sarah W. Berry, a maiden name no record supports. “Benjamin” is tree-level only — no record read so far gives it. He is carried here as Thomas Benjamin Rich, the tree form, with the B. unresolved.

His parents are the open question of this profile. John Rich, a farmer of Symmes Township, died in early 1875 — the Hamilton County Probate Journal records his inventory, Vol. 35, p. 404 — but the inventory contents have not been read, and whether Thomas is named as an heir is unknown. Sarah, John’s wife, was gone from the household by 1860. The 1880 census says Thomas’s father was born in Virginia and his mother in Kentucky, while John and Sarah were both Ohio-born in 1850 and 1860 — an honest contradiction, most likely the 1880 informant guessing wrong about in-law birthplaces. Three Thomas Riches must not be conflated: the Symmes pioneer Thomas (1767–1846) and his wife Mary Ann Berry, their son Thomas (born about 1804) and his wife Christina, and the Warren County Thomas (born about 1810) and his wife Christina Carr. None of their households holds our Thomas except John and Sarah’s.

In 1869 a “T. B. Rich” was Secretary of Emery Lodge No. 258 (F. & A. M.) at Loveland — supported, the identity strongly indicated but not proved. No Civil War service has been found for him, though he was of prime age, 30 to 34; no church affiliation has been found either. An old compiled account’s claim that he was a “blacksmith and railroad carpenter” has no record behind it — only the conductor is verified — and its circa-1905 death date is now superseded: the 1892 obituary and burial notice settle his death and burial.
