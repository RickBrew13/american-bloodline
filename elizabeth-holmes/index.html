Life Story

Elizabeth Holmes was born about 1799 in South Carolina and was last seen on 2 July 1860, a widow living in her daughter Louisa Parsons’ household in Cumberland County, Tennessee. She married William Farr about 1817, probably in North Carolina; no marriage record has been found. They moved to Wayne County, Kentucky about 1822, then to Clinton County, and in 1849 to Morgan County, Tennessee, on the Obed River. Ten children are carried; their daughter Rebecca carries the line, marrying John W. Brewster about 1848–49.

She could not read or write — the 1860 census marks her with an “I” — and no occupation is recorded for her in 1850 or 1860. No record places her in the pews of any church. By 1860 she was the widowed mother living in her daughter’s house, and after that date she vanishes from every free route: no 1870 Cumberland census entry, no death record, no stone. The “1875” death year on some tree profiles is unsourced.

Her maiden name is the hardest question on this page. “Holmes” is the working name, carried plain on this page by a settled ruling — but no record ties the name Holmes to this Elizabeth. The earliest occurrence anywhere is an unsourced 2005 user tree, and the compiled account that once repeated the name later dropped it, calling her “Elizabeth [last name unknown]”. The grand Charleston pedigree — William Walter Holmes Sr. × Margaret Peronneau, a Huguenot childhood, named siblings and eleven half-siblings — is contradicted: Margaret Peronneau’s documented husband is Charleston merchant John Edwards, not a William Walter Holmes. A real 1799 Charleston christening exists, for Elizabeth Harriet Holmes, daughter of William Holmes and Margaret, but the record gives Margaret no surname and no “Walter” or “Peronneau”, and it does not by itself prove that this Elizabeth is ours. Her daughter Vianna’s 1914 death certificate names her mother “Elizabeth Homes” — the strongest direct evidence in the file, and the family itself used “Homes”. A Find a Grave biography for Rebecca calls her mother “Elizabeth Webb”, from an unsourced 2008 memorial; the 1850 next-door neighbour John Webb is suggestive and nothing more. Her parents are unknown.

No portrait of Elizabeth is known — not in the research folder, and not on any tree site checked. No photograph, no stone, no signature survives for her; the 1860 census mark is the closest thing to her own hand.
