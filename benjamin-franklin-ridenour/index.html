Life Story

Benjamin Franklin Ridenour was born 28 March 1845 in Long Bottom, Meigs County, Ohio — so an old compiled account gives it — the son of Martin B. Ridenour, named as his father in his own death register, and, by reconstruction, Nancy Jane Hawley. The exact day and locality are UNPROVED: no record read gives them, though the year 1845 and Ohio are SUPPORTED by the stone, the death register, and the 1928 interview. He was five years old in the 1850 census at Letart, Meigs County, in his father Martin’s household. He married Emeline Smith on 14 April 1864 in Jackson County, in the middle of his Civil War service: the wedding fell during a three-month home leave, a story he told in his own words in a 1928 newspaper feature, “A Civil War Romance.”

He had enlisted in Company F of the Ninth West Virginia Infantry for a three-year term, mustered 28 February 1862 at the mouth of Mill Creek under Captain O. G. Chase. In his own telling, the first move was to Guyandotte, then Kanawha Falls, Meadow Bluff near Lewisburg, Summerville, and back on what was known as “Lightburn’s Retreat”; then Buckhannon, Beverly, Romney and Fayetteville. He was home three months instead of one because he could not get to his regiment — and it was during that stay at home that he married Emeline Smith. He rejoined at Martinsburg, then Sandy Hook, Harper’s Ferry, Winchester, Newton, Fisher’s Hill, and Cedar Creek, “which was the biggest battle of the war and the last one”; then Staunton, and back to Cumberland, Maryland, where they got their discharges in July 1865. Then they came back to Wheeling and got their pay — his being $552.50. One compiled service record card reads his company as Co. K, transferred 30 April 1862 from Co. I: Co. F against Co. K is still open, the full packet not read, and his Veteran company after the 9th consolidated with the 5th on 9 November 1864 is UNPROVED. The 1928 feature itself has not been found in full text free — Fisher’s 1977 articles quote it at length, from a photocopy held by a granddaughter, Mrs. Ruth Arnett of Birmingham, Alabama.

After the war the couple settled at Mount Alto, Jackson County, where Benjamin served for many years as minister of Hopewell Baptist Church, and they raised fifteen children — Alpha, Nancy Caroline, Elizabeth, Walter William, Mary Minerva, Hawley Ernest, Lewis Delver, Emory Martin, Iva, Cecil, Annie, Kenneth Franklin, Ida Mae, Cleophas, and Esta Virginia — all fifteen listed on the parents’ shared stone at Hopewell. Nancy Caroline carries the line, marrying Walter Weston Polsley. Benjamin was a farmer as well as a minister; his ordination date and his years at Hopewell have not been found.

Emeline died on 17 April 1930 at Mount Alto and was buried at Hopewell on 20 April. Benjamin died on 25 April 1932, aged eighty-seven, and was buried beside her under their shared stone. No portrait of the couple was known until the Find a Grave memorials were read: the same elderly-couple portrait sits on both spouses’ memorials, posted by arnold daugherty on 13 December 2011, with no caption naming the sitters. Its identification rests on that placement, and the same portrait is Benjamin’s main photo on Geni — it is carried here at SUPPORTED, and it is the hero of this page.
