Life Story

David W. Brown was born 15 September 1800 by the family register in the Lovett Reed pension file, read on the original in November 2026; the 1850 census gives about 1799 instead, and the two dates sit about a year apart. Tennessee is his birthplace by the census; a sworn statement made sixty years later says North Carolina, and the census controls. He called himself “of Roane” in 1827, when he sold sixty acres on Cumberland Mountain to Johnson Parham and five acres to Jesse Brown — both deeds in Bledsoe County, both witnessed by William Nail, the clergyman who also vouched for Lovett Reed. The deeds are documented; an old note in the research folder saying no land deed for David had been found is contradicted by them. He was a labourer.

He married Polly Reed, daughter of Lovett and Sibby Reed — the pension file names her twice over, in Sibby’s 1840 declaration and in David and Polly’s own January 1841 testimony. No marriage record survives, and the date is unproved: by about 1819 if the register’s Jackson, born 10 October 1819, is their child, otherwise in the 1820s. The 1850 census, taken 25 October 1850 in Bledsoe County, finds the household of eleven — David and Mary, both 51, with Margaret, Barbary, Lucy, Thomas, Larkin, Rebecca, Elizabeth, and the infant twins Stephen and Lucy, both five months old. He was gone by June 1860. No death record, grave, or estate has been found.

His parents are not listed on this page — no record names them. A pairing carried in the research folder, Thomas “Pompey” Brown × Lucy Gentry, has no record behind it: no record calls Thomas “Pompey”, the dates do not fit, and the one “Pompey Brown” on FamilySearch (1750–1778) is a different, impossible claim that FamilySearch itself flags. Five candidates were weighed and none reaches the bar. The one pull that might have settled it, the Marion County Deed Book A heir list for John Brown of Knox County, was read in full in November 2026 and carries no David. The 1840 Fentress candidate is withdrawn — the row at the cited position is “David B. Warren”, not David Brown.

Mary Catherine’s sworn 1907 application names her maternal great-grandfather Pompey Brown, “full-blooded Cherokee Indian (mother’s side)”. The oath says “Great Grandfather” — Mary’s own great-grandfather, that is, Lucy’s grandfather, father of Dave or of Polly. It does not say which side Pompey is on. Pompey is carried as sworn testimony only, not as a documented ancestor; an old compiled account adding a generation, calling Lucy Pompey’s great-granddaughter, is contradicted by the direct image read. No portrait of David exists, and no grave has been located.
