Life Story

Joel Casto was born about 1834 in Virginia, the son of George W. Casto × Sarah Ours, and married Mary M. Bradley on 17 September 1857 in Jackson County, when he was twenty-four and she was twenty. The marriage register writes her “Mary M Bradley”, names no middle name for her, and names the parents “Geo & Sarah” and “Jas & Nancy”. They settled at Staats Mills on Tug Fork, where the censuses find them from 1850 through 1880, Joel farming the land his father’s family had worked since George settled there in 1816.

Eight children are carried, and the censuses trace them household by household: Benjamin E., born about 1858, who died aged ten in 1868; Nancy Jane, born 15 July 1860, who married James M. Fisher in 1878 and carries the line; William Monroe, born 1863; Frederick Mintin, born 1865; Lougenia, born 1867; Okey Johnson, born 1870; Martha L., born 1872; and Lilly F., born 1876. In 1900, fourteen years after Joel’s death, his widow Mary answered the census-taker with eight children born and seven living — fitting these eight exactly, Benjamin E. the one dead.

No Union federal service is found for Joel. The 1890 Veterans Schedule for Jackson County, read directly, lists no Joel Casto among veterans and no widow Mary M. Casto among widows; the NPS Civil War Soldiers and Sailors System search found no Joel Casto; and no M508 Union service-record hit was found. A Joel Casto is listed in the Upshur County 133rd Militia roster, mustered 16 September 1862 at Buckhannon, but the roster gives no age or residence, so identity with our Joel is UNPROVED and no militia service is claimed here.

Joel died on 25 September 1886 in Jackson County, aged fifty-two, and is buried in Old Siniaville Cemetery at Staats Mills. His death rests on the cemetery transcription, read directly — “Casto, Joel Died Sept 25, 1886 Aged 52” — because no death-register entry for him has been found. No portrait of Joel exists anywhere, and his stone is fallen and broken, its inscription not legible, so the hero is a placeholder.
