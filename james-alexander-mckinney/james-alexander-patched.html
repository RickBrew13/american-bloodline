Life Story

James Alexander McKinney was born in April 1857 in Tennessee, the son of Elizabeth "Betsy" McKinney. His father has never been named in any record found — the line is a true brick wall, and no telling of his identity has surfaced anywhere, including in the family letters asked about as late as October 2026. In 1867, when James was ten, his mother married Ezekiel Cochran, and James grew up in his stepfather's household: the 1880 census of Morgan County lists him there as "Alexander McKinney," relationship "stepson," alongside his grandmother Catherine McKinney, aged eighty-seven.

On 31 January 1881, in Morgan County, he married Amanda J. Davis, the daughter of Ransom Davis and Sarah A. E. Pitman, who had settled on Bone Camp Creek when Ransom bought eighty-three acres there in 1875. James and Amanda raised their children on that land in Morgan and Scott Counties. In 1883 James bought one hundred acres on Alley's Creek jointly with his stepbrother Lincoln Cochran, and in 1898 the Morgan County tax list charged him for twenty-seven and a half acres — exactly one-third of his father-in-law's Bone Camp Creek tract, her brother William having sold his third in 1896.

Amanda died about 1893, by family tradition only — no death record or grave has been found — and James married second, about 1893, Beulah Pittman, whom the 1900 census lists as "Buny," married seven years. That census, taken in Scott County, shows James alive at forty-three as "Aleck," living with his widowed mother Betsy, who reported one child born and one living, and with Beulah and eight children in the household. He was absent from the 1910 census, so he died between 1900 and 1910; no death record or grave has been found. No photograph of James has been found.
