Life Story

Sarah A. E. Pittman was born about 1836–37 in Tennessee, the daughter of William Pitman and Sarah "Sally" Melton — a link carried as supported, not proved. No record names her as William's daughter in so many words; the case rests on her place as Sarah Ann, aged 14, in William's 1850 Morgan County household, on the family's census chain, and on her brother Ephraim Pitman's 1875 deed to Ransom. The records spell the surname Pitman, with one T; the Pittman form with two T's is the one carried in the family listing. A rival attribution — that she was the daughter of Thomas Pittman and Susan Lawson of Muhlenberg County — is contradicted: Thomas and Susan's own daughter Sarah A. E. was born in Kentucky about 1829–32 and was still unmarried in their household in 1870, while our Sarah was born in Tennessee and was Ransom Davis's wife with children by then. Two different women.

In 1860 Sarah was living with her brother William Pitman in Butler County, Kentucky, the plausible meeting point for a Muhlenberg marriage. On 16 June 1861 she married Ransom Davis at the house of J. Hunt, by Abner Wood; the minister's return survives. They raised five children — Amanda, Jesse, William, Jerusha, and Balaam — on Mud River in Muhlenberg County and, after 1875, on Bone Camp Creek in Morgan County, Tennessee. Her daughter Amanda married James Alexander McKinney in 1881; her son William married Farsina Brewster in 1888.

Ransom died by 1890. Sarah paid the tax on the 83 acres in 1891 and died between then and the 1896–97 division of the land among the heirs; the exact date, place, and burial are unproven, and no death record has been found. A Find a Grave index entry naming a "Sarah Ann Wright Pittman," died 1906, is an unverified index-only lead and is not carried as fact — it is rejected against the 1891 tax payment and the 1896–97 heirs' division. No photograph of Sarah has been found.
