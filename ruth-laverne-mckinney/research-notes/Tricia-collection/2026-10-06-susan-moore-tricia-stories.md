## Message 1 — Sunday 3:32 PM

My sister was an amazing person. My hero. When we were young it didn't start out that great though. We lived in a neighborhood where most of the girls were around my age and we all hung out together. Tricia was always tagging along and to be honest we were not always nice. We made up a story one time and told her that we were secretly princesses and the queen mother didn't want us around anyone that wasn't a princess, but that if she did what we said we could make her one too. We took her to this big field behind our house and set her on a stool and covered her with a bunch of blankets, told her she could not talk or move or she couldn't become a princess. Then we left her there by herself in the field. We would keep walking around her and close by trying to disguise our voices to see what she would do. She never made a sound and never moved. She just sat there. Now mind you it was summertime and 90 degrees, but she just sat there. We left her there for about half an hour or so and then went back for her. She was so sweaty and hot but she was so proud of herself. She said she never moved even though she was really scared but she really wanted to be a princess. My mom couldn't figure out why Trish was so sweaty and thirsty when we got home but she never told on us. Another time we were down in our basement again with my friends. My parents sold these cleaning products called Bestline. There were dozens of boxes (cases) down in the storage room in the basement and we would climb on the boxes all the time. So we were climbing on the boxes and we would call Tricia to come to one of us to be "safe." She would go to that person and then that person would pretend to be a ghost and scare her. We kept doing this each taking turns scaring her until she was climbing on a box and slipped and fell. She cracked her head and had to go get stitches. She still never told on us. Just that we were climbing on boxes.

## Message 2 — Sunday 4:09 PM

We would go to Beckley West Virginia several times a year. The "homestead" was there. Nana's brother and sister still lived in the house that my great grandfather Daddy Hurt built. It was awesome. It was right next to and up from a railroad track. There was a bridge over the tracks and a huge dirt hill going down to the tracks with hundreds of pigeons living under that bridge. Tricia and I played under that bridge for hours, sliding down the dirt hill over and over again. We had a blast. It was our secret kingdom, our own little world, But by the time we got back to the house we were so covered in dirt and pigeon poop that we weren't allowed in the house until Aunt Mel (Nana's sister) cleaned us up. She would take a broom and sweep off the loose stuff and then take the hose to us for the rest. Then we had to stay outside till we dried. Those were the days. We had so much fun there and we actually got along. Mom and Aunt Mel would take us on day trips too up there. I remember touring a coal mine which was really cool even for me and I'm claustrophobic. Then we toured the White sulphur Springs hotel but they left out the secret bunker part. We got to see the actual water source that the sulphur water came from and taste it. We Weren't supposed to but Tricia and I didn't always do what we were supposed to. We snuck a drink and yes, it was gross and almost made me puke but we did it. When we were young I had a hoppity hop that I absolutely loved and bounced on all the time. Well I made the huge mistake of saying to Trish that the rubber was so tight that you couldn't stick a pin in it. Of course she had to test my theory and prove me wrong. She stuck a pin in it and popped it. I was not happy. Mom tried to fix it but it didn't work. Then she was playing with my cat one time and decided she wanted some juice. So I opened the frig about an hour later and found my cat. The cat was extremely cold and extremely pissed. In the house we grew up in the garage was in the back under the house and the driveway circled around and went down to the garage. I was down at the garage door one day and Tricia decided to drop a brick on me. The very corner of it scraped right next to my eye and I still have the scar to this day. As we got older we got along a lot better but we were very different people. Tricia was outgoing, fun loving, adventurous, and the most courageous person I ever knew. She could talk to anyone with confidence about anything. She was so independent and self assured. She travelled the world and had fun. She told me a story about one of her trips to Japan. They had a layover there because she was an international flight attendant for Delta. She decided to do some exploring. She came across a street musician somewhere and started to dance. She was soon joined by an elderly Japanese lady and they both danced to the music and had a great time. Tricia didn't speak her language and the lady didn't speak English, but they danced and laughed and when the music stopped they hugged and went on their ways. That was my sister. Beautiful, fun and full of life. She was my hero and I miss her every day.
