# Dawn Brewster — Grandma Ruth & Tom memories (email 2026-10-10)

Source: Email from Dawn Brewster <dawn_brewster26@yahoo.com>, subject "Gma & Tom for book", received Sat Oct 10, 2026 1:14 PM EDT. Filed verbatim below, then key points pulled for story use.

## Full text (verbatim)

Grandma and Tom where always a big part of our lives, without them we would not be who we are today.  All of my best memories of childhood were with Grandma and Tom.  Grandma always took us back to school shopping and made sure we had everything we needed.  When growing up, when things were bad at home I moved in with Grandma and Tom multiple times. I loved when we would go to Tom's parents farm and helped them pick (eat) all the strawberries from the field.  Grandma had the best hugs, I always say when I am having a day that I need 
Holidays were always spent at Grandma and Tom's, grandma had special glassware/china in the china cabinet that we used and the house always smelled amazing.  For Christmas morning we would have ham souffle (egg junk as we called it) and sticky rolls.  We always joked that Grandma had to make one pan of sticky rolls for Mike and one for the rest of us.  Grandma did get to teach Melissa and I how to make these so we could continue this tradition.  Tommy made his famous cookies for us all the time and there would always be a tupperware container in the dining room that we would sneak handfuls of them.  He could never tell us the recipe, he just threw the stuff in the bowl, but they were perfect every time (Melissa did figure out the recipe after trying it several times on her own, thankfully).  Grandma always would cook or have food ready for us, you were never leaving grandma's without eating.  She made the best pinto beans and cornbread (Melissa of course can make her pinto beans), I often ask Melissa to make me some pintos when I am really missing grandma, and grilled swiss cheese sandwiches, the best (are both my comfort foods to this day).  When Rick and I were kids, Grandma and Tom would take us to see the trains at Christmas time in Downtown Cincinnati.  Then we would drive around to see the Christmas lights in different neighborhoods.  
Grandma had the best formal dresses that were of a different time and I would love to play dress up in them when I was younger.  She also kept all her old school stuff, from when she was a teacher, and I would love to play school, she had an antique desk in the basement that I would use. Tommy would take us to Red's games and Monster Truck shows.  
When I had my daughter, Tori, she was grandma's best friend.  Grandma had just retired and Tori became her world.  Grandma would buy her the cutest dresses but they were too small.  I had to explain to grandma, we have to do a booty check grandma, if her booty (diaper) is out it's too small and she can't wear it.  Grandma would bake goodies with Tori all the time, several times when I would pick up Tori after I got off work there were many of their creations in the kitchen.  Tori definitely got her love of baking from all her time with grandma.  Grandma and Tom got my son, Brandon his first basketball hoop.  From a very young age Brandon loved basketball and Grandma and Tom were so thrilled to get him his first hoop.  When I worked and or had school grandma and Tom kept Tori and Brandon.  They would pick them up from school.  Tom had a joke with the teachers, the teachers would say your here to pick up the good one and Tom would say well I'd be taking the wrong kid home.  Tom would take them to McDonald's to get a treat on the way home. 
Grandma helped plan my first wedding, I wanted so badly to have grandma make my wedding cake, I had seen all the beautiful and amazing wedding cakes she had done for others, but she wasn't able to.  Grandma was there every step of the way and the wedding was beautiful.  I loved spending that time with her and have that special moment.
Our final trip with grandma and Tom, I call it the trip that changed everything.  We all knew grandma shouldn't make the trip to West Virginia for Aunt Sonia's funeral, but there was never telling grandma no she couldn't do something.  Throughout the trip, we would stop at rest stops, the final one we stopped at we asked grandma if she needed help and she insisted she didn't, she was fine.  She took a couple steps out of the car and fell.  A gentleman nearby ran to her and we did to, to try to help her.  We got her up and back in the car, we said we would turn around and go home and she said no, we were going to West Virginia, she wanted to go see her brother and home.  We reluctantly finished the trip there, and grandma acted like she was fine the whole way.  When we got there the boys had to carry grandma to sit by uncle Eddie, she could not walk since the fall.  She finished out the funeral sitting there and talking to everyone.  I think she knew that this would be her last time to see everyone and go home even before taking the trip and that's why she insisted on going to begin with.  As soon as we got back to Cincinnati we took her straight to the hospital.  She broke her hip and pelvis on the fall.  We understood then how important the trip was to her and what it meant that she had to be there.  After that, she was in the hospital for a while, then a nursing home.  She eventually went home, but she had a hospital bed in the living room.  Tom & Rick had to have an alarm put on the bed because she kept trying to get out of the bed.  She would tell me stories about family members, that had passed, that were coming to see her.  With her dementia, I think she thought that she was still in the nursing home, she would always say how mean the ladies were to her, and come up with some elaborate escape plan for me to help her escape.  I would have to explain to her Grandma, you're at home and you can't get out of the bed because you'll get hurt again if you fall.  The day that she passed me, Tori and Melissa went to see her.  I had asked them to leave the room so I could have a minute with grandma alone.  I told her thank you for everything, that without her and Tom we wouldn't be the people we are today. That it was ok for her to go, she didn't have to be in pain anymore and she could go be with aunt Tricia and Nana and I loved her.  I felt like I gave her permission to go, it was the hardest thing I've ever had to do, and later that night she passed away.
