### 1. Parents Story/garry-susan-story-my-parents-book-chapter-1.md (My Parents chapter)
- Tom McGregor "stepped up to become a father to Susan and her younger sister Tricia." Tom's parents "Mamawl and Papawl" were Kentuckians in Harrison, Ohio, with a country garden where the kids picked sweet berries and pulled peanuts.
- Beckley and the Secret Kingdom: several times a year the family went to Beckley, West Virginia. The "homestead" was there; Nana's brother and sister still lived in the house Susan's great-grandfather Daddy Hurt built, right next to and up from a railroad track. A bridge over the tracks, a huge dirt hill down to them, hundreds of pigeons under the bridge. Susan and Tricia played under the bridge for hours, sliding down the dirt hill over and over — "their secret kingdom — their own little world." They came back so covered in dirt and pigeon poop they weren't allowed inside until Aunt Mel (Nana's sister) cleaned them up: broom for the loose stuff, then the hose, then stay outside till they dried. "Those were the days. They had so much fun there, and they actually got along."
- Day trips with Mom and Aunt Mel: touring a coal mine ("really cool even for me and I'm claustrophobic"); touring the White Sulphur Springs hotel, "but the guides left out the secret bunker part"; seeing the actual water source the sulphur water came from and tasting it — they weren't supposed to, "but Susan and Tricia didn't always do what they were supposed to. They snuck a drink. It was gross and almost made Susan puke, but they did it."
- Two Sisters section, caption: "Group photograph from Tricia's first wedding — Dawn, Ruth, Susan holding baby Michael, Tricia and John Woodhouse, Shawn, Rick, and Tom." (Susan holding baby Michael dates this wedding to ~1988 or later.)
- The hoppity hop: Susan loved hers; she told Tricia the rubber was so tight you couldn't stick a pin in it; Tricia tested it, stuck a pin in, popped it. Mom tried to fix it; it didn't work.
- The cat in the fridge: Tricia was playing with Susan's cat, decided she wanted juice; Susan opened the fridge about an hour later and found her cat in there — "extremely cold and extremely pissed."
- The brick: garage in back under the house, driveway circling down to it. Susan was down at the garage door one day; Tricia dropped a brick; the very corner scraped right next to Susan's eye. She still has the scar.
- The princess trick: neighborhood girls around Susan's age hung out together; Tricia always tagging along; they told her they were secretly princesses and the queen mother didn't want them around anyone who wasn't a princess, but if Tricia did what they said they could make her one too. They took her to a big field behind the house, set her on a stool, covered her with blankets, told her she could not talk or move or she couldn't become a princess, then left her there alone. They walked around her disguising their voices to see what she'd do. She never made a sound, never moved — summertime, 90 degrees, about half an hour. She was sweaty and hot but so proud: she never moved even though she was really scared, because she really wanted to be a princess. Their mom couldn't figure out why Tricia was so sweaty and thirsty. Tricia never told on them.
- The basement boxes: parents sold cleaning products called Bestline; dozens of cases stacked in the basement storage room; kids climbed on them all the time. They'd call Tricia over to one of them to be "safe"; she'd go to that person, who'd pretend to be a ghost and scare her. Taking turns, until she was climbing on a box, slipped and fell, cracked her head, had to get stitches. She still never told on them — just said they were climbing on boxes.
- As they got older they got along a lot better but stayed very different people.
- Grown Tricia: "Tricia became an international flight attendant for Delta. On a layover in Japan, she went exploring, came across a street musician, and started to dance. An elderly Japanese lady joined her. Neither spoke the other's language. They danced and laughed, and when the music stopped, they hugged and went on their ways. That was Tricia. Beautiful, fun, full of life. Susan's hero. She misses her every day."
- Also in this chapter: Susan was 7 when Richard died (Oct 3, 1968, age 36, in his sleep in a truck sleeper berth); photo caption "Garry, Susan, Rick, and Dawn outdoors at Ruth's house at the picnic table — likely shortly before the split" (no Tricia in that photo).
