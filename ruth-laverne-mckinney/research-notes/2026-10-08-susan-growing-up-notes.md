## Beckley, West Virginia — The Homestead

Susan and Tricia went to Beckley, West Virginia several times a year. The "homestead" was there. Nana's brother and sister still lived in the house that Susan's great-grandfather Daddy Hurt built. It was right next to and up from a railroad track.

There was a bridge over the tracks and a huge dirt hill going down to the tracks, with hundreds of pigeons living under that bridge. Tricia and Susan played under that bridge for hours, sliding down the dirt hill over and over. They called it their "secret kingdom" — their own little world.

By the time they got back to the house they were so covered in dirt and pigeon poop they weren't allowed in the house until Aunt Mel (Nana's sister) cleaned them up. She would take a broom and sweep off the loose stuff, then take the hose to them for the rest. Then they had to stay outside till they dried.

Mom and Aunt Mel took them on day trips up there. Susan remembers:
- Touring a coal mine — "really cool even for me and I'm claustrophobic"
- Touring the White Sulphur Springs hotel — "they left out the secret bunker part"
- Seeing the actual water source the sulphur water came from and tasting it. They weren't supposed to, but Tricia and Susan didn't always do what they were supposed to. They snuck a drink. It was gross and almost made Susan puke, but they did it.

## Susan and Tricia as Kids

**The hoppity hop:** When young, Susan had a hoppity hop she absolutely loved and bounced on all the time. She made the mistake of saying to Tricia that the rubber was so tight you couldn't stick a pin in it. Tricia had to test the theory and prove her wrong — stuck a pin in it and popped it. Susan was not happy. Mom tried to fix it but it didn't work.

**The cat in the fridge:** Tricia was playing with Susan's cat one time and decided she wanted some juice. Susan opened the fridge about an hour later and found her cat. The cat was extremely cold and extremely pissed.

**The brick:** In the house they grew up in, the garage was in the back under the house and the driveway circled around and went down to the garage. Susan was down at the garage door one day and Tricia decided to drop a brick on her. The very corner of it scraped right next to Susan's eye. She still has the scar to this day.

**The princess trick:** When young, Susan lived in a neighborhood where most of the girls were around her age and they all hung out together. Tricia was always tagging along and they were not always nice. They made up a story and told Tricia they were secretly princesses and the queen mother didn't want them around anyone that wasn't a princess, but if she did what they said they could make her one too. They took her to a big field behind the house, set her on a stool, covered her with blankets, told her she could not talk or move or she couldn't become a princess. Then they left her there by herself in the field. They kept walking around her and close by, disguising their voices to see what she would do. She never made a sound and never moved — just sat there. It was summertime and 90 degrees. They left her there about half an hour, then went back. She was so sweaty and hot but so proud of herself. She said she never moved even though she was really scared, but she really wanted to be a princess. Their mom couldn't figure out why Trish was so sweaty and thirsty when they got home, but Tricia never told on them.

**The basement boxes:** Down in the basement with Susan's friends. Their parents sold cleaning products called Bestline. There were dozens of boxes (cases) in the storage room and they climbed on them all the time. They'd call Tricia to come to one of them to be "safe." She'd go to that person, then that person would pretend to be a ghost and scare her. They kept doing this, taking turns, until she was climbing on a box and slipped and fell. She cracked her head and had to get stitches. She still never told on them — just said they were climbing on boxes.

**As they got older** they got along a lot better, but they were very different people.

## Tricia Grown

Tricia was outgoing, fun-loving, adventurous, and the most courageous person Susan ever knew. She could talk to anyone with confidence about anything. So independent and self-assured. She travelled the world and had fun.

She was an international flight attendant for Delta. Susan tells a story about one of Tricia's trips to Japan: they had a layover, Tricia decided to explore, came across a street musician and started to dance. She was soon joined by an elderly Japanese lady and they both danced to the music and had a great time. Tricia didn't speak her language and the lady didn't speak English, but they danced and laughed, and when the music stopped they hugged and went on their ways.

"That was my sister. Beautiful, fun and full of life. She was my hero and I miss her every day." — Susan Moore
