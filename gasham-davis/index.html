Life Story

Gasham Davis was born 21 March 1811 in Muhlenberg County, Kentucky, twin of his sister Lucy — the first children born in Kentucky after the family moved from Rowan County, North Carolina. He was named Gasham for Gershom Hunt, his grandmother Charity Hunt's grandfather; the name runs through Charity's family from the Long Island Moores. His parents, Jesse Davis and Charity Hunt, had married in Rowan County in 1799. Jesse died before 29 November 1830, when his estate was settled.

On 17 June 1831 Gasham bought 150 acres on the Muddy River from his widowed sister Mary Davis Fleming. He farmed there and kept a ferry across the river on the road to Bowling Green. He gave the ferry up himself in February 1843 — John J. Engler was licensed at the same crossing — and took it back eight months later, in October 1843, as owner of land on both sides of the river.

He married Permelia A. Whitaker about 1837; no marriage record has been found in Kentucky or Indiana, and the elopement story — a ladder, a horseback ride to Rochester, a river packet to Indiana — is family tradition only, with no record behind it. Five children are documented or supported, and a sixth, Charity A., is listed with her parentage unproved. He died about April 1847, aged 36, leaving no will; his widow declined to administer the estate, and Enos Hunt was appointed. No cause of death is recorded, and no burial has been found.

Research Notes and Open Questions

  • How to read the grades: DOCUMENTED means a record was read directly. SUPPORTED means the evidence points that way but no single record proves it. FAMILY ACCOUNT means it comes from the family's own telling. UNPROVED means it is a lead only, with no record behind it yet.
  • Parents: Jesse × Charity's marriage is documented (16 March 1799, Rowan County, North Carolina); the link to Gasham rests on the family Bible transcription and the compiled histories, so it is carried as supported, not documented. Jesse's own parents are unproved — Henry Davis × Fanny is a lead only, and Henry's 1805 will names Randall, Henry, and a daughter, never Jesse.
  • Name: Gasham, Gershom, and Gashom are one name in different hands; "Gish" on Squire's death certificate is the same name. He was named for Gershom Hunt (born 1723), his grandmother Charity's grandfather. The claim that Gershom Moore was Charity's great-grandfather, living on Long Island by 1652, is wrong in detail — he was her great-great-great-grandfather, born about 1645.
  • Twins: Gasham × Lucy, born 21 March 1811 — one twin pair. Joseph × Sarah are not twins: Sarah was born 12 November 1820.
  • Marriage: about 1837 is the working date — Anderson gives "1837 (?)", a 1954 paper says "about 1837", and every documented child was born 1838–1847. No marriage record was found: Muhlenberg Marriage Book 1 was searched in full and Register Book 2, pp. 1–20, was read, with no entry; the Indiana books were not read page by page. The 1831 date rested on Nancy J.'s supposed 1832 birth — she was Nancy J. Mairs. The elopement is family tradition only.
  • Ferry: given up February 1843 on his own motion (Order Book 5, p. 49), re-licensed October 1843 (p. 80), ended April 1847 at his death (pp. 248–251, via Anderson). The crossing's exact spot is not in any record — "near Forgy Mill Bridge" is the compiled histories' placement, not a deed or order.
  • Children: five are documented or supported — John P., Ransom, Enos, Squire Harper, Lydia Margaret. Charity A. is listed with her parentage unproved: her marriage is documented, but no record names her parents, and she was born about 1835, before the 1837 wedding. Nancy J. is not a daughter: the 1848 marriage register names the bride Nancy J. Mairs. The "Susan Davis, 5" in the 1850 census is read as Squire, mis-recorded, not a separate child.
  • Death: about April 1847, no cause recorded, no burial found. The $42.75 estate-sale figure in the compiled histories is uncited and is not carried as a fact.
  • 1840 census: Gough cites a Gasham household at p. 354A, line 26, with four children; the 1840 index search found no Gasham in Muhlenberg County. Unresolved — the 1840 household is not cited as established here.
  • Open pulls: the 1840 census image at p. 354A; the Order Book 5, pp. 248–251, image; the deed book and page for the 1831 purchase and the 1851 circuit-court sale of the 150 acres; the Davis Bible photostat.
