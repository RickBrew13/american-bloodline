Life Story

Weston Polsley was born 17 April 1833 at Wellsburg, Brooke County, Virginia — a date and place carried at UNPROVED, resting on Bryan 1939 alone, since no primary birth record can exist: Virginia registration began in 1853. He was the third child of Daniel Haymond Polsley × Eliza Vilette Brown, a parentage carried at SUPPORTED on three converging lines: Bryan 1939 lists Weston among Daniel’s children; Nina Gist’s letter — Adaline’s daughter — says “Uncle Weston and Uncle John built the summer house”; and the 1850 Mason household has a 17-year-old son in Weston’s exact birth-order slot. The one shadow over that parentage is the 1850 image itself, which reads the 17-year-old as “Martin”, not “Weston” — whether Martin is Weston under a double name, Weston was absent in 1850, or the 17 April 1833 birthdate belongs to Martin is still open.

Weston married Harriett Sophia Rine on two licenses, both DOCUMENTED: 12 December 1859, naming “H. S. Ryan, daughter of George Ryan”, and a second, undated license naming “H. S. Rine, daughter of George Rine”. The ceremony date of 17 March 1860 rests on an old compiled account only, and the “12 Feb 1860” date carried on FamilySearch and WikiTree is index-derived — almost certainly the preceding Harford entry’s date. Seven children are carried: Frank E., born 1861; Walter W., born 1862, who carries the direct line; Emma Gertrude, born 1864; John D., born about 1866, who died of diphtheria in 1868 aged about eighteen months; George James, born 1868, whose own state death certificate names Weston Polsley × Sophia Rine as his parents; Jenny, born 1870, whose very name is UNPROVED — five names, one child; and Myrtle, born and died 19 July 1872 / 19 July 1900, a teacher who died on her 28th birthday.

The 1870 census finds the family in Cooper District, Mason County — “Wesley” Polsley 37, Harriet S. 30, Frank E. 9, Walter W. 7, Emma G. 5, George 3, and Mary 9/12 — the household image read directly. Weston was absent from his father Daniel’s household in 1860, consistent with his March 1860 marriage. Daniel himself was a public man — Western Transcript editor, lieutenant governor of the Restored Government of Virginia, judge, and U.S. Representative — but no portrait of Weston exists anywhere, and his shared stone at Evergreen Cemetery is barely legible under lichen, so the hero here is a placeholder.

Weston died 28 January 1872, after a short illness, according to the Weekly Register obituary of 1 February 1872, which calls him the “son of Judge Polsley”; Find a Grave gives 29 January, and both dates are carried with the obituary leading. He is buried at Evergreen Cemetery, Letart, beside Harriett, under a shared stone reading “WESTON POLSLEY / 1833 — 1872”. Harriett lived on until 13 January 1919, remarrying after Weston’s death — her second husband Nelson Ogden, and her daughter Rose Ogden Mason, are why the 1900 census counts her 8 children born and 7 living against Weston’s seven.
