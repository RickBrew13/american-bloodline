Life Story

Isaac Staats was born about 1815 or 1816 in Jackson County, Virginia, and married Eliza Tolla on 8 August 1833 in Jackson County, when he was about seventeen and she about fifteen. The marriage register names the minister, George Casto, and no parents for either spouse. They settled at Staats Mills, where the 1850 census finds Isaac farming with his wife Anne E. and eight children in the household. Thirteen children followed in all, and his son William F. carries the line.

The mill is the place where the family story needed correcting. An old compiled account says Isaac “erected the first mill” at Staats Mills, and a later telling dates his mill to the 1860s. The deed settles it: Jacob Pfost bought 260 acres from Nicholas Lee in 1851, built the first grist mill at the mouth of Millseat Run, and on 8 May 1858 sold the one-acre tract around the mill to Isaac for $350. Isaac owned and ran the mill from 1858; he did not build the first one. He sold it to his son Enoch in 1877. The covered bridge at the mill — the Staats Mill Bridge, also called the Tug Fork Covered Bridge — is dated 1867 by the highway marker, 1887 by the Historic American Engineering Record and the National Register, and 1888 by West Virginia Tourism; the 1887 date leads.

Isaac’s Civil War service is probable, not settled. An Isaac Staats appears on the July 1863 roster of Captain Nicholas Casto’s company, 141st Militia, West Virginia State Troops, and a later compilation gives his enlistment as 23 July 1863 and discharge as 20 May 1865 — but a second Isaac Staats appears among 1864 recruits, the 1921 genealogy is silent on any service, and an old compiled account says he “remained on his land, managing the mill” through the war. This was state militia service, not federal, and no Confederate service is attached to him. His son William F.’s “Home Scouts” line in the same county history is in the same position — one line, no roster — and is not claimed either.

Ann Eliza died on 9 January 1876, and Isaac married again the next year, Mariah Brickels, on 25 February 1877; there were no children by that marriage. He died on 8 April 1890, aged 74 by the stone, and is buried with Ann Eliza under their shared stone in Old Siniaville Cemetery, Row 6. The stone photograph’s inscription is weathered past reading — the dates here come from the cemetery transcription, not from the photograph.

His parents are carried as Cornelius Staats × Anna Carney, the position in the research folder, at SUPPORTED — an old compiled account and the 1921 genealogy agree, but no primary record links Isaac to Cornelius. Cornelius is said to have died about 1816, a soldier in the War of 1812, leaving Anna with Isaac and Polly; the War of 1812 death rests on one county history only, with no service record, pension, or bounty-land entry found, and is carried as UNPROVED. Anna married twice more after Cornelius — first William R. Randolph, then Enoch C. Thomas about 1820 — and an old telling that one of her husbands “died in the Union Army” is refuted: it was her son, William R. Randolph, who died 8 May 1863 at Winchester, Virginia. No husband of hers died in the Union Army.

No portrait of Isaac is placed on this page. A portrait of him is carried on Geni and on FamilySearch, but it was not retrievable in this pass and its provenance is unchecked, so the shared headstone is the hero. A tintype of his son Riley exists on Geni and Find a Grave; no child of this couple has a profile page, so no child’s portrait is placed here.
