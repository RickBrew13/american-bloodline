Life Story

Elizabeth Nightengal was born in June 1855 in Virginia, the daughter of Aaron Nightingale and Abigail Wheeler Rice — the carried ruling, from the FamilySearch timeline in the couple profile; no primary record for the parentage has been found. On 14 September 1878 she married Morrison Clark in Dearborn County, Indiana, recorded as Lizzie Young. By 1880 she was keeping house at Aurora with Morrison and two small daughters, Annie Young and Ida Mae. By 1900 the family was at 424 Crescent Avenue in Covington, Kentucky, Morrison a puddler in the rolling mill, and the census counted what the family had lost: eight children born, four living. In 1910 she and Morrison were still together in Newport, with their son Elmer, 24, at home. Morrison died in 1917. Elizabeth’s death is unknown — no death or burial record has been found for her. No portrait of Elizabeth has been found.

There were two Elizabeth Nightengals, and their records must never be mixed. The other Elizabeth was born 19 June 1855 at Tipton, Staffordshire, England, the daughter of Aaron Nightingale and Elizabeth Heathcock; she married Joseph H. Young in 1873, was widowed in 1876, and died 16 February 1930 at Clifton, Campbell County, Kentucky. Her Kentucky death certificate, her Tipton baptism, the 1861 England census, and the Find a Grave memorials that carry her name all belong to her — not to Morrison’s wife. The two Aaron Nightingales — the American born in 1788 and the English puddler born about 1838 — must never be merged, and neither must the two Elizabeths.

Her birthplace carries the open conflict: the carried ruling says Virginia, while the 1880 and 1900 censuses both say England, and her daughter Ida’s death certificate says Yorkshire, England. The parentage itself is unproved — no record in the research folder cites Abigail Wheeler Rice or a Virginia birth, and the arithmetic is a difficulty: parents of 67 and 64 at an 1855 birth, twenty-four years after their ninth child, Persis, in 1831. The ruling stands as the position; the conflict is carried here, not settled. No church affiliation has been found for her.
