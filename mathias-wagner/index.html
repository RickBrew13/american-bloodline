Life Story

Mathias Wagner was born on 21 September 1832 in Jimbolia, in the Banat — his baptism was read directly in the Jimbolia baptisms register, page 287, which names his parents as Nikolaus Wagner and Barbara Thomesz of Hatzfeld. No photograph of Mathias has been found.

On 7 September 1856, at Hatzfeld, Mathias married Margarethe Schuld, 22, of Kleinjetscha — the marriage register entry, page 91, entry 44, calls him a bachelor of Hatzfeld, aged 23, and her a maiden, aged 22, with witnesses Nicolaus Raden and Josephus Schuld. The register names no parents for either; the parentage on this page rests on their two baptism entries. They had four children, carried in Hornung’s Hatzfeld Familienbuch, family 24418: Katharina, born 29 July 1857; Mathias, born 9 April 1859; Barbara, born 26 June 1860; and Nikolaus, born 27 September 1862. Only Barbara is register-verified — her baptism names Mathias and Margaretha as her parents. Barbara married Philippus Palmer; she is the direct line.

Mathias is carried as dying on 5 April 1871 at Gilad, from Hornung’s Familienbuch citing Neff’s Tschakowa book — but no register entry has been pulled for it, and December 1911 ship-list entries name a Mathias Wagner still living in Hatzfeld as the father-in-law of Johann Schuld. If that man is ours, the 1871 death is wrong; a namesake is possible. His burial is unknown, and no occupation or military service is recorded for him. Margarethe lived on until 13 March 1906, by the date carried in compiled sources.

Research Notes and Open Questions

  • Grades on this page: DOCUMENTED means an original record was read (the baptism and marriage register entries; the Kleinjetscha registers on FamilySearch film 858420); SUPPORTED means a claim attested by filed research — Hornung’s Familienbuch, the signed-in tree checks — but not by a register read here; UNPROVED means a claim carried from the research folder with no corroboration found.
  • Parentage: Mathias’s link to Nikolaus Wagner × Barbara Thomesz is documented — his own baptism entry names them. Margarethe’s parents, Wilhelmus Schuldt × Catharina Rusterin, are documented from her baptism entry, read from the image on FamilySearch film 858420 (item 5, image 253, register p. 210). The register spells her surname Schuldt; the family form is Schuld. Luxracines gives her parents as Wilhelm Schulte × Katharina Roster — the same people under spelling variants, not a different family.
  • Widow or maiden? — the couple’s central open question, carried honestly on both sides. (a) The Hatzfeld marriage register (1856) calls Margarethe a maiden, 22, and the notes on FamilySearch, Findmypast, WikiTree and Ancestry all say the Josef Mayer claim is unresolved against the register; no Mayer spouse or child appears on any profile. (b) The folder’s 10-29 read of luxracines family F5585 claims a marriage of Josef Mayer × Margaretha Schuld on 23 January 1853 at Iecea Mică, to a woman with Margarethe’s exact birth date (9 April 1834) and parents (Wilhelm Schulte × Katharina Roster — spelling variants of Wilhelmus Schuldt × Catharina Rusterin); Josef Mayer died 11 March 1855, and a child of the marriage was born 5 May 1854. That luxracines entry could not be re-read here — it needs a member login, and none is held for that site. (c) The May 1854 Mayer-child baptism is not in FamilySearch film 858420 (its baptisms end in January 1836). (d) Giel’s S253 entry sits at the Bavarian State Library as a loanable print copy only and cannot be read online; nothing has been ordered or paid for. The 10-21 case against the 1853 marriage is weaker than it looked: the Kleinjetscha marriage volume cataloged 1852–1893 in fact begins 24 August 1856, so an 1853 marriage would not appear in it at all. If the 1853 marriage is real, Margarethe was a widow at her 1856 wedding and the couple had FIVE children — the Mayer son Josef, born 5 May 1854, belongs in the family. If it is a conflation, two Margarethas would share an identical birth date and identical parents. No Mayer marriage is listed as a fact on this page; it is carried here only as an unresolved claim.
