Family Relationship Notes

The 1922 Alabama marriage record names Mary Schillaci as Joseph’s wife. The 1930 and 1940 census images list children in the household, and later marriage certificates, Social Security files, and Josephine’s 2007 obituary provide additional parent links. The profile retains all named children from those records rather than relying only on the smaller compiled-tree list.

Joseph’s own parents have not been identified. A San Cataldo christening for a Joseph Carruba, baptised 14 October 1892, born 13 October 1892, naming parents Salvatore Carruba and Liboria Mistretta, was read from the image on 28 November 2026 and is not him — his birth is 31 August 1891 in the Social Security Death Index and the Kentucky death index. His brother Norman Carruba is proved by the 1920 census image: Joseph, 27, single, is listed as “Brother” in Norman’s household at Millport, Alabama, both coal miners, and the reading was confirmed by a second look at the image. A second tree spouse, Mary Tudisco, has no marriage event or children and is not included as an established spouse; her tree sources were read on 28 November 2026 and are all Alabama records for Maria and Marie Tudisco, Tedesco and Tadisco that never name Joseph — she belongs to Louis Carruba in the tree.

The family surname appears as Carruba and Carubba; Mary’s surname appears as Schillaci, Schillace, Schillari, and Shillaci. The profile uses Carruba and Schillaci, matching the 1922 marriage record and key census entries.

Life Story

Joseph Carruba was born 31 August 1891 in Italy. He married Mary Schillaci on 17 June 1922 at Thomas, in Jefferson County, Alabama. Their first children were born in Alabama — Sam about 1924, Rosa about 1926, Carmella about 1929 — and the family moved to Harlan County, Kentucky about 1930. Josephine was born at Totz in 1931, Susie about 1933, and Cora on 22 September 1934. By the 1940 census the household at Cumberland held Joseph and Mary with eight of their children.

Joseph died 10 December 1970 in Harlan County and is buried at Cumberland. He worked as a coal miner in 1920, 1930 and 1940. In 1920 he was living at Millport, Alabama, in the household of his brother Norman Carruba, also a coal miner. His own parents are not named in any record found; the San Cataldo christening for a Joseph Carruba, born 13 October 1892 to Salvatore Carruba and Liboria Mistretta, was read from the image and is not him. His daughter Cora's 1953 marriage certificate names him as her father, and the 1940 census lists her as his daughter — the two records that identify him as her father.

Research Notes and Open Questions

  • The family name is spelled Carruba and Carubba across the records; the profile uses Carruba, the spelling on Cora's marriage certificate and the censuses. His wife's name is spelled Schillaci, Schillace, Schillari and Shillaci; the profile uses Schillaci, the spelling on their 1922 marriage record.
  • Joseph’s own parents are not named in any record found. The San Cataldo christening for a Joseph Carruba, baptised 14 October 1892, born 13 October 1892, naming parents Salvatore Carruba and Liboria Mistretta, was read from the image on 28 November 2026 and is not him — his birth is 31 August 1891. The record that would settle his parents is his 1970 Kentucky death certificate, which names parents and birthplace; it is not in FamilySearch’s free image collections.
  • The family tree lists only seven of his children (Sam, Carmella, Josephine, Susie, Tina Maria, John and Thomas) and omits Cora, Rosa, Katherine, Margaret and Helen; a relationship was deleted on the tree in April 2026. The list here follows the censuses, the marriage certificates and the 2007 obituary.
  • The tree also lists a second spouse, Mary Tudisco, with no marriage events and no children; that attachment is unproved and is not carried here. Her tree sources were read on 28 November 2026: all Alabama records for Maria and Marie Tudisco, Tedesco and Tadisco, none naming Joseph. She belongs to Louis Carruba in the tree.
  • The tree copies the marriage place as "Thomas, Prairie, Arkansas" from an index; the original marriage record says Thomas, Jefferson County, Alabama.
  • His brother Norman Carruba is proved by the 1920 census image: Joseph, 27, single, is listed as “Brother” in Norman’s household at Millport, Alabama, and the two lived next door to each other in Harlan County in 1930 (household 432). Norman was born about 1894–1895 in Italy, married Cora Romano, and worked as a coal miner. The 1920 record is attached on the tree to a separate Joe Carruba person, not to Joseph’s profile. No profile has been built for Norman yet.
  • No 1950 census household has been found for Joseph, Mary or Cora in Harlan County or in Birmingham, Alabama, under Carruba or Carubba; the family may have been enumerated under another spelling, or under a child’s name.
  • He worked as a coal miner in 1920, 1930 and 1940 — Miner, Coal mines in 1920; Loader, Coal in 1930; Miner, Coal mine, 52 weeks, in 1940.
  • His arrival year disagrees between the censuses: 1914 in 1920 and 1910 in 1930, and Norman’s years disagree the same way. The closest passenger-list candidate, Giuseppe Carrubba of Collesano, arrived 17 August 1910 and was read from the image — a different man. No passenger list for Joseph or Mary was found.
  • No World War I draft card for him was found in the indexed records, and no naturalization record. The 1940 Birmingham naturalization petition under the name Joe Carruba is Giuseppe Carruba, born 1909 in Sutera. Joseph was still listed as an alien in the 1940 census.
  • A Joseph Carruba was born 25 November 1935 in Kentucky, mother Mary Schillaci, in the Kentucky birth index. He does not appear in the 1940 household, where he would have been 4. Whether he died young or the entry belongs to another Joseph is open.
  • Josephine’s birthplace conflicts: the Kentucky birth index and the 1940 census give Kentucky, while her 2007 obituary gives Birmingham, Alabama.
  • The 1930 census lists an infant daughter Mary, 7 months, and no Carmella; the 1940 census lists Carmella, 11, and no Mary. They may be the same child recorded under different names, or the infant Mary may have died young. Carmie Lou, who died in infancy in Birmingham in 1928, is a separate child.
