Life Story

Albert Crochen was the father of Artherine E. Crochen. The 1920 census lists him in Hayneville, Lowndes County, Alabama, age 35, with his wife Callie, 30, and four children: Fannie, 12; Albert Jr., 7; “Artha Rene,” 9; and Anna May, 4. The enumerator wrote the family name “Crackeson” and the daughter’s name “Arta Rene” — the rendering of Artherine. Artherine’s 1973 Alabama death record names her father as “Albert Crocker” and her mother as “Callie Martin,” with her spouse Leon Niles. His FamilySearch profile gives his name as “Albert L. Crocker” and his wife as “Collie” Crochen. The family surname is spelled Crochen, Crochan, Crocker, and Crackeson across records — same family, spellings carried as recorded. By 1930 the family had moved to Birmingham, Jefferson County, where Albert worked in a steel plant; the 1930, 1940 and 1950 censuses all place them there. The 1900 and 1910 censuses place Albert as a son in George and Hannah Crocheron’s Hayneville household, and his 1962 death index names George as his father.

Research Notes and Open Questions

  • 21 November 2026 — parents now supported. The 1870, 1880, 1900 and 1910 censuses all place Albert in George and Hannah Crocheron/Crochen’s Hayneville household, and Albert’s 1962 death index names his father as George Crochen. George and Hannah are now listed as his parents. One reading of the 1910 image instead labels Albert son-in-law (which would make George Callie’s father) — the index labels him “Son,” the 1900 census labels him “son,” and the death index names George as his father, so the weight is on George as Albert’s father; the conflicting reading is carried here, not hidden. The Ancestry extract naming Henry Crochen as father of an Albert born Hayneville 1880, died 1962, was checked on FamilySearch and not found; Henry remains UNPROVED and is not the same man as George. 1900 census · 1962 death index
  • Birmingham cemetery continuity. Shadow Lawn Memorial Gardens records Albert Crochen interred 11 March 1962 and names Callie Crochen as heir. This independently supports the Birmingham couple connection, but does not identify Albert’s parents. Shadow Lawn record
  • Siblings now supported: Lee, Louis (Lewis), Emanuel (Maniel/Manuel), Fannie, Winnie (Wippie), Emily (Emma) and Julia Crochen, all listed as George and Hannah’s children in the 1870, 1880 and 1900 censuses; Winnie appears as “Winnie Cook, sister” in Albert’s 1940 household. Hannah’s 1932 death record names her father as Jesse Konnon. “Daisy Cheatham” appears in no census and is not listed as a sibling. Hannah’s 1932 death
  • Next evidence: the 1962 death certificate image and Hannah’s 1932 death certificate image are both restricted to a FamilySearch center or affiliate library — the exact death dates, birthplace and informant still need an in-person pull. The 1910 relationship-label conflict (son vs son-in-law) also needs a fresh read of the original image. Full search logs are in the linked Albert folder.
  • The family surname varies by record — Crochen, Crochan, Crocker, Crackeson. Spellings are carried as recorded, not smoothed over; the death record’s “Crocker” is from the index, and the certificate image is restricted, so it should be checked at a FamilySearch center or affiliate library.
  • The tree profile’s dates “1889–1889” for Albert are not used. They are internally impossible — FamilySearch itself flags “Birth after Father’s Death” three times — and the 1900 census gives his birth as September 1888, the 1962 death index gives 1889, and he was 35 in the 1920 census; he died in 1962.
  • One son, not two. The tree’s “Albert Lewis (1909–1973)” and “Albert (1911–)” are one son, Lewis Albert Crochan, born 25 December 1911 per the birth register (his draft card and Social Security record give 11 November 1911 — not settled). He married Thelma Pugh 10 July 1933 and Willie Mosley 23 December 1953; what ended the first marriage is not known. The 1909 date has no record behind it. Birth register · 1933 marriage
  • Two daughters, given names not recorded, were born to Albert and Callie in Hayneville — 7 July 1912 and 1 September 1914 (birth registers). Neither appears in the 1920 census; their fates are not proved.
  • No Alabama birth record for Artherine has been found; her birth year is not settled — reported 9 October 1909, but the 1930 and 1940 censuses put her at 17 and 27 (born about 1913).
  • The 1940 “Crockan” household is Emanuel’s family. The Hayneville household indexed as Crockan (Eddie Mae, Alfred Lee, Florence, Joe Bell) is the family of Emanuel (Maniel/Manuel) Crochon, Albert’s brother — Emanuel’s death record names his parents as George Crochon and Hannah Canion, the same parents as Albert’s. Eddie Mae (born Bowie) was Albert’s sister-in-law; the children were his nephews and niece. Joe Bell was not found in any search.
  • 1906 marriage not found. No marriage record for Albert and Callie Martin was found in Lowndes County or Alabama, 1900–1910. The only 1906 Lowndes marriage for an Albert is Albert Crocheron to Mattie Martin, 28 November 1906 — a different woman; do not treat Mattie as Callie. The “Callie Martin in Alabama County Marriages” source on the tree is the son’s 1953 marriage, not a 1906 marriage. The marriage remains unconfirmed. Mattie Martin license
