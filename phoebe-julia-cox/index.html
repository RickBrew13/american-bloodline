Life Story

Phoebe Cox was born about 1800 in Virginia and was alive on 23 January 1888, when the Wayne County, Kentucky, court declared her a pauper. Her exact death date, place and burial are unknown. She married James Gray Brewster; no marriage record has been found in Virginia or Tennessee. The name “Julia” appears once, on the 1870 census, where she is written “Julia”, aged 70 — whether it was her middle name is unproved, and every other record calls her Phoebe, Phebe or Feba.

Her parents are carried as Henry Cox × Rebecca Flanary, at UNPROVED. The pairing is contested: the same names belong, in the research folder and on WikiTree, to a different Phoebe — Phoebe Julia Bailey, wife of James Franklin Bailey, who died 2 January 1881 in Carter County, Kentucky — while our Phoebe was alive in January 1888. The DAR record that corroborates the Henry × Rebecca marriage names no Phoebe. The 8 September 1804 birth in Scott County and the 2 January 1881 death that appear on some tree profiles are the Bailey Phoebe’s; Scott County did not exist until 1814.

She lived her whole adult life on Clear Fork, in Fentress and Morgan Counties, Tennessee, and then in Kentucky, where the family moved in their sixties, in the years around the Civil War, through country torn by guerrilla war. Seventeen children are listed on this page, and the honest range is 14–18, not the 23 of an old compiled account: Jeremiah F. and Frederick Miller are very likely one boy, and three of the youngest may be her daughter Rebecca’s children, not her own. Two of her sons died violently — Jesse, struck by lightning in August 1856, and Frederick Miller, murdered on election day in August 1883. Her son John W., born 3 August 1823, became a farmer and a Free Will Baptist minister and carries the line. She was proved alive on 23 January 1888, about three months after his death, when the Wayne County court declared her a pauper; the family story that she stood at his grave in cold rain and caught her death within weeks is refuted as stated. No portrait of Phoebe is known — not in the research folder, and not on any tree site checked.

Research Notes and Open Questions

  • Grades on this page: DOCUMENTED means an original record was read; SUPPORTED means a claim attested by filed research — the census images, the court-order image, the signed-in tree checks — but not by an original record read here; UNPROVED means a claim carried from the research folder with no corroboration found.
  • Parents: Phoebe’s parents are carried as Henry Cox × Rebecca Flanary at UNPROVED. The pairing is contested. The research folder and WikiTree carry the Henry × Rebecca pairing for a different Phoebe — Phoebe Julia Bailey, wife of James Franklin Bailey, died 2 January 1881 in Carter County, Kentucky (WikiTree Cox-3904; her parents Henry Cox Cox-976 × Rebecca Flanary Flanary-12; her children Andrew Jackson Bailey and Rebecca Darnell) — while our Phoebe was alive 23 January 1888. The DAR record A040489, which corroborates the Henry × Rebecca marriage, is the record of John Elisha Flanary, not Henry Cox, and names no Phoebe; its descendant line runs through a son, Henry J. Cox, born 1818. The 1850, 1870 and 1880 censuses show the Bailey Phoebe as Phebe/Pheba Baley/Bailey in Carter County, Kentucky. Our Phoebe’s WikiTree profile (Cox-20258) and Geni profile both carry the Bailey Phoebe’s 1804/1881 data in their headers, contradicted by their own bodies.
  • “Julia”: the word is on the surviving 1870 schedule image — DOCUMENTED. “Julia was her middle name” is UNPROVED. Donna Gough’s view (email, 28 October 2026, personal opinion, not a record) is that “Julia” may be a mistranscription in the hand-copying chain — enumerator to state copy to federal copy — for example “Febe” misread as “Julia”. Every other document has Phoebe or a variant, no Julia at all. The 1981 Lola letter cited for “Phoebe Julia” was not verified.
  • Birth: about 1800, Virginia, against 8 September 1804, Scott County — contradicted. The 1804 date is the Bailey Phoebe’s data, and Scott County did not exist until 1814 (the ground was Washington County).
  • Death: 2 January 1881, Carter County — contradicted for our Phoebe; that death belongs to the Bailey Phoebe. Our Phoebe was proved alive 23 January 1888.
  • Henry Cox’s death: 31 December 1831, shot by Mr. Yates while turkey hunting (old compiled account, WikiTree) against 31 December 1837 (DAR). Both conflicted; the turkey-hunting story is lore — no primary record, and no Yates appears in the Stony Creek minutes.
  • Rebecca Flanary’s death: about 1850 (DAR) against 1860 (Find a Grave memorial 206306822, modern stone, unread) against alive January 1852 (proved by the birth register) and alive in the 1850 census, age 62, Carter County, Kentucky, with $1,500 real estate. Unresolved.
  • Henry × Rebecca marriage: 6 December 1802 (WikiTree) against 6 September 1802 (a compiled Cox site); no surviving register — the first Russell County marriage register was lost in the 1872 fire.
  • David Clinton Cocke: a conflation. The father-figure is David Cock Sr. (born about 1756), married Jemima Leach, whose 1826 will names “my son Henry”. “David Clinton Cocke”, born 12 June 1785, baptised 22 August 1801, is Henry’s younger brother, not his father. “Clinton” is a compiled attribution not to be used as an identifier; the will is signed “David Cock”.
  • Marriage: not found. “Virginia Marriages, 1785–1940” (1,264,889 records) has no James Brewster × Phoebe Cox marriage and no James Brewster groom at all. The Scott County, Virginia, marriage transcription 1815–1850 was read directly: Brewster 0, Bruster 0, Brister 0. Fentress County marriages survive only from 1905 (1905 fire); Morgan County only from 1862 (1863 fire).
