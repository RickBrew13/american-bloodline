Family Relationship Notes

FamilySearch’s United States Social Security NUMIDENT entry for Leon Niles (record 6K7Q-ZC4F) explicitly lists Mary Riggby as a parent and Major Niles as a parent. The associated person-detail pages identify Leon as Mary’s son and Major as her spouse. Those links all come from the same NUMIDENT correspondence entry; they are not independent records.

Mary Rigby was born in Alabama in April 1881, the daughter of Charles Rigby and Silla Kimball Rigby. In 1900 she was 19, living in her father’s household in Perdido, Baldwin County, with her siblings Ida, Charley, Lillian, and Francis. She married Major Nye on 25 November 1901 in Baldwin County. In 1910 she was living in Stockton with Major and their children Clinton, Jessie, Robin, and Learmon.

Her mother’s name was found in the 1880 census and the 1871 marriage record: Charles married Silla Kimball on 17 July 1871, and the June 1880 census shows Charles and his wife Sillah with daughters Ella and Ida. Mary was born ten months after that census, while Sillah was Charles’s wife, and no second marriage for Charles has been found between 1880 and 1900.

Research Notes and Open Questions

  • The NUMIDENT is the only located record naming Mary as Leon’s mother. FamilySearch’s linked Mary entry says “son,” and the linked Major entry says “spouse”; those are related views of the same source, not independent corroboration.
  • Leon’s 1950 census entry supports his Birmingham household but names no parents. Its age-derived birth year (about 1910) differs from the NUMIDENT birth date (1908); preserve the discrepancy rather than treating the census age as exact.
  • 25 November 2026: Mary’s family was found in the early records under the surname Rigby, and her parents are Charles Rigby and Silla Kimball Rigby. The 1900 census lists Mary, 19, born April 1881, in her father’s household in Perdido; the 1880 census shows her mother Sillah with Charles and daughters Ella and Ida in Stockton; and her marriage to Major Nye on 25 November 1901 in Baldwin County is recorded on page 140 of the county marriages.
  • Find a Grave exact-name searches did not identify a plausible Alabama memorial for Mary Riggby. Exact-name results returned unrelated older English memorials; a broad Mary Rigby Alabama search returned many same-name records without a verified link. None is assigned.
  • No death record for Mary has been found. Alabama deaths from 1910 to 1922 were searched without a match. A 1950 Mobile death record for a Mary Nay, born about 1876, parents unknown, is not assigned to her: the birth year is five or six years off and no spouse is named.
  • Learmon, age 1 in the 1910 census, may be Leon under an earlier name; see Leon’s page.
  • No photograph or headstone photo for Mary has been found; Find a Grave, BillionGraves, FamilySearch tree photos, and free newspapers were searched on 25 November 2026.
  • Next useful records: a death record for Mary Nye after 1910; the 1920 census for the family; the deciding record for whether Learmon is Leon (see Leon’s page).
  • Follow-up dated 25 November 2026: a sample of the Alabama 1900 and 1910 Niles Soundex cards did not verify Mary in a household or identify Mary’s parents or siblings. The sample was not exhaustive. A reported Black William Niles household in Montgomery in 1910 (William, 54; wife Angie, 47; son Grady, 19) remains an unlinked lead; its card locator and full transcription were not retained, so it is not assigned to Mary Riggby Niles or Leon. A separate Birmingham N-420 Soundex card coded White is a different, excluded household. See the dated research note in this profile folder.
