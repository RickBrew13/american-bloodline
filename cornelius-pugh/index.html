Family Relationship Notes

Cornelius Pugh was born 6 October 1902 in Bullock County, Alabama, and died in 1961 in Birmingham. His World War II draft card gives his birth date and place and his employer as the Southern Car Manufacturing Company, and the censuses put him at 615 13th Street, Birmingham. He married Rosa Belle White on 7 September 1924 in Jefferson County. The 1930 census finds him head of his own household in Birmingham — himself, his wife Rosabelle, and their daughter Ruby, age 7 — and the 1950 census finds him still at 615 13th Street, a laborer at a freight car company, with his wife Rosa, his brother-in-law Charlie Moody, his sister-in-law Annie B. Moody, and his niece Wilodean Davis.

He first appears in the records as Cornelius Pew, 8, in the 1910 census at Midway, Bullock County, and again as Cornelius Pew, 17, in the 1920 census at Beat 2 (Enon). Two other Pughs of his generation lived in Bullock County — Vessie C. Pugh, born 8 October 1903, and Emma C. Pugh, born 11 October 1901 — but nothing ties them to Cornelius.

Roger Pugh is very likely his brother, but it is not proved. In the 1940 census Roger heads the household at the same address, 615 13th Street, with his wife Rosa; Ruby and her husband Joseph Collins live there as his niece and nephew, and Roger works as a machinist at a car company, the same trade as Cornelius. No record names Roger’s parents, and no census before 1940 has been found for him, so he is not listed here as a sibling.

His wife’s name is not fully settled. The 1924 marriage record calls her Beesa Belle White in one copy and Rosa Belle White in another; the 1930 census calls her Rosabelle Pugh; Ruby’s 1939 marriage names her mother Rosabelle Grant and her 1945 marriage names her Rosa Bell White. Her 1951 death record names her parents Pete White and Sallie Parker. Grant and White may be two names for one woman, or two different women — the records do not settle that, and no record yet explains the Grant form.

One more puzzle: the Rosa in these households is 22 in 1930 as Cornelius’s wife, 34 in 1940 as Roger’s wife, and 44 in 1950 as Cornelius’s wife again. That reads as one woman only if she was Cornelius’s wife, then Roger’s, then Cornelius’s again; the other possibility is two Birmingham women named Rosa, both born about 1906. It is left open.

The Norman Patterson lead does not connect to this Cornelius. Norman’s Social Security record names his father as Cornelius Pugh and his mother as Mattie Patterson, but his obituary names his parents as Henry Paige and Mattie Patterson Paige, and the tree keeps a separate Cornelius for that lead. No marriage of any Cornelius Pugh to Mattie Patterson exists. The Ancestry proposal that David H. Pugh and Sarah Elizabeth Bush were Cornelius’s parents has no support on FamilySearch — no record names them as the parents of a Cornelius Pugh.

His mother is Winnie Pew, named in the 1910 and 1920 censuses of Bullock County, Alabama. In 1910 she is 45, widowed, head of household at Midway, with Cornelius, 8, as her youngest child at home; in 1920 she is 54, widowed, head of household at Beat 2 (Enon), with Cornelius, 17, still at home. The family name is spelled Pew in both censuses; Cornelius appears as Pugh in all his adult records. No record yet links the two spellings directly. His father is Will Brent, named as the father of Winnie’s son Will on Will’s Social Security record; no record names Brent as Cornelius’s father directly — he is listed on Rick’s word, as Winnie’s husband and the probable father of her children.

Life Story

Cornelius Pugh was born on 6 October 1902 in Bullock County, Alabama. As a boy he lived in Bullock County as Cornelius Pew — the 1910 census has him at Midway, eight years old, and the 1920 census has him at Beat 2 (Enon), seventeen. By 1924 he was in Birmingham, where he married Rosa Belle White on 7 September of that year. In 1930 he headed his own household there with his wife and their young daughter Ruby, working as a machinist in the car wheel works. His daughter Ruby married Joseph Leslie Collins in Birmingham in 1939 and George Howard Moore in Prattville in 1945, and both marriage records name Cornelius as her father. His World War II draft card, registered in February 1942, puts him at 615 13th Street in Birmingham, employed by the Southern Car Manufacturing Company. The 1950 census finds him still at that address, a laborer at a freight car company, living with his wife Rosa, his brother-in-law and sister-in-law Charlie and Annie Moody, and his niece Wilodean Davis. He died in Birmingham in 1961, aged 59, and is buried at Shadowlawn Memorial Park. The likeliest next record — the image of that death certificate — can only be read at a FamilySearch center.
