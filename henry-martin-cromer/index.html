Family Relationship Notes

FamilySearch and the Rockcastle County Cromer compilation identify Henry Martin “Hen” Cromer’s parents as David Crockett Cromer and Elizabeth Southard. The 1860 original census shows a David Croomer (age 21) with wife Elizabeth and children William (3) and Mary C. (1) in Rockcastle County. Henry was born in 1869 and is not on that page; this is contextual support for a candidate household, not a direct Henry parent record.

Confidence in the David-and-Elizabeth parent assignment is tentative (about 65%). Henry’s 1955 death certificate names his father only as “Dave Cromer” and leaves the mother unknown. The census age for David conflicts with compiled 1834 and family-letter 1838 birth-year claims. Preserve those conflicts.

FamilySearch’s compiled profile lists Henry and Sarah Louisa’s ten children. The 1910 original census directly enumerates eight; Rosa Lee is not in the indexed household and Austin was born later. In 1930 Henry appears with later wife Martha and step-sons Arley and Earl Doan; do not attach the Doan boys to Sarah Louisa.

Research Notes and Open Questions

  • Parent assignment: David Crockett Cromer and Elizabeth Southard are a tentative working parent couple for Henry (about 65%). His death certificate names father “Dave Cromer” but does not name the mother. The 1860 census supports a David Croomer/Elizabeth household but predates Henry’s birth; it does not prove the parent-child relationship.
  • David’s birth year is inconsistent across sources: the FamilySearch profile says 1834, a family-letter transcription reports 1838, and the 1860 census image appears to give age 21. Do not force these into one date.
  • Children with Sarah Louisa: the ten-person list is compiled; the 1910 household directly includes eight children. Rosa Lee is not in the indexed 1910 household; Austin was born after it. The 1930 household includes Henry’s later wife and stepchildren.
  • Portrait search: the FamilySearch profile shows one attached photo count, but the image could not be retrieved for visual identification. No portrait is asserted here; the profile displays documentary record scans.
