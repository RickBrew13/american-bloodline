Life Story

Catharine Staples was born about 1800–1801 in Virginia. On 24 October 1822 she married Lampkin McKinney in Jefferson County, Tennessee; the marriage register names her "Catharine Staples" — the one document that gives her maiden name.

She kept house through the family's move to Morgan County and the farming years at Crooked Fork. The records call her Catharine in 1822, 1860 and 1880, and Elizabeth in 1850; the research treats the two names as one woman.

In June 1880 she was living with her daughter Elizabeth and son-in-law Ezekiel Cochran in Morgan County, recorded at age 87 — an age that does not fit the other records. She was dead by 1900, when the heirs' deed was signed without her. No record names her father; the question stays open, and the full discussion is in the research folder.

Research Notes and Open Questions

  • How to read the grades: DOCUMENTED means a record was read directly. SUPPORTED means the evidence points that way but no single record proves it. FAMILY ACCOUNT means it comes from the family's own telling. UNPROVED means it is a lead only, with no record behind it yet.
  • Parents: No record names Catharine's father. The question stays open; the full candidate discussion is in the research folder.
  • Name: the 1822 register spells her "Catharine" — that spelling leads here. "Catherine" (1860, 1880) and "Elizabeth" (1850) are recorded variants of the same woman. The combined name "Catherine Elizabeth" appears in no record and is not used.
  • Birth year: about 1800–1801 from the 1850 and 1860 censuses; the 1880 age of 87 would make it about 1793 and does not fit.
  • Death: dead by 1900 is supported by her absence from the 22 January 1900 heirs' deed — absence from a deed is not a death record, so the grade is supported, not documented.
  • One unread document could settle her father's identity: the family Bible page in a Revolutionary War pension file, which is subscription-only online and has not been read. The research folder holds the details.
  • Possible twins: Rankin and Nancy Ann were both 3 in 1850; Alexander and Martha were both 3 in 1860 — both pairs unproved.
  • Ellen (born about 1828) and Elean (born about 1835): the most coherent reading is two daughters; one daughter with a bad age in one census stays possible.
  • Age drift across censuses: Andrew is 22 in 1850 and 38 in 1860; Pleasant 20 in 1850 and 33 in 1860. Birth years are carried as ranges; no child's birth year is documented.
  • The 14-child roster carried on the trees is not adopted: it counts Elizabeth twice and includes Alexander, Martha, Catherine and Rufus without proving they are children.
  • Open pulls: the family Bible page in the Revolutionary War pension file (subscription-only; free at a library or family history center); the children's Tennessee death certificates; the second Elizabeth McKinney (born about 1843) on one tree, unproved.
