Life Story

Lampkin McKinney was born about 1799. His birthplace is in conflict between the two censuses that record it: the 1850 census image reads North Carolina, the 1860 census reads Virginia. He first appears as head of household in Jefferson County, Tennessee in 1830, and again in 1840. On 24 October 1822 he married Catharine Staples in Jefferson County; the marriage register (No. 1628) is the one document that gives her maiden name.

Between 1840 and February 1846 the family moved to Morgan County, Tennessee, where Lampkin witnessed a deed in February 1846. By 1850 he was farming at Crooked Fork with $100 in real estate; by 1860 he was still farming there and could not read or write. The family held an interest in a 75-acre tract on the Emory River, entered in 1826; his heirs quitclaimed their interest in it in 1899 and 1900.

Nine children are named in the census households and the heirs' deed; three of them — Rankin, Pleasant and Betsy — signed as his heirs in 1900. No portrait and no gravestone of Lampkin is known. He was alive on 15 June 1860 and dead by 22 January 1900. No record names his father; the question stays open, and the full discussion is in the research folder.

Research Notes and Open Questions

  • How to read the grades: DOCUMENTED means a record was read directly. SUPPORTED means the evidence points that way but no single record proves it. FAMILY ACCOUNT means it comes from the family's own telling. UNPROVED means it is a lead only, with no record behind it yet.
  • Parents: No record names Lampkin's father. The question stays open; the full candidate discussion is in the research folder.
  • Birthplace in conflict: the 1850 census image reads "N.C."; the 1860 census reads Virginia. The 1860 birthplace column has not been image-checked.
  • Birth year about 1799: 1830, 1840 and 1860 agree; the 1850 age of 60 is the outlier, confirmed from the image itself.
  • The 1810 "James Lampkin" household: the image's row total is 6, so the old claim of "10–16 boys" is retired. The household apparently included two males aged 10–16, one of whom could have been Lampkin — a weakened compatibility only.
  • "Died circa 1860" in an old compiled account is contradicted: Lampkin was alive 15 June 1860.
  • An 1858 poll tax of $1.15 is cited in one compiled account with no record behind it; it is not carried as a fact.
  • Possible twins: Rankin and Nancy Ann were both 3 in 1850; Alexander and Martha were both 3 in 1860 — both pairs unproved.
  • Ellen (born about 1828) and Elean (born about 1835): the most coherent reading is two daughters; one daughter with a bad age in one census stays possible.
  • The 1860 colour column marks Alexander and Martha "M"; every other household member's column is blank. Whether the marks are an enumerator's error or meaningful is unknowable; they are recorded here as written.
  • Age drift across censuses: Andrew is 22 in 1850 and 38 in 1860; Pleasant 20 in 1850 and 33 in 1860. Birth years are carried as ranges; no child's birth year is documented.
  • The 14-child roster carried on the trees is not adopted: it counts Elizabeth twice and includes Alexander, Martha, Catherine and Rufus without proving they are children.
  • Open pulls: re-read the 1899/1900 quitclaim deeds and the 1826 entry images in one signed-in session; image-check the 1860 birthplace column and the blank 1860 estate values; a third read of the 1840 Jefferson household names; the children's Tennessee death certificates; the second Elizabeth McKinney (born about 1843) on one tree, unproved.
