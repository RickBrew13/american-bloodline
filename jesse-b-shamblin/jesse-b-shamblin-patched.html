Life Story

Jesse B. Shamblin was born about 1796 in Harrison County, Virginia — or about 1793 by the 1850 census age, or 1797 by FamilySearch — and no birth record settles it. He was a farmer on Grass Lick in Jackson County, where the local history calls him the first pioneer on the left fork: “Of the pioneers, Jesse Shambllin was the first.” He and Isaiah Shamblen are listed as resident landowners at Grasslick from 1832 to 1842. In January 1822, in Mason County — Jackson County did not exist until 1831 — he married Margaret Parsons, in a double wedding with his brother George and her sister Barbara Ellen Parsons, eight days apart by the carried dates. They had eight children, and the 1850 census finds the household at District 27: Jesse, 57, farmer; Margaret, 46; Wilson, 18, a blacksmith; Ezekial, 16; George, 14; Jesse, 9; Henry, 7.

His death is the open question of this page, and it is carried as a three-way conflict, not settled. The stone says 20 March 1852, aged 55 years 5 months. A will dated 31 January 1853 and a probate order of 11 April 1853, with his son John as executor, say he was alive into 1853. And family word, in Jim White’s grandmother’s Bible, says he was struck by lightning in August 1856 and buried in Wayne County — which fits the thunderstorm story told in the family. No one date is adopted here; the original will image and the full estate file are the single most valuable next pull. His burial is likewise unsettled: the transcription places him in the Shamblin Cemetery on the Simmons Farm at Kenna, while Find a Grave places him at Warren Chapel Cemetery.

His parents are carried as Isaiah Isaack Shamblin × Martha J. Parsons — the position in the research folder, at UNPROVED. No primary record links Jesse to Isaiah, and the conflicts run deep: Martha’s surname (Parsons against Cunningham), Isaiah’s own parentage (Aaron C. Chamblin Jr. × Monica Shore — elimination only, and the 1788 Botetourt will of Aaron Chamblin Sr. excludes Isaiah as his son), and a tree that gives an Isaiah born 1810 as father, which is chronologically impossible. The best untried route to his parents runs through his sister Polly, who married Daniel Taylor Casto — the local history calls her probably Jesse’s sister, and a 1997 query names Isaiah as Polly’s father.

No portrait of Jesse exists, and none of Margaret either. No military service has been found for him, though he was of age for the War of 1812 — no service, muster, pension or bounty-land record, and the Fold3 check was blocked. His son George Washington’s alleged service in the 7th West Virginia Cavalry is disproved — he is not in the official 1864 state roster. The service in the family belongs to the collateral line: George Shamblin, his brother, served in Captain William King’s Company of State militia and died in service, and George’s sons Isaiah and Lawson served in Company G, 3rd West Virginia Cavalry, and Thaddeus in Company F, 7th West Virginia Cavalry. Their sons Isaiah, Lawson and Thaddeus are first cousins of Jesse’s son George Washington, not his younger brothers, whatever the old memories say.
