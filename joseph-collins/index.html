Life Story

Joseph Collins is known here through the 1939 Alabama marriage index, which names him as the father of a 21-year-old groom also indexed as Joseph Collins. The FamilySearch public family tree pairs Joseph with Sarah Johnson and identifies their son as Joseph Leslie Collins. Leslie’s later records support that identity link. Beyond these records, Joseph’s dates, birthplace, later life, marriage to Sarah, and parentage are not established. A 1940 census index entry for a same-age Joe Collins with Sara Rogers is kept separate because it conflicts on the mother’s surname and marital status.

His mother died around 1927, when Homer was sixteen. His father later married Julia Bell Kelly, and that tied the family to the Kellys twice over: Julia's sister Sarah Hazel Kelly, a Georgia girl, became Homer's wife.

Homer and Sarah married on 6 August 1930 in Hamilton, Ohio. Six children followed through the 1930s: Eunice, Eugene, the twins Elmer and Earl, a daughter, and Homer Jr. In 1940 the family of eight lived on Ford Road in Morgan County, in the same house as five years before.

By 1950 they had moved north to Cincinnati, to 517 12th Street, and by 1953 to 519 E. 12th Street. In January 1952 the twins joined the Marines on their seventeenth birthday. Elmer served in California; Earl went to Korea and was wounded on 27 July 1953, the last day of the war; the family was told it was on Pork Chop Hill, but the Marines' last fight was at Boulder City and Hill 111. Homer lost Earl to a car crash in Kentucky in 1959.

Sarah died in Hamilton, Ohio, on 28 February 1987. Homer died in Palm Beach, Florida, on 8 March 1991, at seventy-nine, formerly of College Hill. He was buried beside Sarah at Spring Grove Cemetery in Cincinnati, under their shared stone. He left twenty-four grandchildren and thirty-eight great-grandchildren.

Research Notes and Open Questions

  • The 1939 FamilySearch marriage index is a record source. It explicitly names Joseph Collins and Sarah Johnson as the parents of a 21-year-old groom named Joseph Collins. The event/index does not by itself prove either parent’s birth/death details or that the two parents were married to each other.
  • FamilySearch public tree profiles GDS3-SRK and GDS3-7M5 pair these people and list at least one son, Joseph Leslie Collins. Treat as a useful compiled-family source; its two displayed source links are duplicate references to the 1939 marriage index, not independent corroboration.
  • Joseph Leslie identity bridge: 1939 groom Joseph Collins, age 21; Leslie’s NUMIDENT names father Joseph Collins and mother Ruby M Pugh; Leslie’s 1959 marriage names her father Joseph Leslie Collins and mother Ruby Mae Pugh. Together these support identifying the groom as Joseph Leslie, but the 1939 image remains unreadable here.
  • A 1940 census index lists Joe Collins, 21, in Alabama City with mother Sara Rogers, widowed, and marks Joe single. It is not assigned because “Sara Rogers” conflicts with Sarah Johnson and the 1940 index status conflicts with the known 1939 marriage. The original schedule was not inspected.
