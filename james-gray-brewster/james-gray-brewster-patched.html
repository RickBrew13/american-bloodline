Life Story

James Gray Brewster Sr. was born about 1798 in Virginia and died in October 1887 in Wayne County, Kentucky. He was a blacksmith — the 1850, 1860 and 1870 censuses all give that trade — and a farmer by 1880. He lived most of his life on Clear Fork, in Fentress and Morgan Counties, Tennessee, and moved to Kentucky in his sixties, in the years around the Civil War. He married Phoebe Cox; no marriage record has been found. Their son John W. Brewster, born 3 August 1823, carries the line.

The land records are the firmest ground. Three Tennessee grants stand in his name — #465 (100 acres, 1828), #468 (6.25 acres, 1827) and #506 (100 acres, 1828) — and two land entries were read in full: Entry #929 of 7 December 1847, in which “Jas Gray Brewster” enters 2,500 acres on the Laurel Fork of White Oak Creek, “so as to include the present residence of said Brewster,” and Entry #948 of 12 May 1849, “James Brewster & his son Jesse Brewster.” The middle name Gray is in the 1847 entry twice. In July 1841 he sold 106.25 acres on Clear Fork to Isaac Stockton for $30. An old compiled account’s “grant #467” is not his. Brewster Ford on Clear Fork is documented as a place-name; that it was named for James is unproved.

His last years were hard. The Wayne County court declared him a pauper twice — 24 October 1883, with neighbour Samuel McBeath authorised to provide for him at $25 a year, and again 26 October 1886, with L. Phillips as his committee at $20 a year. Phoebe was declared a pauper on 23 January 1888, three months after his death. He is buried in no marked grave that has been found; family tradition says Brewster Hollow, and the Otter Creek and Slick Ford ground is the same country. Cedar-wood markers would explain the absence of a stone.

The children are carried honestly at 14–18, not the 23 of an old compiled account. Seventeen names are listed on this page; Jeremiah F. and Frederick Miller are very likely one boy, and three of the youngest — Adaline, Susanah and Monroe G. — may be the children of his daughter Rebecca, not his own. Two of his sons died violently: Jesse, struck by lightning in August 1856, and Frederick Miller, murdered on election day in August 1883 at Neatsville, his killers hanged the following March. His son John W. became a farmer and a Free Will Baptist minister and carries the line.

His mother is carried as Susannah Millard, at UNPROVED — the placement traces to one unsourced 1998 forum reply, and no record names James as her son. His father is not listed on this page. The origin of “Gray” in his name is unknown: Gray families appear beside Brewsters in the 1796 and 1801 Russell County, Virginia, tax lists, but that proves presence only, not a link. An elder “James Gray Brewster” of the 1700s, in Augusta and Rockingham Counties, Virginia, is a different man — no period record styles him “Gray” — and the two are not merged.

He lived through the Civil War in the Upper Cumberland: Fentress County voted 84 percent against secession, Morgan 93 percent, Scott 96.5 percent, and the country was torn by guerrilla war — Champ Ferguson, Tinker Dave Beaty’s Union scouts — through the years his family crossed into Kentucky. No church membership has been found for him, and no military service is proved. No portrait of James is known — not in the research folder, and not on any tree site checked.
