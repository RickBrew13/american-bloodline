Her father Ransom — a Muhlenberg County farmer, draft-registered in 1863, later a justice of the peace in Morgan County — bought the Bone Camp Creek tract in Morgan County in 1875, and the family settled there. He was the younger Ransom, born about 1840, the son of Gersham Davis and Permelia Whitaker — not the Ransom born in 1813, Jesse Davis's son. Life Story

Amanda J. Davis was born in 1862 in Muhlenberg County, Kentucky, the daughter of Ransom Davis and Sarah A. E. Pitman. Her father, a Muhlenberg County farmer later a justice of the peace in Morgan County, Tennessee, bought eighty-three acres on Bone Camp Creek in Morgan County in 1875, from Amanda's uncle Ephraim Pitman, and the family settled there. Amanda's grandparents were Gersham Davis and Permelia Whitaker on her father's side, and William Pitman and Sally Melton on her mother's — the older books' placement of Gersham and Permelia as her parents is one generation off.

On 31 January 1881, in Morgan County, Amanda married James Alexander McKinney, whose mother Betsy had married Ezekiel Cochran in 1867. Amanda and James raised their children on the Bone Camp Creek land in Morgan and Scott Counties; in 1898 James was taxed on one-third of her father's tract, her brother William having sold his third in 1896. Her brother Balaam Agy Davis is documented at Sunbright, buried in Union Grove Cemetery.

Amanda died about 1893 in Morgan County, by family tradition only — no death record or grave has been found, and the date is deliberately not entered on her tree profiles. Its only support is its consistency with James's remarriage about that year, to Beulah Pittman. No photograph of Amanda has been found.
