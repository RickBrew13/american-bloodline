Family Relationship Notes

The 1950 U.S. Census places James W. McGregor, 42, born Kentucky and working as a minister in a church, in Boone County, Kentucky, with wife Mary E. and sons William C., James T., and Wayne M. This directly supports the couple and household relationships, while the image/index does not name James’s parents.

Ora Butler Locker’s 1962 McGregor genealogy (printed p. 48) identifies James as the son of William Clarence McGregor and Cora Johnson, records their 21 February 1935 marriage, and lists Roy Logan as William Clarence’s brother (James’s paternal uncle), not James’s sibling. Rick’s recorded Geni family-tree work independently gives William Clarence and Cora B. McGregor as James’s parents. These are derivative family-history/tree sources, not a birth certificate; the working parent assignment is about 80% confidence, not a proved relationship.

The same 1962 genealogy gives James’s birth as 22 January 1908 and death as 23 January 1999 in later family-tree / memorial reports. The attached Find a Grave photo associated with the James and Marye memorials appears to show a marker reading “JAMES W. JAN 2, 1908 JUN 2, 1998 / MARY E. APR 11, 1908 JAN 2, 1998,” which conflicts with the family-group dates and Marye’s 1994 obituary. I do not use that image as their verified marker or silently resolve the date conflict.

Bill’s 2021 obituary names James W. and Marye (Hooper) McGregor as his parents and identifies James Thomas and Wayne Martin as brothers. Tom’s Ohio death certificate names James McGregor as his father and Marye Hooper as his mother. For Tom’s own birth year, the certificate says 1936 while Geni and family trees say 1937; that conflict remains visible.

Life Story

Rev. James William “Papaw” McGregor was born in Benton, Kentucky, in 1908 according to the family genealogy and was recorded as a minister in the 1950 census. He and Marye Elizabeth Hooper raised three sons—Bill, Tom, and Wayne. Family memories place the couple later in Harrison, Ohio, where they lived for a time in a renovated one-room schoolhouse with a large garden. Their son Bill’s obituary and Tom’s death certificate support the parent-child relationships. The exact details of James’s death and burial remain less secure than the family group.
