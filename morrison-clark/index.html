Life Story

Morrison Clark was born 11 July 1851 in Rising Sun, Ohio County, Indiana, on the Ohio River — the son, supported but not proved, of Brower H. Clark and Sarah Padgett. In 1870, listed as nineteen, he was a flatboatman, living in Brower’s household at Rising Sun with his younger brothers Albert and Charles and his sister Ida. He married first, if the folder’s carry is right, Jennie Crouch in 1873; no record of that marriage has been found. On 14 September 1878 he married Elizabeth Nightengal — recorded as Lizzie Young — in Dearborn County, Indiana. By 1880 he was a blacksmith at Aurora, with Elizabeth and two small daughters, Annie Young and Ida Mae. By 1900 the family had crossed the river into Kentucky: Morrison was a puddler in a Covington rolling mill, living at 424 Crescent Avenue with Elizabeth, Laura, and Elmer, and a nephew, Willie A. Nightingale, also a puddler. The 1900 census counts the loss plainly: eight children born, four living. In 1910 Morrison and Elizabeth were still together in Newport, with their son Elmer, 24, at home. Morrison died 31 August 1917 at Newport, of dysentery, aged 66, and was buried in Highland Cemetery at Fort Mitchell, in Section 12, grave 722. Elizabeth’s death is unknown — no death or burial record has been found for her. No portrait of Morrison has been found.

His parentage is the open question of this profile. The 1870 household puts a nineteen-year-old Morrison in Brower Clark’s house at Rising Sun, the town Morrison’s own records name as his birthplace, and the blacksmith’s trade runs from Brower — a blacksmith’s striker in 1860 — to Morrison the blacksmith in 1880. Against that, the complete county-wide read of Ohio County in 1860 found B. H. Clark with Sarah J. and only a two-year-old daughter: no Morrison anywhere in the county at age eight or nine. The 1880 Aurora household of “Brewer Clark” reports parents born in New York and Pennsylvania, against Morrison’s Indiana-born parents. Whether Brower of 1870 and Brewer of 1880 are the same man is unproved. The parentage is carried at about 75% — supported, not proved — and no tree should attach Brower and Sarah without that caveat.

His first marriage is likewise unsettled. The folder carries a marriage to Jennie Crouch on 31 March 1873, but no record of it sits in the research folder, and FamilySearch names the wife Jane Ann Crouch, 1851–1930. Geni shows a divorce from Jennie Crouch. FamilySearch gives the couple a daughter, Rosetta Clark, born 1874; Find a Grave gives Morrison a daughter Musa Grace Clark Worsham, 1873–1951 — the two may be the same daughter under different names, and the question is unresolved. His second marriage, to Elizabeth, is the documented one. Why Elizabeth married as Lizzie Young is open — the Joseph H. Young marriage of 1873 belongs to the other Elizabeth Nightengal as far as the records show. Morrison was a child during the Civil War and has no military service. No church affiliation has been found for him.
