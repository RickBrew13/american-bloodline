# Leroy C. Moore × Mila Moore — research update

**Date:** 25 November 2026

## Evidence reviewed

The FamilySearch 1940 U.S. Census indexes the household in Detroit Ward 6, Wayne County, ED 84-246, sheet 13B, line 63, digital folder 005461733, image 352. It names Leroy Moore, age 42, Iowa-born, head; Mila Moore, age 23, Indiana-born, wife; and Patrick, age four, and Michael, age two, sons. Ages imply Leroy born about 1897–1898 and Mila about 1916–1917. The indexed race field is White for both. The 1940 original sheet was not reviewed in this pass.

The 1950 census images were retrieved and visually reviewed: sheet 27A (FamilySearch image 28; index entry dated 7 Apr) and continuation sheet 28A (image 29; Patrick entry dated 10 Apr), Dearborn, Wayne County, ED 84-103, digital folder 108985260. FamilySearch indexes the head as “Leray C Moore,” age 52, married, line 27, with wife Elizabeth, age 39; address 3337; occupation grinder, auto manufacturer. The continuation lists Raymond J. and Isabell Kroft, Florence M., and Marvin E. as stepchildren, and Patrick L. (14), Michael J. (12), and Gary W. (birth recorded Dec 1949) as sons. The record does not name the Moore boys’ mother or explain Mila’s absence. The 1950 age/birth year is consistent with the 1940 estimate for Leroy. Archival copies of both census page images are saved under `leroy-c-moore/documents/` with citations and image links on both profiles.

The 1950 records corroborate Leroy’s fatherhood of Patrick and Michael, and independently add Gary W. as a son. Gary is listed on Leroy’s profile only; the record does not connect him to Mila. The four stepchildren are not represented as Leroy’s biological children. No separate profiles were created.

## Family oral history and unresolved conflict

A descendant-reported family account says Leroy met his first wife Mila during World War I and describes her as “full-blood Native American”; her tribe is unknown. Preserve this as oral family history, not proof of ancestry, enrollment, or blood degree. The 1940 age of 23 implies Mila was about an infant/toddler in 1917–1918, a substantial chronology conflict. Do not assume the census age is wrong, change the reported period, or merge another same-name Mila without a bridge. The 1940 “White” field is the enumerator’s category, not proof for or against Native ancestry. NARA’s collection description says Indian Census Rolls chiefly cover people formally affiliated with federally supervised tribes; no such roll has yet been searched for or identified for Mila.

FamilySearch’s “Similar Records” display on Mila’s 1940 record surfaced a title “Myrtle Or Mila Christina Moore” in the Michigan Death Certificates, 1921–1952 collection. No record ID or direct identity link was accessible in the public view. Treat it as a lead only, not Mila’s death record.
