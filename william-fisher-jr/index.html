Life Story

No photo of William Fisher Jr. has been found yet. This story is built from the research notes in the research folder.

William Fisher Jr. was born about 1798 in Virginia — the county unproven; an old compiled account’s “born 1798 in Kanawha County” has no record behind it. His parents are unknown in the records, a genuine brick wall: no estate, will, deed, or census record names them. He is carried on this page with William Leo Fisher × Mary Ann “Polly” McWilliams as his parents at UNPROVED, per the position on the Each Generation page.

The “Jr.” after his name is a relative designation, not a son-of relationship: the 1850 census index lists both “William Senr. 52” — this man — and “’Wm, Jr’ 29”, a younger William. He is a landholder on Pocatalico, verified on the 1832–1842 resident-landowner tax list, and he heads a household in Jackson County from 1840. His mother is named on the page as “Mary Ann ‘Polly’ McWilliams” per FamilySearch; Geni lists her as “Mary Fisher”.

He married Sarah F. Samuels about 1821 — everything about her is unproved: no record of her parents, birth, marriage, or death survives, and she was dead by 1853. The 1840 census tally #703 for his household counts 9 free white persons and no slaves, and that tally was image-verified. In 1850 the household held Sarah, six children, and a 30-year-old Betsy Holly.

On 4 December 1853 he married the widow Susanna Crites Koontz. The marriage index is found; the record image is restricted to a FamilySearch center or affiliate library. The 1860 census at Grasslick puts William 61 and Susan 43 with the Koontz stepchildren and their own young children Isaac M., James M., and Elizabeth C., plus “Ro S Fisher 27” — read as Robert S. Fisher. The 1870 census in Mill Creek Township reads William 71 and Susan 53 with Isaac M. 15 and James M. 12, and two people not yet identified: “Elvin” 22 and “Margaret” 21.

William’s will, written 6 January 1877 with a codicil of 18 September 1879, was proved 15 March 1880 — his death is bracketed to September 1879 – March 1880. The will was read in full. It is a short document: the wife is never named, only Isaac M. and James M. are called “son”, and each child receives $100. The $100 bequests to the Sarah children are legacies, not proof of paternity. The will names no executor. It was signed by mark. The burial place is unknown — no cemetery transcription checked lists him.

His son James M. Fisher married Nancy Jane Casto in 1878, and through James the line runs down to the Fisher families of Jackson County.
