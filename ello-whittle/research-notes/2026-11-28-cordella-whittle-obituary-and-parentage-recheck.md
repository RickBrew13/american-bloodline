# Avery Ello Whittle — Cordella Whittle obituary and parentage recheck
**Research date:** 28 November 2026  
**Scope:** Rechecked the Tucker/Cordella lead against the transcribed 1951 newspaper obituary and the exact family details it provides.

## Newspaper obituary details
The Shy Genealogist published a transcription of Cordella Whittle's obituary from *The Russell County News*, dated 22 March 1951. It states:
- Cordella was born **14 May 1878** and died **12 March 1951**.
- She married **R. S. Whittle, known as Bob Whittle, on 24 December 1895**; he died **1 December 1949**.
- “To this union” were born **six children**. The transcription names five: **Otha, Rese, Jim Elmer, Caleb and Grady Whittle**.
- She was survived by two sisters and three brothers, 17 grandchildren and three great-grandchildren.
- Funeral services were held at **Clear Fork Baptist Church** on 14 March 1951; burial was in **Old Eli Cemetery**. The funeral home was H. E. Pruitt of Jamestown.

Source: The Shy Genealogist, “The Passing Of Cordella Whittle,” transcription of *The Russell County News*, 22 March 1951: https://theshygenealogist.wordpress.com/page/3/

## What this does — and does not — establish about Stanton Bell Whittle
Stanton Bell Whittle's derivative index gives 10 July 1893 as his birth date. That is about two years before Cordella's documented marriage to Bob Whittle. If the birth date is correct, Cordella could conceivably have been Stanton's mother before that marriage, but the 1951 obituary does **not** name Stanton among the five children it identifies as born to her marriage to Bob. It reports six children but leaves one unnamed in the transcription. The unnamed sixth child cannot be assumed to be Stanton.

The obituary's use of “to this union” makes it especially important not to treat all six children as children of the same parentage without the original newspaper clipping and independent records. It also does not name Cordella's maiden name, parents, or siblings. The Tucker identification remains a secondary family-tree lead only.

## Tucker family lead — retained as a separate, unproven possibility
PeopleLegacy's family grouping for Lillie Bell Tucker McBeath lists Cordelia E. Tucker Whittle (1878–1951) among the children of Eli Tucker (1849–1919) and Narcissus “Arsis” Popplewell Tucker (1849–1908). A 1998 Russell County query independently identifies Louisa “Babe” Tucker as a daughter of Eli and Narcissus, but does not independently establish Cordelia's parentage. These remain compiled/researcher-submitted leads, not proof.

## Records to pursue next
1. Inspect the original 22 March 1951 newspaper clipping image, rather than relying only on the online transcription; determine whether the sixth child's name is legible or omitted by the transcription.
2. Locate the 1900 Russell County population schedule for R. S./Bob and Cordella Whittle. The household should show every resident child, each relationship to the head, birth month/year, and the number of years married/children born/living for Cordella.
3. Search the 1910 Russell County population schedule for the same household and all Whittle children.
4. Search Old Eli Cemetery's original cemetery register/headstones for Cordella, Bob and the children; the online obituary transcription does not establish the exact grave locations.
5. Search Clear Fork Baptist Church and Coffey's Chapel Methodist records for membership, baptism, marriage or funeral entries. Cordella's obituary mentions both church affiliations.
6. Obtain Cordella's death certificate and Bob Whittle's 1949 death certificate, if available, for parents, spouse, informant and residence.

## Profile treatment
Keep Cordella's Tucker parentage and any relationship to Stanton Bell Whittle labelled **unproven**. Do not add her as Stanton's mother, and do not add Otha, Rese, Jim Elmer, Caleb or Grady as Stanton's siblings unless an original census/vital record establishes the relationship. The 1900 census remains the most direct next record.
