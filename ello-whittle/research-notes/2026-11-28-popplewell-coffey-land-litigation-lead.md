# Avery Ello Whittle — 1913 Coffey v. Humble and Popplewell land lead
**Research date:** 28 November 2026  
**Scope:** Followed a Kentucky Court of Appeals case that names a Popplewell land transfer and a woman named Narcissa Coffey. This is an extended Russell County Popplewell-family lead, not a proven Avery/Whittle relationship.

## Court case
**Coffey v. Humble**, 154 Ky. 708, 159 S.W. 554 (Ky. Ct. App. 1913), decided 19 September 1913, concerns a 40-acre tract on Wolf and Dud creeks in Russell County, Kentucky. The infant appellants were **Essie Bell Coffey, Jonah Lee Coffey and Otha Lee Coffey**, represented by statutory guardian Thomas Coffey. The opinion identifies their mother as **Narcissa Coffey**.

The appellees' account of the title chain states that:
- In 1901, Narcissa Coffey acquired the land by purchase and deed from **Junius Popplewell**.
- In 1903, she sold it to James Tucker for $75 cash.
- The land passed through Asa Lawless, Thomas Coffey, William Stephens and Sandy Tucker, then to J. E. Humble.
- The court held the later oral land sales unenforceable under the statute of frauds; the title had not been divested from Narcissa, and at her death passed to her surviving children as heirs. The court reversed and remanded for further proceedings.

This provides a specific 1901 deed from Junius Popplewell to a woman named Narcissa Coffey and a 1913 legal record describing her children and property. The deed itself and the case's underlying circuit-court record have not yet been inspected.

## Possible Popplewell family connection — unproved
A community FamilySearch tree for Caroline Chamberlain and Mason B. Popplewell lists **Junius C. Popplewell** and **Narcissus Popplewell (1849–1908)** among their children. If this is the same Junius who conveyed the 40-acre tract, he may be part of the same Popplewell family group associated with the Tucker ancestry lead.

However, the woman in the case is named **Narcissa Coffey**, not Narcissus Tucker. The case does not give her maiden name, age, spouse, birth date or parents. The fact that she acquired land from Junius Popplewell does not prove that she was his sister or related to him. Do not identify her as Narcissus Popplewell Tucker or attach the three Coffey children to Eli Tucker and Narcissus Popplewell without independent evidence.

There is also an unrelated compiled FamilySearch profile for **Otha Lee Coffey (1883–1942)**, son of Doctor Gan Coffey and Margaret Matilda Popplewell. That profile cannot be merged with the infant appellant Otha Lee Coffey in the 1913 case merely because the names match; the dates/ages need to be checked and the court-case child may be a different person.
