# Avery Ello Whittle — Narcissus Tucker same-name disambiguation
**Research date:** 28 November 2026  
**Scope:** Checked an additional published Kentucky biographical source against the compiled Popplewell/Tucker ancestry lead.

## A second Narcissus Tucker appears in a contemporary published biography
*History of Kentucky*, volume V (1922), pages 434–435, contains a biographical sketch of Russell County physician James Ballinger Tarter. It states that his father Wesley Monroe Tarter married **Narcissus Tucker, born in Russell County in 1858**, and lists their children, including Mollie, James, Joseph, Elmer, Jennie, Chrisman V. and Lola.

Source: William Elsey Connelley and E. M. Coulter, *History of Kentucky*, vol. V (Chicago/New York: American Historical Society, 1922), pp. 434–435, scanned PDF: https://www.therainwatercollection.com/reference/ref059.pdf (relevant text at PDF lines 104–118).

## Do not conflate her with Narcissus Popplewell Tucker
The Tucker/Whittle working hypothesis concerns **Narcissus “Arsis” Popplewell Tucker**, reported born 26 December 1849 and died 23 September 1908, wife of Eli (Ell) Tucker. That is the Narcissus named in Paula Kite's 1998 Russell County genealogy query and in PeopleLegacy's family grouping for Lillie Bell Tucker McBeath.

The 1922 biography's Narcissus Tucker was born in 1858 and married Wesley Monroe Tarter. She is nine years younger than the Popplewell woman and has a different documented husband and child group. The shared given name and county do not establish that they are the same woman. They could be different people, perhaps related, but no kinship is established.

Sources:
- 1922 *History of Kentucky*, vol. V, pp. 434–435: https://www.therainwatercollection.com/reference/ref059.pdf
- KYGenWeb, Russell County Queries 1998, Paula Kite's Tucker/Popplewell query: https://kygenweb.net/russell/queries/queries-1998.html
- PeopleLegacy, Lillie Bell Tucker McBeath: https://peoplelegacy.com/lillie_bell_tucker_mcbeath-3l5n2r

## Consequence for Avery's ancestry research
Keep the 1849 Narcissus Popplewell and the 1858 Narcissus Tucker as separate identities unless original records prove otherwise. Do not attach the Tarter children or Wesley Monroe Tarter as a spouse/parent in the Popplewell family. The 1922 biography provides a new Russell County Tucker/Tarter research cluster that may be collateral, but it does not establish a connection to Cordelia E. Tucker Whittle or Stanton Bell Whittle.

The immediate unresolved question remains whether Cordelia was a daughter of Eli Tucker and Narcissus Popplewell, and whether she had any connection to Stanton Bell Whittle. The 1900 census and original vital records are still required.
