# Avery Ello Whittle — 1900 census Soundex route for Stanton and Cordella
**Research date:** 27 November 2026  
**Scope:** Established a precise archival route to test whether Stanton Whittle was Cordella Whittle's child before her 1895 marriage to R. S. “Bob” Whittle.

## Why the 1900 census matters
Cordella's 22 March 1951 obituary transcription states:
- Cordella was born 14 May 1878.
- She married R. S. “Bob” Whittle on 24 December 1895.
- Six children were born to that marriage; five surviving children were named: Otha, Rese, Jim Elmer, Caleb and Grady.
- Stanton Whittle's derivative birth index gives 10 July 1893, approximately two years before Cordella's marriage.

If the dates are correct, Cordella was about 15 at Stanton's birth. He could be a child from before her marriage, but the 1951 obituary does not identify him among the six children of Bob and Cordella. This remains a hypothesis only.

## Exact Soundex route located
- **Surname Soundex:** WHITTLE = **W340** (H and W are ignored; T=3, L=4).
- **1900 Kentucky Soundex microfilm:** NARA **T1047**, roll segment **W-340 Dora through W-362 Joe F.**
- The W340 cards can identify the original 1900 census enumeration district, page and line. The household card for **Robert S. / R. S. / Bob Whittle** should list each household member, relationship, birth month/year and birthplace. If Stanton was enumerated separately, an individual card may also exist.
- Internet Archive / Wikimedia Commons has a digitized public-domain copy of the relevant Kentucky Soundex microfilm, accession **soundex1900ky187**. The PDF is 3,566 pages; its scan is image-based and was not searchable by name in this session.
- The original 1900 population schedule (microfilm T623-550) is the primary evidence. It records each person's relationship to the household head, month/year of birth, age, marital status, place of birth and parents' birthplaces; for married women it also asks years married and children born/living.

## What remains unresolved
No Soundex card or 1900 population-schedule image was successfully read in this pass. No parentage was changed. The exact 1900 census card/schedule must be examined to determine:
1. Whether Cordella and Bob were enumerated together.
2. Whether Stanton appears in their household and what relationship is written.
3. The number of years Cordella reported being married and the number of children born/living.
4. Whether other Whittle children in the same household can be connected to the 1951 obituary's six-child count.
5. Whether the census gives Cordella's birthplace and parents' birthplaces.

A FamilySearch account, Ancestry access, or a library with the original T1047/T623 microfilm may be needed to view the card and schedule. No record was ordered and no institution was contacted.
