Life Story

Polly Reed was born 2 June 1795 in North Carolina, the sixth child of Lovett Reed and his wife Sibby — her mother’s declaration of 31 March 1840 lists her so, and Polly herself said so under oath in January 1841, “formerly Polly Reed, she being the sixth child of Lovett Reed decd and his widow Sibby Reed.” Both statements were read on the original pension file in November 2026. Lovett died in November 1834 in Bledsoe County; the day is blank in the file.

She married David W. Brown — no marriage record survives, and the date is unproved: by about 1819 if the register’s Jackson is their child, otherwise in the 1820s. The 1850 census finds the household in Bledsoe County, David 51, a labourer, and Mary 51, with nine children. By 21 June 1860 she was a widow in her son Thomas’s household in Cumberland County, and she was dead before the 1870 census. No death record or grave has been found.

Her maiden name is Reed, read on the original file — Geni still displays “(Thomson)” in its header, contradicted by the January 1841 testimony, and an old framing calling her maiden name unknown is stale. Her children number eleven to thirteen by the honest count, with the full roster and its contradictions carried on this page. No portrait of Polly exists, and no grave has been located.

Research Notes and Open Questions

  • Grades on this page: DOCUMENTED means an original record was read; SUPPORTED means a claim attested by filed research — the census images, the signed-in tree checks — but not by an original record read here; UNPROVED means a claim carried from the research folder with no corroboration found.
  • Polly’s maiden name is Reed, read on the original (W5671 image 24, “formerly Polly Reed”). Geni still displays “(Thomson)” — contradicted. An old framing calling her maiden name unknown is stale.
  • Lovett Reed died in November 1834, Bledsoe County — the day is blank in the pension file; WikiTree’s “9 November 1834” carries a day the file does not give, and FamilySearch’s “9 November 1836” is wrong in both year and day. Lovett’s birth is 14 October 1754 by FamilySearch against 17 October 1754 by WikiTree — unresolved. The pension file lists seven children (George, Nancy, James, Peggy, Henry, Polly, Sibby); FamilySearch’s eight-child list omits Peggy and Henry, adds Lucy and John Henry, duplicates James, and has Mary 1795 in place of Polly — the pension list controls.
  • Polly born 2 June 1795, read on the originals (register image 22; Sibby’s declaration image 17).
  • David’s birth: 15 September 1800 by the register, against about 1799 by the census — about a year apart.
  • Eleven children sworn January 1841: the testimony was read on the original (W5671 image 24) — eleven children, ten living.
  • Mary 51 vs 57: the 1850 manuscript re-verified 51; the indexed 57 is a slip.
  • The count contradiction: the register names ten children, yet Barbery and the mother Lucy were both alive in January 1841 and both are missing from it — together they break the sworn count of eleven. Five readings are possible: the testimony count is wrong; the mother Lucy is not David × Polly’s daughter; Barbery is not their daughter; one of the ten register names is not a child of this couple; or the register as transcribed is incomplete. No record decides among them.
  • David’s parents: the pairing carried in the research folder, Thomas “Pompey” Brown × Lucy Gentry, has no record behind it — no record calls Thomas “Pompey” (the oath spells it “Poupey”), the dates do not fit, and FamilySearch’s “Pompey Brown 1750–1778” is a different, impossible claim. Five candidates were weighed and none reaches the bar. The Marion County Deed Book A pull closed 11 November 2026 with no David. The 1840 Fentress candidate is withdrawn: the row is “David B. Warren”, not David Brown.
  • Enumeration date settled: 25 October 1850, manuscript header (leaf n242). The “26 August” of the transcription header does not belong to this sheet, and the “23 October” of the manuscript citation was a misreading of the 25th.
  • “Poupey”: the image spelling on the original (1907 packet image 9); “Pompey” is kept as the running form.
  • “No land deed for David” is contradicted: the 1827 deeds (Book F pp. 184, 285) are DOCUMENTED.
  • Lore, labelled as lore, never evidence: the Crow Path Polly identification (an 1817 reservation Polly as a daughter of John Brown of the Cherokee Nation) — weight of evidence against; David Brown × Rachel Lowry (daughter of Cherokee leader Major George Lowry) — a lead only, chronology untested; the twins as grandchildren — an inference from an unnamed descendant compilation, not a record; “full-blooded” is Mary Catherine’s sworn word in a 1907 legal proceeding, not an adjudicated blood quantum or enrollment.
  • Record loss: Bledsoe County courthouse fire 1908 by the state library’s list, 9 December 1909 by the newspapers — the year differs; the loss of marriage and probate records is agreed. Cumberland County courthouse fire February 1905 — pre-1905 marriages lost. Fentress County courthouse fire 8 December 1904 — the Register’s Books A and B destroyed. Morgan County fires 1826, 1870, and 1904.
