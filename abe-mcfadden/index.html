Life Story

Abe “Mac” McFadden was born in London, Kentucky, in 1905. His 1964 obituary says he first farmed near Brookville, Indiana, and then drove a truck for Burger Brewing Company for fourteen years. It names his first wife as Maggie Marie Mooney McFadden, who died in 1943, and says he married Rose Bolser in 1948. The obituary names their children Laura Lee, Abe Jr., and Etta Marcum. Etta’s own 1970 obituary and Find a Grave memorial identify her as Etta Fay McFadden Marcum, connecting this couple to the Marcum branch. Abe died in Hamilton, Ohio, on 8 December 1964 and was buried at Darrtown Pioneer Cemetery.

Family links shown here are attributed to the named records; no other relative profiles were created for this Gen VI couple.

His mother died around 1927, when Homer was sixteen. His father later married Julia Bell Kelly, and that tied the family to the Kellys twice over: Julia's sister Sarah Hazel Kelly, a Georgia girl, became Homer's wife.

Homer and Sarah married on 6 August 1930 in Hamilton, Ohio. Six children followed through the 1930s: Eunice, Eugene, the twins Elmer and Earl, a daughter, and Homer Jr. In 1940 the family of eight lived on Ford Road in Morgan County, in the same house as five years before.

By 1950 they had moved north to Cincinnati, to 517 12th Street, and by 1953 to 519 E. 12th Street. In January 1952 the twins joined the Marines on their seventeenth birthday. Elmer served in California; Earl went to Korea and was wounded on 27 July 1953, the last day of the war; the family was told it was on Pork Chop Hill, but the Marines' last fight was at Boulder City and Hill 111. Homer lost Earl to a car crash in Kentucky in 1959.

Sarah died in Hamilton, Ohio, on 28 February 1987. Homer died in Palm Beach, Florida, on 8 March 1991, at seventy-nine, formerly of College Hill. He was buried beside Sarah at Spring Grove Cemetery in Cincinnati, under their shared stone. He left twenty-four grandchildren and thirty-eight great-grandchildren.

Research Notes and Open Questions

  • The 1964 newspaper clipping is a valuable contemporaneous source. It says Abe was born in London, Kentucky, on 17 April 1905; died 8 December 1964; had worked as a farmer near Brookville, Indiana, then as a truck driver for Burger Brewing for 14 years; retired in June 1964; was a Teamsters Union member; married Rose Bolser in 1948; and left one son, Abe Jr., two daughters Laura Lee Lawson and Etta Marcum, one sister Mrs. John Overby, 14 grandchildren and two great-grandchildren. It says first wife Maggie Mooney McFadden died in 1943.
  • The obituary identifies his parents as James and Delia Ann Gregory McFadden. Find a Grave’s linked parent entries use James Garfield “Bud” McFadden and Cordilia Ann “Dilly Ann” Gregory. Preserve “Delia” vs “Cordilia” as source forms until a civil record bridges them.
  • Find a Grave separately links three full siblings and five half-siblings. These derivative family links are shown as leads, not as independently established relationships; no sibling profiles were created.
  • The Find a Grave entry for spouse Etta Marcum states an impossible 1940 marriage to Harold Clayton Marcum, even though they were both born in 1927. Do not repeat that date; no reliable marriage date was found.
  • No individual portrait of Abe was located in the reviewed memorial pages. The hero image is the shared grave marker with second wife Rose; the 1964 obituary clipping is preserved in the document archive.
