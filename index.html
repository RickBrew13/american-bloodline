About this project

Why we keep the names

An American Bloodline began with a simple question that most families eventually ask: where did we come from? What started as a few names on a scrap of paper has grown into a documented line reaching back to the Mayflower and forward to the people reading this today.

The work is deliberately plain. No paywall, no subscription, no account to create. Names are grouped by generation and by family side, so a visitor can follow a single branch without wading through the whole tree — and a researcher can see exactly what we have and, just as usefully, what we still do not.

Everything here is shared freely with cousins, researchers, and anyone else who finds a name that matches their own. If you spot an error, we would rather hear it than keep it.

Before you go further

The youngest generations

Generations I–IV are the youngest part of the line — the kids and the parents’ generation, the newest chapters of the story and the ones still being written. They are kept as documents rather than index pages, and they are private: shared only with family who have been given permission to see them.

If you do not already have access, please ask before opening one.

The older generations

Generations V–XVIII are the older generations, running back through the centuries toward the Mayflower — and they are open to the public. Each one has its own index page, and you are welcome to read them: every branch, every surname, every name we have.

The individual profiles behind those names stay private, and are shared only with family.
