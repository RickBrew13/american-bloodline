# Martha (Sullivan) Lewis — parents and sibling review

**Reviewed:** 24 November 2026. **Generation VI target confirmed on the live index:** Arnold Lewis × Martha Sullivan.

## Direct obituary evidence

Martha J. Lewis, née Sullivan, was born 16 July 1928 in St. Louis, Missouri, and died 26 March 2014 at Liberty Village Nursing Home in Maryville, Illinois. Her obituary names her parents as Matthew Sullivan and Lena (Spencer) Sullivan, says she married Arnold Lewis on 16 May 1947, identifies her as retired from Collinsville Unit 10 School District after 20 years, reports Collinsville residence, and states burial at St. John Cemetery in Collinsville.

The obituary names her siblings as Mary Jane Schuab, Dorothy Sullivan, Matthew Edward Sullivan Jr., James S. Sullivan, Robert J. Sullivan, Dan J. Sullivan, Jo Ann Graham, and surviving sister Elizabeth Wright. The spelling “Schuab” is retained as published; no correction to Schaub is assumed.

It names five children: daughters Patricia (Fred) Dominick and Pamela (Greg) Embick; sons Edward A. (Marge) Lewis, Jeffery (Diana) Lewis, and Thomas Lewis. This matches the five-child group in Edward Arnold Lewis Sr.'s 2024 funeral-home obituary (Patty, Pam, Jeff, Tommy, and Edward), although the wife name on Edward's 2024 notice (Margie) conflicts with Rick's family account naming Gloria. That discrepancy remains untouched on Edward's profile.

## Original 1950 census household for her parents

The NARA original 1950 census image for Lesterville Township, Reynolds County (ED 90-5, sheet 4, enumerated 4 April 1950) directly lists Matthew E. Sullivan, 59, head; Lena O. Sullivan, 58, wife; Daniel G. Sullivan, 18, son; and Joann Sullivan, 16, daughter. The image has been inspected directly and is archived in Matthew E. Sullivan's Person Archive. This is consistent with Martha's obituary naming Matthew and Lena as her parents. The names Daniel G. and Joann may correspond to obituary siblings Dan J. Sullivan and Jo Ann Graham, but the initials and later married surname differ; the match remains a lead rather than a resolved identity.
