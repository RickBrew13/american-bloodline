Family Relationship Notes

Ignazia Fanara is named as Pasquale’s mother on his marriage act of 27 February 1886 (Palermo, Brancaccio, Act No. 15): Fanara Ignazia, resident in Palermo, wife of Angelo Carbonaro.

She is named again in the Vallelunga marriage papers of 26 November 1895, where she is described as deceased (“della fu Ignazia Fanara”), as is her husband Angelo. Both were already dead by that date.

Her son Pasquale was born 15 February 1855 in Palermo, worked as a miller, married Rosalia Mule on 27 February 1886 in Brancaccio, was widowed 22 October 1893, and remarried Giuseppa Noto on 19 December 1895 in Vallelunga Pratameno.

Life Story

Ignazia Fanara was the wife of Angelo Carbonaro, a miller of Palermo, and the mother of Pasquale Carbonaro. She is named as Pasquale’s mother on his marriage act of 27 February 1886, resident in Palermo, and again in the Vallelunga marriage papers of 26 November 1895, where she is described as deceased, as is her husband. Her son Pasquale was born 15 February 1855 in Palermo, worked as a miller, was widowed in 1893, and remarried in Vallelunga Pratameno in 1895. No record of Ignazia’s own birth, marriage, or death has been found.

Sources and Evidence

  1. Palermo, Marriage Records 1886, Act No. 15. Pasquale Carbonaro, 31, miller, born Palermo, son of Angelo Carbonaro and Fanara Ignazia, married Mulè Rosalia, 23, born Zisa, daughter of Vincenzo Mule and Pisciotto Anna, 27 February 1886, Casa comunale di Brancaccio, before Michele Patricolo. Both spouses signed. The act was read from the image and re-read letter by letter in a second pass.
  2. Vallelunga Pratameno, Marriage Supplements 1894–1911, letter No. 32, 26 November 1895. Diocese of Caltanissetta: Pasquale Carbonaro, son of the late Angelo and the late Ignazia Fanara, widower since 22 October 1893 of the late Rosalia Mule, born in Palermo in 1855, baptized in the parish of Albergheria, had lived in Vallelunga about nine months, asking to marry Giuseppa Noto, daughter of Francesco Noto and Maria Luzi. The letter states Rosalia’s death date twice. The letter was read from the image and re-read letter by letter in a second pass.
