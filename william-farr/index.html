Life Story

William Farr was born about 1790 in North Carolina and died between 1857 and 1860, probably in Cumberland County, Tennessee. He was a farmer and a flax grower — the 1850 agricultural schedule credits him with 20 pounds of flax and 3 pounds of flaxseed. He married Elizabeth about 1817, probably in North Carolina; no marriage record has been found. They moved to Wayne County, Kentucky about 1822, then to Clinton County, and in 1849 to Morgan County, Tennessee, on the Obed River. Ten children are carried; their daughter Rebecca carries the line, marrying John W. Brewster about 1848–49.

The land records are the firmest ground. In 1831 he bought two tracts on Beaver Creek in Wayne County — 50 acres and 100 acres — and in 1849 he bought 500 acres on the Obed River in Morgan County, adding 150 more acres in 1855. In 1855 and 1857 he made deeds of gift to his daughters Vianna Goss and Louisa Farr. The Obed country became Cumberland County in 1856. The original deed images have not yet been obtained; the land spine rests on a compiled deed reconstruction.

The censuses carry the household. In 1830 in Wayne County the slave columns are blank — no slaves that year, documented. In 1840 in Clinton County the household is three males — a boy under five, a young man of 15–20, and William, 40–50 — and six females; Jasper, then six, and Elizabeth, then about eighteen, are both absent from it, and one extra girl under five is unaccounted for. In 1850 in Morgan County, household 557, William is 60 and a farmer, Elizabeth 51, and the four children at home — Emeline, Jasper, Viannah and Lewis — were all born in Kentucky, not Tennessee. Louisa, then about eight, is absent from the 1850 household, and no trace of her there has been found. The 1830 household also carries a young man of 15–19 who predates the marriage and two extra girls — none of them identified.

His parents are carried here as Joseph Farr Jr. × Nancy Choate, as they stand on the generation page, at UNPROVED — but the placement is contradicted as stated. Joseph Jr. was born about 1780, the 1860 Caldwell census giving his age as 80, and his wife was Mary, not Nancy; his own 1860 will names no William or Elijah. The leading alternative is Joseph Fair Sr. × Nancy, at SUPPORTED: the 1818 Burke County will names his two sons William and Elijah Fair, and the 1800 Burke census fits — but no document yet links that William directly to Wayne County. The Burke deeds of 1818–1825, where William would have sold his inherited half-share, are the lock that has not yet been pulled. “Choate” itself rests only on one contributor’s comment on the 1818 will transcript; the will says only “Nancy”. No portrait of William is known — not in the research folder, and not on any tree site checked.

Research Notes and Open Questions

  • Grades on this page: DOCUMENTED means an original record was read; SUPPORTED means a claim attested by filed research — the census images, the will transcripts, the signed-in tree checks — but not by an original record read here; UNPROVED means a claim carried from the research folder with no corroboration found.
  • Parentage: William’s parents are carried as Joseph Farr Jr. × Nancy Choate at UNPROVED, as they stand on the generation page. The placement is contradicted as stated. Joseph Jr. was born about 1780 — the 1860 Caldwell census, indexed “Joseph Pharr”, gives his age as 80, and his wife was Mary Pharr, 75 — and his own 1860 will, written 25 September 1860, names children Clarissa, Joseph III, Ambrose, Sherewood, David, Mary, Caroline and Myra, with no William and no Elijah. A man born about 1780 cannot father a man born about 1790, and his wife was Mary, not Nancy. The leading alternative is Joseph Fair Sr. × Nancy, at SUPPORTED: the 1818 Burke will names his two sons William and Elijah Fair; the 1800 Burke census fits, with two males 10–15 in Joseph’s household; and William named his apparent eldest son Elijah. No document yet links that William directly to Wayne County, Kentucky — the direct-link document is still missing, a Burke deed of 1818–1825 in which William sells his inherited half-share, or the estate files for Joseph (1818) and Nancy (about 1820). “Choate” rests only on Roy Fair’s 2013 comment appended to the 1818 will transcript; the will itself says only “Nancy”. Geni lists the same Joseph Farr, Jr. as both William’s father and his brother — impossible as stated, the same generation transposition. The alternative Richard Farr Jr. × Lucy of Anson County is UNPROVED and weakened: the 1790 Anson household shows no males under 16, and the Anson estate-files index holds no Farr or Fair estate file.
  • Birth: about 1790, North Carolina. The exact “9 March 1790, Burke, North Carolina” and the death year “1858” carried on some tree profiles are unsourced; the same dates appear in Ancestry member-tree snippets, suggesting a member-tree origin. Burke County as his birthplace is unsourced.

FamilySearch lists Adolphus D. Fair (1820–1881) in place of Elizabeth Farr (about 1822) — one roster slot, two names, no evidence run on Adolphus. No twins were found.
  • Census anomalies: the 1830 household carries a male 15–19, born 1811–15, who predates the marriage of about 1817, and two extra females — none of them identified. The 1850 household omits Louisa, then about eight. The 1840 household omits Jasper, then six, and Elizabeth, then about eighteen, and carries one extra girl under five; the Elijah Farr household at 609 ticks its adult male 30–40, eight or more years too old for an Elijah born 1818. The 1840 images have not been recounted tick by tick.
  • Birthplaces: the four children at home in 1850 — Emeline, Jasper, Viannah and Lewis — were born in Kentucky, not Tennessee, on the 1850 census image; the Tennessee birthplaces in the research folder are wrong.
  • Record loss: Morgan County, Tennessee, courthouse fires are 1826, 1870 and 1904 — there was no 1863 fire; the Civil War destruction of Morgan marriage records is attested but undated. Fentress marriages survive only from 1905, so the 1838 and 1843 Fentress marriages of Elijah and William Jr. cannot be checked in a register. Clinton County, Kentucky, lost records in 1864 and 1980; Wayne County in 1898.
  • “Cherokee Bill” is not this family: the famous outlaw is Crawford Goldsby, born 1876 and hanged 1896, a century after William Farr; the compiled account’s page 46 figure is William Patton, a separate family lore label. No Cherokee framing appears on these pages. The “Nancy Choate” Cherokee claim is quarantined — it exists only in one contributor’s comments, a WikiTree profile and a FamilySearch memory, with no record behind it.
