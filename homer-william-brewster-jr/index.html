Life Story

Homer William Brewster Jr. was born March 18, 1939, in Morgan County, Tennessee, on the Cumberland Plateau, where coal lay under the ridges and the creeks ran cold. He was the son of Homer William Brewster Sr. and Sarah Hazel Kelly, and one of six children — Eugene, the twins Elmer and Earl, born January 17, 1935, Eunice, and Aileen. FAMILY ACCOUNT Earl became a Marine, was wounded in Korea, and died in 1959; Elmer died in 1993. SUPPORTED The family called Homer "Bruce." FAMILY ACCOUNT No military record has been found for him, and no occupation or published mention either. He was of Vietnam-draft age, and the records lane for him stays open.

In the postwar years Homer rode the Hillbilly Highway north to Cincinnati and southwest Ohio, part of the great migration out of the coalfields, carrying a mountain name into a factory city. And there he met Judith Mae Palmer. FAMILY ACCOUNT

They were seventeen when they ran off to Tennessee together. Vicki, their eldest daughter, tells the story as her mother told it to her. Judy had taken a ride with Homer down to Tennessee, where her grandparents were visiting people, and Homer had lied and told her he was eighteen. When her family found out, they had a fit. Judy told Homer they had to get married because she couldn't go back, so they found an old man and paid him ten dollars to stand up and say he was her father, because they were underage and needed somebody to give consent, and they got married. When they came back, her grandpa and the woman who raised her made them marry again, properly this time. They hadn't planned any of it. It just happened because of circumstances. FAMILY ACCOUNT Judy always said their wedding date was April 18, 1956, and the Fentress County, Tennessee, marriage record confirms it, with Homer giving his age as eighteen. DOCUMENTED No record of the second wedding has been located, and no wedding pictures exist.

They settled in the Hamilton and Preble County areas, and there they raised four children. Victoria, called Vicki, came first, born in 1957, the eldest. She married at least twice, and it is Vicki who told the elopement story in full. Michael Eugene followed, born in 1958. Garry Anthony was born December 24, 1959, and he married Susan Ruth Shamblen. Ronald, called Ron, was born in 1962, and his son Ryan lived with Judy for a time. FAMILY ACCOUNT

Michael's name would one day travel further than any of theirs. In the summer of 2012 he robbed eleven banks in ten states, was caught in Roland, Oklahoma, pleaded guilty, and received 135 months in federal prison. The FBI called him the Bucket List Bandit. His release status is not confirmed. SUPPORTED

That first wedding was not the last. The family called Homer "Bruce," and several of Judy's marriages were to him — they married and divorced several times. Susan, who married into the family and watched it up close, remembers it plainly: the marrying and divorcing, over and over, and that he was very abusive. That is the family's telling, carried here as they tell it. FAMILY ACCOUNT The Ohio divorce index records one divorce between them, granted to Judith in Hamilton County on 2 May 1968 after twelve years of marriage, with four minor children. SUPPORTED Homer then married a woman indexed as Flora L., about 1970; they divorced in March 1977, married again that September, and divorced for the last time in November 1978. SUPPORTED After Homer left for good, Judy held the family together from a house in Hartwell, with a pool in the backyard where the grandchildren and cousins swam. FAMILY ACCOUNT

Records now settle Homer's death. He died on September 27, 2000, in Preble County, Ohio, at sixty-one, while living in Cincinnati; he had lived in Dayton before that. The Ohio death index and his death notice in the Dayton Daily News agree on the date, and the index gives the county. SUPPORTED An older family telling puts his death in Florida, but no record found supports it UNPROVED; another family record says 1991, which is the year of his father's death in the Social Security Death Index. He was cremated SUPPORTED, and a memorial service was held at 4 p.m. on Saturday, September 30, 2000, at Newcomer-Barclay Funeral Home in Dayton. DOCUMENTED Susan understands lupus is what killed him — she flags she could be wrong, and says to ask Garry; his death certificate would show the cause. FAMILY ACCOUNT His ashes are held by Vicki's son Jeremy, who says Grandpa lives in his basement. FAMILY ACCOUNT

Research Notes and Open Questions

  • Death: the 1991 date matches Homer Sr. in the SSDI, not Homer Jr. The Dayton Daily News death notice (29 Sep 2000) and the Ohio death index now agree on 27 September 2000. The notice gives no middle name, but it matches the index on date, Cincinnati residence and cremation. Next step: his Ohio death certificate, no. 076423 (Ohio Department of Health or Preble County), which would give the exact place, cause, informant and stated age. It has not been ordered, as it carries a fee. Newcomer-Barclay's records may also survive.
  • Age at death: the death notice gives 67 and the Ohio death index 64, but his 18 March 1939 birth date makes him 61. Both stated ages are UNPROVED and not yet explained; the death certificate would show the age the informant gave.
  • 1956 marriage record found (Fentress County, Tennessee). The Ohio divorce index (1962–2006) shows only one divorce between Homer and Judith, on 2 May 1968. In that entry Homer is counted as married twice by 1968 and Judith once; his second marriage may be the second wedding the family says her grandfather insisted on, or another marriage not yet found. The family telling that they married and divorced several times is not yet borne out by Ohio records UNPROVED; any later remarriage may have been outside Ohio. Next steps: Hamilton County Domestic Relations Court case file for the 1968 divorce, and Hamilton County Probate Court marriage records.
  • Flora L.: his marriage to her about 1970 is not in the Ohio marriage index; it may have taken place outside Ohio. Her maiden name and the date and place of that first marriage are not yet known.
  • Residence: the 1950 census places him in Cincinnati by April 1950, at age 11, so the move north came in his childhood, not his teens as the family recalled. His sister Eunice was born in Cincinnati in 1930, so the family had ties there before he was born.
  • Florida: a public-records listing puts a Homer W. Brewster born 18 March 1939 at Osprey, Sarasota County, Florida, in 1998. If it is him, it may explain the family telling that he died in Florida. His father died in Palm Beach County, Florida, in 1991.
  • No military record found; he was of draft age in the Vietnam era.
  • Marriage-age conflict: the previously examined 1956 Tennessee application appears to record age 18 for both Homer and Judy; the working 1939 birth dates make both 17 on 18 April 1956. Preserve the disagreement; do not resolve it by assumption. The image close-up did not establish the officiant return or signatures.
  • Tree/media sweep, 19 November 2026: live WeRelate person/family pages repeat key dates and links, but the marriage is flagged citation-needed and no children are listed on that family page. Ancestry's public profile repeats identity details and advertises record/media attachments; its record detail and image endpoints require access and the direct photo requests returned 403, so no new image was verified or copied. Geni and FamilySearch direct pages blocked automated access. No new independent evidence for the death, occupation, service, or marriages found in the checked public web results.
