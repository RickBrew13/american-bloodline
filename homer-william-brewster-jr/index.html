Life Story

Homer William Brewster Jr. was born March 18, 1939, in Morgan County, Tennessee, on the Cumberland Plateau, where coal lay under the ridges and the creeks ran cold. He was the son of Homer William Brewster Sr. and Sarah Hazel Kelly, and one of six children — Eugene, the twins Elmer and Earl, born January 17, 1935, Eunice, and Aileen. Earl became a Marine, was wounded in Korea, and died in 1959; Elmer died in 1993. The family called Homer "Bruce." No military record has been found for him, and no occupation or published mention either. He was of Vietnam-draft age, and the records lane for him stays open.

In the postwar years Homer rode the Hillbilly Highway north to Cincinnati and southwest Ohio, part of the great migration out of the coalfields, carrying a mountain name into a factory city. And there he met Judith Mae Palmer.

They were seventeen when they ran off to Tennessee together. Vicki, their eldest daughter, tells the story as her mother told it to her. Judy had taken a ride with Homer down to Tennessee, where her grandparents were visiting people, and Homer had lied and told her he was eighteen. When her family found out, they had a fit. Judy told Homer they had to get married because she couldn't go back, so they found an old man and paid him ten dollars to stand up and say he was her father, because they were underage and needed somebody to give consent, and they got married. When they came back, her grandpa and the woman who raised her made them marry again, properly this time. They hadn't planned any of it. It just happened because of circumstances. Judy always said their wedding date was April 18, 1956, and the Fentress County, Tennessee, marriage record confirms it, with Homer giving his age as eighteen. No record of the second wedding has been located, and no wedding pictures exist.

Homer and Judy settled in the Hamilton and Preble County areas of southwest Ohio and had four children together: Victoria “Vicki” (born 1957), Michael Eugene (born 1958), Garry Anthony (born 24 December 1959), and Ronald “Ron” (born 1962). Garry later married Susan Ruth Shamblen. Their daughter Vicki preserved the family account of the Tennessee elopement.

Michael's name would one day travel further than any of theirs. In the summer of 2012 he robbed eleven banks in ten states, was caught in Roland, Oklahoma, pleaded guilty, and received 135 months in federal prison. The FBI called him the Bucket List Bandit. His release status is not confirmed.

The marriage to Judy was followed by other marriages and divorces. Family members recall that Homer and Judy married and divorced more than once; the exact sequence beyond the documented record remains unresolved. Susan, who married into the family, recalls Homer as abusive. This is a family account and is presented as such, not as an independently established legal finding. The Ohio divorce index documents a divorce between Homer and Judith in Hamilton County on 2 May 1968, after twelve years of marriage, with four minor children. Homer later married a woman indexed as Flora L.; the available Ohio indexes record a divorce in March 1977, a marriage again in September 1977, and another divorce in November 1978.

The Ohio death index records Homer William Brewster’s death on 27 September 2000 in Preble County, Ohio, with Cincinnati listed as his residence and cremation as his disposition. His recorded birth date, 18 March 1939, makes him sixty-one. The index entry does not provide a certificate image, cause of death, or informant. A separate brief notice in the Dayton Daily News, dated 29 September 2000, names a Homer Brewster, age sixty-seven, formerly of Dayton and of Cincinnati, and lists a memorial service for Saturday at Newcomer-Barclay. The notice may refer to him, but it does not give a middle name or relatives, and its age conflicts with the birth date; its identity therefore remains unconfirmed. An older family telling puts his death in Florida, but no record found supports it; another family record says 1991, which is the year of his father’s death in the Social Security Death Index. Susan understands lupus is what killed Homer, but she flags that she could be wrong and says to ask Garry; the death certificate is needed to establish cause. A family account says his ashes are held by Vicki’s son Jeremy, who jokes that Grandpa lives in his basement.

Research Notes and Open Questions

  • Death: the 1991 date matches Homer Sr. in the SSDI, not Homer Jr. The Ohio death index records Homer Jr.'s death on 27 September 2000 in Preble County, with Cincinnati residence and cremation as disposition. The Dayton Daily News notice is only a possible same-name match: it gives age 67, no middle name or relatives, and cannot yet be securely tied to Homer Jr. Next step: request certificate 076423 from Preble County Public Health, 615 Hillcrest Drive, Eaton, OH 45320, phone 937-472-0087 (birth/death certificates extension 4). The official fee is $25; call to confirm statewide availability before ordering. See the official Vital Statistics page and the Homer-specific record follow-up note. The certificate has not been ordered.
  • Age at death: the Dayton Daily News notice gives 67 and the Ohio death index gives 64, but his recorded 18 March 1939 birth date makes him 61 on 27 September 2000. Both indexed/printed ages conflict with the calculated age; the death certificate should show the age supplied by the informant.
  • 1956 marriage record found (Fentress County, Tennessee). The Ohio divorce index (1962–2006) shows one divorce between Homer and Judith, on 2 May 1968. That entry counts Homer as married twice and Judith once. The standard divorce statistical form defines the marriage-count field to include all marriages, including the present marriage being dissolved; if the index count was transcribed correctly, Homer had a prior marriage before Judith, whose marriage to him was her first. This is a new, unresolved spouse lead—not proof of who the earlier wife was. It should not be explained away as the family-recalled second wedding ceremony. The 1977 Flora divorce/marriage indexes and the 1978 divorce count also need to be reconciled against original abstracts. The family telling that Homer and Judith married/divorced more than once is not yet borne out by the located Ohio indexes; any later remarriage may have been outside Ohio. Next steps: obtain the 1968 abstract and complete petition-through-decree file from the Hamilton County Clerk, then search county marriage records for Homer's earlier marriage. The Clerk's Document Center is at 1000 Main Street, Room 315, Cincinnati; phone (513) 946-5693. See the divorce-file retrieval note and marriage-count research note. The Probate Court online marriage-license index begins in 1984, so it cannot settle the earlier marriages.
  • Flora L.: his marriage to her about 1970 is not in the Ohio marriage index; it may have taken place outside Ohio. Her maiden name and the date and place of that first marriage are not yet known.
  • Edna Sharon Bailey: an online-tree relationship reports a marriage on 18 June 1966 in Franklin County, Indiana, but no certificate or independent index entry has been verified. The Indiana State Library’s official Indiana Marriages 1958–2022 index is the next targeted check; it supports searching both applicants, date, county, and certificate number, and warns that transcription errors may exist. Because the reported 1966 marriage overlaps the indexed Homer–Judith marriage that ended in May 1968, Edna remains an unverified lead, not a confirmed spouse. Do not remove her from the research trail or present the marriage as established.
  • Hamilton County property: the 1975 deed index lists Homer W. Brewster and Flora L. as grantees; the 1976 grantor-to-grantee index lists Homer as grantor and Flora as grantee on 8 January 1976 (Book 4040, p.738). The original deeds have not been examined, so the property address and reason for transfer remain unknown. A separate 26 November 1969 index entry lists “Brewster, Homer et al” to James A. Katsanis, Book 3708, p.344; it is not attributed to Homer Jr. until the original deed identifies him. See the deed retrieval note.
  • Residence: the 1950 census places him in Cincinnati by April 1950, at age 11, so the move north came in his childhood, not his teens as the family recalled. His sister Eunice was born in Cincinnati in 1930, so the family had ties there before he was born.
  • Florida: a public-records listing puts a Homer W. Brewster born 18 March 1939 at Osprey, Sarasota County, Florida, in 1998. If it is him, it may explain the family telling that he died in Florida. His father died in Palm Beach County, Florida, in 1991.
  • No military record found; he was of draft age in the Vietnam era.
  • Occupation and residence: no verified adult occupation or post-1950 address yet. Cincinnati and suburban city directories from 1942 onward may identify addresses, employers, occupations, and spouses; see the Hamilton County Genealogical Society directory guide and the directory research plan. No directory entry has yet been verified for Homer Jr.
  • Marriage-age conflict: the previously examined 1956 Tennessee application appears to record age 18 for both Homer and Judy; the working 1939 birth dates make both 17 on 18 April 1956. Preserve the disagreement; do not resolve it by assumption. The image close-up did not establish the officiant return or signatures.
