Life Story

Homer William Brewster Jr. was born March 18, 1939, in Morgan County, Tennessee, on the Cumberland Plateau, where coal lay under the ridges and the creeks ran cold. He was the son of Homer William Brewster Sr. and Sarah Hazel Kelly, and one of six children — Eugene, Elmer, Earl, Eunice, and Aileene. The family history book describes Elmer and Earl as twin brothers; both are listed with a birth date of January 17, 1935, and the 1940 census places them together at age five. The book's twin statement is family-history evidence, but no original birth record has yet been found to independently confirm it. The family called Homer "Bruce." No adult occupation has been established, and no military service record has been verified. Homer turned eighteen on 18 March 1957; a Selective Service classification history may survive at the National Archives, but it has not been requested.

The 1940 census index places the family in Morgan County, Tennessee. By 15 April 1950, when Homer was eleven, the original National Archives census sheet places him in his parents' Cincinnati, Hamilton County, Ohio household. The family history remembers the move north as part of the Hillbilly Highway migration out of the coalfields, but the census fixes the move to his childhood, not his young adulthood. Homer later met Judith Mae Palmer in Ohio.

They were seventeen when they ran off to Tennessee together. Vicki, their eldest daughter, tells the story as her mother told it to her. Judy had taken a ride with Homer down to Tennessee, where her grandparents were visiting people, and Homer had lied and told her he was eighteen. When her family found out, they had a fit. Judy told Homer they had to get married because she couldn't go back, so they found an old man and paid him ten dollars to stand up and say he was her father, because they were underage and needed somebody to give consent, and they got married. When they came back, her grandpa and the woman who raised her made them marry again, properly this time. They hadn't planned any of it. It just happened because of circumstances. Judy always said their wedding date was April 18, 1956, and the Fentress County, Tennessee, marriage record confirms it, with Homer giving his age as eighteen. No record of the second wedding has been located, and no wedding pictures exist.

Homer and Judy settled in southwest Ohio and had four children together: Victoria “Vicki” (born 1957), Michael Eugene (born 1958), Garry Anthony (born 24 December 1959), and Ronald “Ron” (born 1962). Each child's own profile carries that person's later life and family details.

The marriage to Judy was followed by other marriages and divorces. Family members recall that Homer and Judy married and divorced more than once; the exact sequence beyond the located records remains unresolved. The Ohio divorce index records a Hamilton County divorce between Homer and Judith on 2 May 1968, after twelve years of marriage, with four minor children. The abstract's “Number of this marriage” field is indexed as 2 for Homer and 1 for Judith; if that transcription is correct, the marriage to Judith was Homer's second and her first, indicating an earlier marriage for Homer whose spouse and date have not been found. This is separate from Vicki's account of a second wedding ceremony after their Tennessee elopement. The September 1977 marriage index records two previous marriages for both Homer and Flora, and the November 1978 divorce numbers their marriage as Homer's third. Together with the 1968 record, this supports a working sequence of an unidentified first marriage before Judy, Judy as his second marriage, and Flora as his third; no first wife has been identified. A separate March 1977 divorce index entry for a Homer W. Brewster and Flora L. reports a seven-year marriage and marriage number 2. That count does not fit the later sequence if it is the same couple, so the March entry may be a same-name record or an index error and is not yet attached to them. Susan, who married into the family, recalls Homer as abusive. This is a family account, presented as such and not as an independently established legal finding.

The Ohio death index records Homer William Brewster’s death on 27 September 2000 in Preble County, Ohio, with Cincinnati listed as his residence and cremation as his disposition. His recorded birth date, 18 March 1939, makes him sixty-one. The index entry does not provide a certificate image, cause of death, or informant. A separate brief notice in the Dayton Daily News, dated 29 September 2000, names a Homer Brewster, age sixty-seven, formerly of Dayton and of Cincinnati, and lists a memorial service for Saturday at Newcomer-Barclay. The notice may refer to him, but it does not give a middle name or relatives, and its age conflicts with the birth date; its identity therefore remains unconfirmed. An older family telling puts his death in Florida, but no record found supports it; another family record says 1991, which is the year of his father’s death in the Social Security Death Index. Susan understands lupus is what killed Homer, but she flags that she could be wrong and says to ask Garry; the death certificate is needed to establish cause. A family account says his ashes are held by Vicki’s son Jeremy, who jokes that Grandpa lives in his basement.

Research Notes and Open Questions

  • Death: the 1991 date matches Homer Sr. in the SSDI, not Homer Jr. The Ohio death index records Homer Jr.'s death on 27 September 2000 in Preble County, with Cincinnati residence and cremation as disposition. The Dayton Daily News notice is only a possible same-name match: it gives age 67, no middle name or relatives, and cannot yet be securely tied to Homer Jr. Next step: request certificate 076423 from Preble County Public Health, 615 Hillcrest Drive, Eaton, OH 45320, phone 937-472-0087 (birth/death certificates extension 4). The official fee is $25; call to confirm statewide availability before ordering. See the official Vital Statistics page and the Homer-specific record follow-up note. The certificate has not been ordered.
  • Age at death: the Dayton Daily News notice gives 67 and the Ohio death index gives 64, but his recorded 18 March 1939 birth date makes him 61 on 27 September 2000. Both indexed/printed ages conflict with the calculated age; the death certificate should show the age supplied by the informant.
  • 1956 marriage record found (Fentress County, Tennessee). The Ohio divorce index (1962–2006) shows one divorce between Homer and Judith, on 2 May 1968. That entry counts Homer as married twice and Judith once. Ohio's certified divorce abstract labels this field “Number of this marriage.” If the 1968 index was transcribed correctly, Homer’s value of 2 means his marriage to Judith was his second, while Judith’s value of 1 means her marriage to him was her first. This indicates at least one earlier marriage for Homer before Judith. This is a new, unresolved spouse lead—not proof of who the earlier wife was. It should not be explained away as the family-recalled second wedding ceremony. The 1977 Flora divorce/marriage indexes and the 1978 divorce count conflict in their marriage-order values: the March 1977 divorce lists Homer and Flora as number 2, the September 1977 marriage lists two past marriages for each, and the November 1978 divorce lists number 3. The original abstracts are needed to determine whether the March entry is the same couple or a same-name record. The family telling that Homer and Judith married/divorced more than once is not yet borne out by the located Ohio indexes; any later remarriage may have been outside Ohio. Next steps: obtain the 1968 abstract and complete petition-through-decree file from the Hamilton County Clerk, then search county marriage records for Homer's earlier marriage. The Clerk's Document Center is at 1000 Main Street, Room 315, Cincinnati; phone (513) 946-5693. See the divorce-file retrieval note and marriage-count research note. The Probate Court online marriage-license index begins in 1984, so it cannot settle the earlier marriages.
  • Flora L.: the Ohio index confirms Homer W. Brewster Jr. married Flora on 12 Sep 1977; the 27 Nov 1978 divorce numbers that marriage as his third. A separate 18 Mar 1977 divorce entry for a Homer W. Brewster and Flora L. (seven-year duration; marriage number 2) is not yet linked to this couple because its marriage-order count conflicts with the later record. Do not infer a first marriage to Flora about 1970 unless the March 1977 entry is proven to be the same couple. Her maiden name and the date/place of any earlier marriage remain unknown.
  • Edna Sharon Bailey: an online-tree relationship reports a marriage on 18 June 1966 in Franklin County, Indiana, but no certificate or independent index entry has been verified. The Indiana State Library’s official Indiana Marriages 1958–2022 index is the next targeted check; it supports searching both applicants, date, county, and certificate number, and warns that transcription errors may exist. Because the reported 1966 marriage overlaps the indexed Homer–Judith marriage that ended in May 1968, Edna remains an unverified lead, not a confirmed spouse. Do not remove her from the research trail or present the marriage as established.
  • Hamilton County property: the 1975 deed index lists Homer W. Brewster and Flora L. as grantees; the 1976 grantor-to-grantee index lists Homer as grantor and Flora as grantee on 8 January 1976 (Book 4040, p.738). The original deeds have not been examined, so the property address and reason for transfer remain unknown. A separate 26 November 1969 index entry lists “Brewster, Homer et al” to James A. Katsanis, Book 3708, p.344; it is not attributed to Homer Jr. until the original deed identifies him. See the deed retrieval note.
  • Residence: the 1950 census places him in Cincinnati by April 1950, at age 11, so the move north came in his childhood, not his teens as the family recalled. His sister Eunice was born in Cincinnati in 1930, so the family had ties there before he was born.
  • Florida: a public-records listing puts a Homer W. Brewster born 18 March 1939 at Osprey, Sarasota County, Florida, in 1998. If it is him, it may explain the family telling that he died in Florida. His father died in Palm Beach County, Florida, in 1991.
  • Military: no service record has been verified. Homer turned 18 on 18 March 1957 and was within the Selective Service registration period. NARA St. Louis holds his potential draft card and classification history; the latter may show induction, branch, or separation. A $27 classification-history request includes the card. No request has been submitted. See the NARA instructions and the research note.
  • Occupation and residence: no verified adult occupation or post-1950 address yet. Cincinnati and suburban city directories from 1942 onward may identify addresses, employers, occupations, and spouses; see the Hamilton County Genealogical Society directory guide and the directory research plan. No directory entry has yet been verified for Homer Jr.
  • Marriage-age conflict: the previously examined 1956 Tennessee application appears to record age 18 for both Homer and Judy; the working 1939 birth dates make both 17 on 18 April 1956. Preserve the disagreement; do not resolve it by assumption. The image close-up did not establish the officiant return or signatures.
