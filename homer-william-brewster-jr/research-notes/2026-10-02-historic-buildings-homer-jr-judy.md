## FINDING 1 — Holy Cross Church & Monastery, Mt. Adams, Cincinnati (1055 St. Paul Pl.)
**Grade: building history VERIFIED; wedding there SUPPORTED (family-book level, parish records not checked).**
- The book states William P. Palmer × Mary Jane Williams married **August 6, 1938, at Holy Cross church in Cincinnati**. No marriage record independently located; presented in the book unmarked (as fact), so graded SUPPORTED, not verified.
- The parish is the Mt. Adams Holy Cross: organized 1873 by English-speaking members splitting from Immaculata; first wooden church 1873; **permanent structure 1895** (the building in the filed photo); Passionist monastery; served mostly Irish immigrants; grade school added 1891; merged with Immaculata 1970 → Holy Cross-Immaculata; church deconsecrated 1974; monastery closed 1977 and sold; now Towne Properties corporate HQ + event center (church renovated 2014). **NRHP #78002078, listed September 13, 1978**; Cincinnati Local Historic Landmark. Parish records now at Immaculata, 30 Guido St (Immaculata Church itself NRHP-listed Dec 29, 1978).
- Sources: Wikipedia "Holy Cross Monastery and Church" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Cross_Monastery_and_Church); RootsWeb Cincinnati Catholic parish profiles (https://sites.rootsweb.com/~ohhamilt/catholic/parish1.html); NRHP listing page (https://nationalregisterofhistoricplaces.com/OH/Hamilton/vacant.html).
- **Image filed (EXACT BUILDING):** `pictures/holy-cross-monastery-chapel-mt-adams.jpg` + `.caption.txt` — the 1895 church, photo by Greg5030, CC BY-SA 3.0 via Wikimedia Commons. This is the church that stood at the wedding parish.

## FINDING 3 — Hartwell neighborhood, Cincinnati (Garry's childhood neighborhood)
**Grade: neighborhood history VERIFIED; Garry raised there SUPPORTED (family-book level).**
- The book states Garry was raised in Hartwell, "the historic, working-class neighborhood at the northern tip of Cincinnati — a railroad town named for John Wesley Hartwell, vice president of the Cincinnati, Hamilton & Dayton Railroad, who once offered a free year of rail passes to anyone who bought land there."
- VERIFIED: Hartwell is Cincinnati's northernmost neighborhood; grew around the CH&D and related rail lines (OSU railroad-and-city study); retains Victorian-era homes and Sears kit homes; Vine Street commercial corridor; **annexed by Cincinnati in 1912** per the City's Hartwell neighborhood plan — the book says 1911 (minor discrepancy, preserved); I-75 ("Millcreek Expressway") laid down 1959 just east of Hartwell, cutting it off from Arlington Heights/Edgemont Terrace. Trinity Church, Hartwell, erected as an Episcopal parish in **1888** (Nelson's History of Cincinnati, via Internet Archive).
- Sources: City of Cincinnati Hartwell NBD Plan 2012 (https://vendorselfservice.cincinnati-oh.gov/sites/planning/assets/Neighborhood%20Plans/Hartwell/2012%20Hartwell%20NBD%20Plan.pdf); ACHP Cincinnati page (https://www.achp.gov/preserve-america/community/cincinnati-ohio).
