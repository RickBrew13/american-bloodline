Life Story

Telitha Jane Casto was born 31 July 1826, by the shared stone, and married Nicholas L. Casto on 7 August 1845 in Jackson County, when she was nineteen and he was nineteen. The marriage register writes her “Letha Casto” and names the minister, Elij. J. Rollins, but no parents for either spouse. Her maiden name was Casto: she married her cousin. They settled on Tug Fork near Staats Mills, where the censuses find them from 1850 through 1900. Twelve children are carried — the 1900 census counts twelve born to Telitha, eight living — and their daughter Emily Jane carries the line, marrying William F. Staats in 1866.

Telitha was a member of the Sinaiville Methodist Protestant Church, organised 1840, the first Methodist Protestant church in the county; her funeral was held from that church. The censuses trace the family’s growth and loss across fifty years: Louisa and Emily Jane married out in the 1860s, Martha A. died aged five in 1857, the twins Alice and Sarah were born in 1860, and by 1880 the household held Nicholas, Telitha, and the younger children. In 1900, married fifty-two years, she answered the census-taker’s question with twelve children born and eight living.

Her parents are carried as William “Devil Bill” Casto Jr. × Martha “Patsy” Parsons, the position in the research folder, at SUPPORTED — a strong reconstruction, not proved. The 1849 Parsons-heirs deed names “William Casto and Martha his wife” among Charles Parsons’s heirs, and Charles Asbury Parsons Sr. is DAR Patriot A088124. The “Devil Bill” nickname has no attributable origin — no reason for it was ever found in any record — and the larger-than-life telling is deflated by the records: her father lived about ninety-four years, not past one hundred, and had eleven probable children, not seventeen.

Telitha died on 17 July 1913 at her son Everett’s home near Staats Mills, aged eighty-six, and is buried with Nicholas under their shared stone in Old Siniaville Cemetery. The stone’s dates are legible and read from the photograph itself. No portrait of Telitha exists anywhere — not in the folder, and not on any tree site checked — so the shared headstone is the hero.
