Life Story

Elmer Ellis Brewster and his twin brother Earl were born on January 17, 1935, in the Tennessee mountains, at Brewstertown, sons of Homer William Brewster Sr. and Sarah Hazel Kelly. The family was still in Morgan County, Tennessee, in 1940, and by 1950 had moved north to Cincinnati, Ohio, where Elmer grew up on 12th Street. A family note says that on February 17, 1951, at sixteen, he eloped with Ruth, who was fourteen; a separate family note names Mary Jane as the 1951 bride. The 1993 obituary names Mary Jane as his wife at death, but does not resolve the conflicting account or prove when any marriage occurred.

On their seventeenth birthday in January 1952, Elmer and Earl volunteered for the Marine Corps together; Elmer served in California and was not sent overseas. He went on to spend more than 35 years as a consultant to printing firms, setting up new businesses, and lived for a time in Missouri before moving to West Palm Beach, Florida, about 1989. In his last three years he was supervisor of the Lantana Toll Plaza.

He died on August 21, 1993, aged 58, survived by his wife Mary Jane, five children, ten grandchildren, his brother Homer Jr. and his sister Aileene. His funeral was held at Lake Worth, with interment planned at Lake Worth Memory Gardens; no cemetery register or individual marker has yet confirmed that the burial took place there.

Additional Quick-Fact Details

  • Military: U.S. Marine Corps, enlisted 17 January 1952 with his twin Earl; served in California, not sent overseas
  • Work: Consultant to printing firms, setting up new businesses, for over 35 years; supervisor at the Lantana Toll Plaza, Florida, in his last three years
  • Residence: Morgan County, Tennessee, 1940; Cincinnati, Ohio, 1950; Perrysville [probably Perryville], Missouri, until about 1989 (town spelling); West Palm Beach, Florida, 1989–1993
  • Conflicting family accounts about 17 Feb 1951: one says Elmer eloped with Ruth (age 14); another names Mary Jane as bride. The 22 Aug 1993 Palm Beach Post funeral notice establishes Mary Jane as his wife at death, but does not resolve the earlier account or prove two marriages. No marriage record has been located. The family identifies Ruth as mother of all five children; no independent record naming her as their mother is cited here.
  • Ruth's maiden name, Wainscott, is by her Find a Grave memorial and two online trees, but no record yet names her as Elmer's wife. A 1951 marriage record would settle it: try Campbell or Kenton County, Kentucky (teenage couples from Cincinnati often married across the river), and Hamilton County, Ohio.
  • Mary Jane's maiden name and their marriage date are not known.
  • Death date conflict: a GenealogyBank obituary index entry gives 17 August 1993. His obituary, the Florida death index and the Social Security indexes all give 21 August, which is used here.
  • Place of death: the records give his residence (West Palm Beach) and the county of death (Palm Beach); the exact place is not stated. Florida death certificate 95968 would settle it.
  • The original Palm Beach Post clipping prints “Perrysville, Missouri.” Perryville is a possible interpretation because it is the known Perry County county seat, but no independent address or residence record has established the town. Preserve the newspaper spelling until corroborated.
  • Burial: the newspaper says interment was to follow at Lake Worth Memory Gardens, but no cemetery interment register or individual marker has been independently located. Treat Lake Worth as the reported planned burial, not yet verified completed interment. A South Florida National Cemetery search did not produce an attributable match.
  • Military: his U.S. Marine Corps service record can be requested from the National Personnel Records Center.
  • Race is recorded differently in the indexes: "Indian" in the Florida death index and "White" in the Social Security Numident. Both are shown here exactly as indexed; neither is evidence of tribal identity.
  • Geni carries "(Twin)" in Elmer's name; it is shown here as a note, not part of his name.
  • The only photo of Elmer found so far is the one shared on Geni by Cathy Beth Burton; no headstone has been found. His obituary clipping is in the gallery.
