Life Story

Otto F. Graff was born 5 January 1880 in Tell City, Perry County, Indiana. The 1880 census lists him as 5 months old in his father’s household there, and the 1900 census and his 1903 marriage record also give Indiana as his birthplace, though his death certificate writes Kentucky. His parents are John Graf and Anna Albertine Zaugg; his death certificate names his father John Graff and his mother Anna. His older sister Clara was born in Lawrenceburg, Indiana, in 1874, which puts the family on the Ohio River border country in the 1870s.

He married Carrie Erion on 29 July 1903 in Hamilton County, Ohio, and the couple settled in Cincinnati. Their son Frank G. Graff was born in Cincinnati on 21 October 1906. The 1910 and 1920 censuses show the three of them together in Cincinnati, Frank aged 3 and then 13. By 1930 Otto was working as a machinist and living at 549 W McMicken Avenue, with Frank, a pressman, at the same address.

Otto died in Cincinnati on 21 December 1932, at 9:10 in the morning, and was buried at St. Mary Cemetery, St. Bernard, on 24 December 1932. His death certificate gives his occupation as brass worker. His wife Carrie outlived him by eleven years, dying in 1943.

Otto Graff Sr., born about 1902, is listed as a son on this profile.

Research Notes and Open Questions

23 November 2026 — a free-records search looked for Otto F.’s birth and death records, his marriage record, the 1900 and 1930 censuses, an obituary, and a burial. The birth record was not found, and the place of his birth stays open — Kentucky by the 1910 census, Indiana by the 1920 census. His death certificate, in the Ohio Deaths collection, has not been read; it would name his birthplace and his parents, and give his burial place. No obituary and no Find a Grave memorial were found.

His parents are named as John Graf and Anna Albertine Zaugg. No record read so far names them as his parents. His sister Clara’s burial record names their mother Anna Albertine Zaugg Graff, which ties the name to the family independently. John and Anna were married before 14 February 1874, when Clara was born in Lawrenceburg, Indiana, but the marriage record itself has not been found; Dearborn County, Indiana, is the likeliest place to look. Anna’s birthplace and immigration — Zaugg is a Swiss name — have not been found in any record.

Next: a signed-in search of the Ohio Deaths collection for Otto’s 1932 certificate; the 1903 marriage image; the 1900 census (Otto aged 20, likely with John and Anna) and the 1930 census; the Cincinnati birth cards at the University of Cincinnati for an 1880 birth; Clara Stautberg’s Find a Grave memorial and the other occupants of her Evergreen Cemetery plot; Dearborn County marriage records before 1874.

23 November 2026 — Otto’s Ohio death certificate has now been read in full. It names his father John Graff, born in Indiana, and his mother Anna, born in Indiana, with no surname given for her. It gives his birthplace as Kentucky, against Indiana in the 1880 census, the 1900 census, and his 1903 marriage record; the 1880 census lists him as 5 months old at Tell City, Indiana, in June 1880. He died 21 December 1932 at 9:10 in the morning at 549 W. McMicken Avenue, Cincinnati, and was buried at St. Mary Cemetery on 24 December 1932. The cause of death is only partly legible on the certificate and reads tentatively as chronic interstitial nephritis with cerebral embolism. The marriage record, also read, was 29 July 1903, Hamilton County, by Rev. Jacob Baxter; it spells his mother’s name “Anna Zauck” and gives his birthplace as Indiana and his occupation as carriage blacksmith. The 1880 census household at Tell City lists two older children, Caroline, 18, and Alfred, 17, both born in Switzerland before John’s 1873 marriage to Anna, so they are John’s children by his first marriage — should they be listed here as Otto’s half-siblings? The 1930 census entry for an “Otto F Graf” at Peru, Indiana, is a different man, born about 1881 to Henry and Mary Graf, and is not our Otto. The 1900 census sheet as read gives Conrad born February 1883, aged 17, against the 1880 census (Conrad 3) and the tree (29 April 1877); that reading may be wrong and is not settled. Clara’s Find a Grave memorial is still blocked and has not been read.
