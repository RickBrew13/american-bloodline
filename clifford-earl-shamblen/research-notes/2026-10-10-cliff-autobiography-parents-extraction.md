# Cliff's Autobiography — Parents Extraction (Arvel × Georgia), 2026-10-10

**Source:** "My Autobiography: Clifford Earl Shamblen" (written 2024; 24,973 words), full text read 2026-10-10.
**Informant:** Clifford Earl Shamblen (b. Jan 27, 1938, Fairplain WV), youngest of four children of Arvel Lawrence Shamblen × Georgia Mae Fisher Shamblen. Firsthand account — graded DOCUMENTED unless marked otherwise.
**Note:** Cliff calls his father "Pop" and his mother "Mimi" throughout. An earlier extraction (2026-10-07) covered the same autobiography; this note records what that extraction missed or under-detailed, plus corrections.

## Name and identity
- Pop's nickname origin is UNKNOWN even to Cliff: "I think the Pop nickname existed long before that. I'm not sure how and when he got it." (Ch1) — SUPPORTED as his uncertain memory.
- Mimi's nickname "Mimi" came from Greg Hoff (Betty's oldest son). (Ch1)
- All four children were born AT HOME. (Ch1)

## The Fairplain farm years (to ~1944)
- Farmhouse at the corner of Charleston Road and Given Road; ~8 miles from Ripley, ~30 miles from Charleston. (Ch1)
- Farm layout: farmhouse, corn crib, large barn, outhouse, cellar shed, equipment shed; large field where Shamblen's Run met Grass Lick Creek across Charleston Road; apple orchard across Given Road (mostly green sour apples, "had to be eaten with salt"); crop fields to the south, then hilly terrain. "Respectable size." (Ch1)
- House: no central heat; two fireplaces downstairs; David Clayton Shamblen lived in one side, the family in the other; no basement; cellar shed outside for food storage ("Refrigerators hadn't been invented yet"); hallway and stairs separated the two front living rooms; shared kitchen/dining across the back with hand-pumped well water ("very hard — iron, minerals"); everyone ate in the kitchen; outhouse; weekly baths in the kitchen with water heated on the wood stove — children shared the same bathwater. House later torn down; a new home was built where the outhouse had been. (Ch1)
- Shamblen's Run: ~200 yards south of the house, tributary feeding Grass Lick Creek; a "Shamblen's Run" nameplate once stood on the culvert/bridge carrying Charleston Road over it (gone now, but the run is still on Google Maps). (Ch1)
- Childhood games: bucket-on-head running races around the house (yard was fenced); cow rides; hayloft jumping. The three older children once stole a jar of canned peaches from the cellar shed and ate them in the corn crib — "didn't get into much trouble," Mother said they could have had peaches whenever they wanted. "I can't recall us ever being short on food" (post-Depression). (Ch1)
- Pop drove a Model-A Ford with tassels hanging around the windows; Cliff played with the tassels; as the youngest he sat up front with Mother. (Ch1)
- Georgia "didn't like the situation of living with Granddad Shamblen." (Ch1)

## Pop's work and the move to Charleston
- Pop learned carpentry from his father-in-law Verdi Fisher: "I can only imagine Granddad Fisher and Pop building things together. Neither spoke a lot." (Ch2)
- As a young carpenter Pop traveled out of state for work, "as far as Tennessee" (heard stories; graded SUPPORTED). Later hired as carpenter/millwright at DuPont in Belle, WV (~20 miles east of Charleston). Commute from Fairplain: 30 miles on Charleston Road to Charleston (through town via Washington Street), then 20 more miles to Belle. (Ch2)
- Family moved first to a rented house on a hill in Sissonville (~halfway to Charleston), then ~1944 (Cliff age 5–6) to 827 West Washington Street, Charleston — second house from the left. Mother "was just glad we had our own home." (Ch2)
- Pop was DRAFTED after the move to Charleston: his records were still at Fairplain, "and they had to draft some married men there. Had his record been switched to Charleston, he probably wouldn't have been drafted since there were many more unmarried men there." Basic training at Fort Knox, KY — the family visited him there (near Louisville). Mother also saw him "somewhere on the east coast just before he shipped off to Europe." (Ch2) — DOCUMENTED (Cliff's memory of the visits).

## Faith and church life
- "Mother was very religious and got us all in going to church weekly" — Calvary Baptist Church, corner of Maryland Avenue and Lee Street (<1/2 mile from the house; children walked). Mother was a Sunday School teacher (also Summer Bible school); Pop became a church usher LATER. (Ch2)
- Years later Cliff and his second wife visited Calvary Baptist one Sunday morning "and a couple of people still remembered Georgia and Arvel." (Ch2)
- Cliff on his parents' faith formation of him: "While Mother taught me to be a better person, Pop taught me how to build/repair things." (Ch6)
- Pop read the Bible DAILY at the nursing home (Cliff does the same now, "maybe for him and I it's recognizing we're getting older"). (Ch18)
- Appendix B (80th-birthday Q&A): "What do you remember most about your mom and dad? Mother – very religious. Pop – stoic, quiet, dry sense of humor." In an uncharacteristic move, Pop once "put an ice cube down the front of Gerry's blouse at a family picnic." (App B)

## The Charleston household
- 827 Washington Street: three upstairs bedrooms — parents in one, Betty in one, the three boys sharing the larger front bedroom (one bed against the unused fireplace). Hot nights: out the front window onto the porch roof. No AC; later Pop installed a window fan in the stairway window. Gas floor furnace between dining and living rooms; upstairs got cold in winter. (Ch2)
- Basement play area (small); always cats as pets — "dogs would get killed quickly trying to cross the busy Washington Street." Lowell and Cliff made cardboard box houses for the cats; "the cats never seemed to like them." Hobbies (8mm projector, printing press, crystal set) from a shop on Virginia Street. (Ch2)
- Basement flooded in heavy rain (water off the hill across the street backing up the drain); Pop fixed it with a trap in the joint driveway drain line — Cliff dug the trench, watching for the mailman hoping for a letter from Gerry. (Ch8)
- Food: after Pop acquired the farm he butchered cows and stored the meat (family-meal-size packages) at a local freezer company — "steaks, roast beef and the like for many meals each week," a couple of years running. Cliff "basically foundered on such meat products" and prefers fish/chicken/turkey to this day. One Christmas Pop bought cartons of chocolate covered cherries (24 boxes per carton, "a few cartons") — Cliff pigged out and can't stand them now; Betty still likes them. (Ch2)

## Money, work ethic, and raising children
- "Mother was the backbone of the immediate family since Pop was more the quiet type. Also, Pop only had an eighth-grade education; and he wasn't a proficient reader. While he handled most of the money matters, Mother had most of the say in taking care of us children and running the house." (Ch3)
- Mother took department-store jobs (the Diamond; Stone & Thomas, Charleston) "so she could buy us better clothes. She didn't think Pop provided sufficiently for that." Both stores now gone. (Ch3)
- Pop set up a $1,000 college promise FOR EACH of the children. Richard used his at Coyne; Cliff used his toward Cincinnati. (Ch7, Ch8)
- Pop's parenting lesson (re: letting Richard go to Coyne over his objections): "as a parent, sometimes you have to let your children make their own mistakes, because they will learn more from their own mistakes than from whatever you try to explain to them." (Ch7)
- Pop's construction philosophy: "lick and a promise" — "the job doesn't have to be perfect... if you take the time to make everything perfect, you probably won't make a sufficient profit." (Ch6)
- Pop taught Cliff carpentry, painting, plumbing, sheet-rock, floor sanding/staining (learned working on the Elizabeth Street apartments). (Ch6)
- Pop teased Cliff about going bald: "being a Fisher and getting bald as I got older." (Ch5)
- Mother's comment that damaged the family: "How will Clifford ever finish college if Gerry has a child every year?" — "I think it really destroyed their relationship" (with Gerry); Christmases in Charleston afterward were "very chilly." (Ch9)
- Appendix B: Mother was "a housewife, and then a store clerk in later years. Pop was a carpenter at Dupont, and he learned this trade from Grandpa Fisher." (App B)

## The farm as an investment
- Pop BOUGHT the Shamblen farm from his father David Clayton Shamblen with installment payments (Cliff learned this from Betty), "during the years we lived there, and shortly thereafter." Raised and sold a few cattle (8–12 cows); a renter lived in the farmhouse, tended farm and cattle — free rent plus ~$1.00/month pay. Later natural gas was discovered under the farm; a metered well went in; second income stream until he sold. Sale proceeds bought the Elizabeth Street rental property in east Charleston. (Ch3)
- Pop butchered farm cows for the freezer-company meat (above). (Ch2)
- Pop later added a third apartment unit to the Elizabeth Street house himself (working full-time at DuPont); sons Richard, Lowell, and Cliff helped — Pop first offered Cliff half pay, then raised him to full: "He said I worked as hard, and accomplished as much, as they did." (Ch6)

## Pop's three brushes with death (Cliff's phrase — "if I'm following Pop's pattern")
1. Ruptured appendix as a young man. (Ch6)
2. Floor-staining burns: staining floors with a gasoline-mixture stain at the Elizabeth Street apartments; forgot to turn off the water heater; fumes ignited; escaped by jumping out a window; weeks in hospital, several skin grafts. Cliff had left for Cincinnati days before and felt guilty; Mother said it was a blessing — "otherwise we both might have gotten burned, or worse." (Ch6)
3. Bowel obstruction after retirement. (Ch6)
- Died of a heart attack at his retirement home ("the Lord finally took him"). (Ch6)

## Family vacations and holidays (new)
- The family took vacations to Washington, DC and to Canada (Appendix B). DC: "delicious apple candy signs, but they bought some and threw it out because it wasn't good"; Lowell said his pie had hard crust "because he was eating the cardboard under it." Canada: "got lost finding the hotel... lots of stairs to the bedroom." (App B)
- Christmas: Cliff got a blue-and-white Roadmaster bike at age 7–8 for paper routes; live Christmas trees were cut on the farm. (App B)

## Sibling dynamics at home (for the parents' story)
- Richard and Betty were close (both teens, dating); Lowell and Cliff "likewise were together much of the time" — Lowell sometimes treated little brother as a nuisance. (Ch2)
- When Richard left home, Mother and Pop took the larger bedroom; Lowell and Cliff got the smaller one (steeper back-porch roof, harder to sneak onto). (Ch7)

## CONTRADICTIONS / FLAGS vs existing research
1. **Pop's siblings:** the current story draft lists Arvel's siblings as "Bevin Moody, Frankie Odell, Orpha Pearl, Roma LaFayette, and Sarah Louise" (five names for six claimed siblings). Cliff names SIX siblings: Frankie Odel (Parsons), Orpha Pearl (Casto), Moody, Rome, Beryl, and Louise (Foglesong) — and "Sarah Louise" is Arvel's MOTHER (Sarah Louise Staats), not a sibling. The draft's sibling list is wrong; Cliff's is firsthand. Name variants: Bevin Moody/Moody; Roma LaFayette/Rome. (Ch3)
2. **"Verdi" spelling:** Cliff consistently writes "Verdi Fisher" for his maternal grandfather; research/tree files use "Virdie Lee Fisher"; Cliff's own 2026-09-08 FB comment wrote "Verdie." Three variants on record — needs a primary record to settle. (Ch1, Ch2)
3. **Richard's birth year:** Cliff says "about eight years older than me" (~1930); records say Feb 4, 1932 (6 years older). Cliff's ages are approximate throughout ("about"). (Ch1, Ch7)
4. **Name order:** Cliff calls his brother "Lawrence Richard" throughout; official records say "Richard Lawrence." Same person per matching details. (Ch1, Ch7)
5. Pop's WWII service: Cliff says drafted, Fort Knox basic, shipped to Europe — no mention of Camp Atterbury; the old book says discharged through Camp Atterbury Dec 1945. Not contradictory (different ends of the service), but Cliff adds the draft circumstance and Europe.
