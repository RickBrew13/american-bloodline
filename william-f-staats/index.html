Life Story

William F. Staats was born in 1841 at Staats Mill, in Jackson County, Virginia, and he lived his whole life on the family land there — farming four miles south of Ripley, in the mill community his father's family had built its name on. The 1850 census catches him at ten years old in his father Isaac's household, and the next firm record is his marriage: on 3 June 1866 he married Emily Jane Casto, sixteen years old, daughter of Nicholas and Telitha Casto, in a wedding the Jackson County marriage index records with both couples' parents named.

Eight children followed between 1867 and 1883: Cordelia, Ava Belle, Sarah Louisa, Hollie Franklin, Enoch, Dora Anna, Cora Lee, and Edda D., who died at three years old and lies in Old Siniaville Cemetery. The children's lives spread out from the farm — Enoch became a teacher and then county clerk of Jackson County, and Enoch's son William Faye became mayor of Mullens, West Virginia, in 1965 — but William himself stayed on the home farm, a Methodist Protestant and, in the county history's phrase, an independent Democrat.

One line in that county history says William “was a member of the Home Scouts during the Civil war.” It is the only source that places him in any service, and no muster roll, roster, or veterans-schedule entry for him has been found under any spelling of the name, so this page claims no military service for him. The home-defense companies of Jackson County were real — the state adjutant general's 1863 report describes organized independent companies there, made of men who knew the country — but William's name is not in any free roster, and the one physical collection that might hold it, the West Virginia University microfilm of Union Civil War military records, has not been digitized. He died on the farm in 1914 and was buried beside Emily in Old Siniaville Cemetery, under a shared stone that reads “William ‘Father’ 1841–1914.”

Research Notes and Open Questions

  • How the grades work. DOCUMENTED means a record was read (a register, a certificate, a census image, a stone inscription); SUPPORTED means the claim rests on corroborating sources but no single primary record was read; FAMILY ACCOUNT means a compiled family book or county history is the source; UNPROVED means the claim is carried as a lead only, with no supporting record found.
  • Exact days not adopted. The 1921 genealogy gives William's birth as 15 August 1841 and his death day as 1 September, but the genealogy contradicts itself (its own entry reads 1911 where its children table reads 1914, and a later extract reads the table year as 1840), and no record gives either day. This page carries years only. The 1913 death year found in some accounts is contradicted twice, by the stone and by the 1923 county history, which agree on 1914.
  • What “F.” stands for is unknown. No record expands William's middle initial.
  • Home Scouts: unverified, not claimed. The claim rests on one line in the 1923 county history. Searches found no muster roll, no National Park Service entry, no Confederate record, and no 1890 veterans-schedule entry for William under Staats, Statts, Stots, Stutz, or Stoots; the 1863 West Virginia adjutant general's report contains zero Staats hits. The only route not yet tried is the West Virginia University microfilm of Union Civil War military records (1861–1865), which explicitly includes scouts, militia, and home-guard units but is not digitized. His father Isaac's “Home Guards” line in the same sketch is in the same position, and a July 1863 militia roster entry for Isaac in Captain Nicholas Casto's company, 141st Militia, stands at about 70 percent — evidence exists, attribution probable, not settled.
  • Do not conflate the three Isaacs. The Staats Mill Isaac (William's father) is not the Isaac of the 1921 genealogy who served in the 9th West Virginia Infantry and moved to Dayton, Ohio. And the 1923 sketch's founder Isaac (born 1799 in Rockingham County, died 1885) conflicts with the folder's Isaac (born about 1816, died 8 April 1890, aged 74) and with the genealogy's Isaac (born about 1803) — one man with contradictory dates, not three men, and the question belongs to his own pass.
  • Cornelius Staats and the War of 1812. House's 1906 history says Anna Carney's first husband Cornelius, son of Abraham Staats, was a soldier in the War of 1812 and was killed, leaving Isaac and Polly. No service record, pension file, or bounty-land entry has been found for him in any free index; the claim's existence is established, the service is not.
  • Second wife not adopted. FamilySearch carries “Elvira Post” as a second wife for William, with William A. Staats as her child, entered by other contributors; Geni also shows Elvira as a spouse. Nothing in the research folder supports either, and both are left untouched, not adopted.
  • Cordelia's Stout marriage not adopted. Geni lists George Floyd Stout as a second husband for Cordelia, and her Geni display name is “Cordelia Parsons-Stout.” The research folder found no support for a Stout marriage anywhere, so it is not carried on the card.
  • Ava Belle's husband and death year. The 1921 genealogy spells him Pfost and gives her death as 19 August 1906; the 1923 county history spells him Post and gives her death as 1900. Geni splits the difference — her own surname Pfost, her spouse Jacob Post, her children Boyd Pfost and Roy Elsworth Pfost. Neither spelling nor year is settled by a record.
  • Hollie's wife. The 1921 genealogy says Hollie married Pearl Knopp on 25 October 1896. Geni shows his wife as Anna Pearl Hill. Whether those are the same woman is unresolved; the card follows the genealogy.
  • Enoch's marriage year and the mayor's birth year. Enoch married Allie Carney in 1899 per the county history, 30 November 1894 per the genealogy — unresolved. His son William Faye Staats, mayor of Mullens in 1965 (confirmed in Political Graveyard, which also lists a 1952 run for the West Virginia House of Delegates), was born 12 November 1900 per the county history and 12 November 1901 per the genealogy — the 1900 year leads two sources to one but is still compiled-grade, and “Faye” itself is unverified.
  • Geni years that differ from the card. Geni gives Enoch 1876–1957 (card: born 7 October 1875), Cora 1880 with no death year (card: 11 November 1881–1964), Dora 1877–1938 (card: 31 March 1878; the 1938 death year matches a tree-level lead), Cordelia's death 1894 (card carries the 1898/1894 conflict), and Ava's death 1906 (card carries the 1900/1906 conflict). The card follows the genealogy and the county history, not Geni.
  • Edda D. Her stone stands in Old Siniaville Cemetery, Row 7, and the 1921 genealogy names her as the eighth child, but she matches no other named child and has no Geni profile; Find a Grave lists her (1883–1886) as a child on Emily's memorial. She is carried here unlinked, with her death year as 1886 or 1888.
  • No photographs of William or Emily have been found. The couple folder holds three contextual views — the Jackson County Courthouse at Ripley, the Staats Mill covered bridge, and a West Virginia gristmill — and none is shown here. No obituary for either spouse has been found, and the 1866 marriage register image itself has not been viewed.
  • Staats Mills naming. Three competing traditions explain the spelling Statts Mills: confusion with Stouts Mills in Gilmer County; a penmanship story in which Enoch Staats's handwritten “a” was read as “t” by the government; and the 1921 genealogy's own reversed claims. All three are unproved, and none is stated on the page.
