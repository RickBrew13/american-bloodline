Life Story

Samuel Paris McKinney was born 29 June 1822 in Virginia — the stone evidence (the Willie Mills Cemetery survey, an Oates stone quotation, and Find a Grave) agrees on the date, though the censuses run a year younger, and his birthplace is unresolved among Tazewell County, Giles County, and Barkers Ridge. He was a farmer at Barkers Ridge, Wyoming County, where the censuses find him from 1850 through 1880. He married Mary Elizabeth Dillion about 1843 — no record of the marriage has been found — and they raised twelve children there.

Land came after 1850: 35 acres in 1856, 1857, and 1858, then 35 acres at Barkers Ridge and 60 at Barkers Creek in 1859–60, by the land-tax lists. The 1850 census finds him 27, farming, with Elizabeth 29 and three small children; the 1860 household holds nine children; the 1870 household — found only after the index misfiled it under Wood County — holds Samuel 47 and Elizabeth 48 with nine children at home; the 1880 household holds seven. In February 1881 six of his sons joined the church together at the Barker's Ridge schoolhouse under Reverend Whitman, and three began preaching in 1883.

His parents are carried as Joseph Floyd McKinney × Martha Dillon, married 26 September 1822 in Tazewell County — the marriage is documented, but no record names Samuel as their son, so the link stands unproved. The 1940 family account calls him Joseph's eldest son, and the 1850 census puts father and son in adjoining households. A militia record exists for a Samuel P. McKinney — Private, Company G, 190th Regiment, Virginia Militia — but nothing in the index ties that man to this Samuel, so no service is attached to him here. The Find a Grave claim of Union service with an August 1861 Morgantown enlistment mixes that militia index entry with a different man's Union pension card.

He died 16 February 1898 at Barkers Ridge, aged 75, and is buried at Willie Mills Cemetery, Basin, beside Betsy — the stone evidence agrees on both dates, though no photograph of either stone has been found, and no death-register entry for him has been found. The only known photograph of either of them is the paired portrait carried here: the same image sits on both of their Find a Grave memorials and on Geni. The memorial caption names the woman as "Martha Dillon"; that is wrong — the woman is Betsy, his wife. Martha Dillon was his mother.

Research Notes and Open Questions

  • Grades on this page: DOCUMENTED means an original record was read; SUPPORTED means a claim attested by filed research — the census images, the marriage-register image, the cemetery survey, the signed-in tree checks — but not by an original record read here; UNPROVED means a claim carried from the research folder with no corroboration found.
  • Birth and death: the stone evidence (survey, Oates quote, Find a Grave) agrees on 29 June 1822 and 16 February 1898, while all three censuses run one year younger. The 1823 birth year and the 5 February 1898 death day are compiled minorities. His birth falls about three months before his parents' 26 September 1822 marriage — an open tension, with no record resolving it either way.
  • Birthplace: three-way and unresolved — Tazewell County, Giles County, or Barkers Ridge. The censuses say only "Virginia".
  • The parent link is UNPROVED: the 1940 family account and the 1850 census adjacency support it, and the trees all attach Joseph × Martha, but no record names Samuel as their son. The deciding record is his 1898 death-register entry, which an exhaustive West Virginia index search did not find; the county register images and a courthouse copy are the remaining routes.
  • Civil War: a militia record exists for a Samuel P. McKinney, Private, Company G, 190th Regiment, Virginia Militia, but the index gives no age or residence, so whether it is this Samuel is unproved and no service is attached to him here. The Find a Grave claim of Union service with an August 1861 Morgantown enlistment is a conflation of that militia index entry with a different Samuel's Union pension card (7th West Virginia Infantry, 1889). The 1890 Union veterans schedule is the remaining test. The family story of a Daniel Boone McKinney in the 190th cannot stand as told: the only Daniel Boone McKinney in this family was born 3 May 1861, aged 0 to 4 during the war.
  • Marriage, about 1843: no record found. The Tazewell registers for 1800-1852 are negative, and Logan County marriages before 1853 are not in the state index, so no state-index entry can exist for an 1843 Logan marriage. The remaining published route is Murray's Early Logan County West Virginia Marriage Records 1839-1892. The Logan marriage register itself is still unlocated: the film and DGS numbers once cited for it resolve in the FamilySearch catalog to a Pendleton County collection.
