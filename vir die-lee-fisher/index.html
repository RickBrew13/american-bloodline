Life Story

Virdie Lee Fisher was born at Fairplain in Jackson County, West Virginia, the son of James M. Fisher and Nancy Jane Casto, both named on his death certificate. His birth year is carried as 1884: his shared headstone and the 1939 Polsley genealogy give 25 February 1884, and both of his draft registration cards read the same date. An old compiled account gives 29 March 1882, and his death certificate's birth-year field reads 1881, though the certificate's own age of 79 years 9 months fits 1884 better.

He married Rhoda Alice Polsley on 4 June 1905 at the bride's residence in Jackson County, the license having been issued two days earlier. He farmed at Fairplain and Ripley all his life; his death certificate and his 1918 draft card both call him a farmer, while his grandson Cliff Shamblen remembered him as a carpenter who taught Arvel the trade. The Fisher farm was later sold to make room for Interstate 77.

Virdie and Rhoda had eight children, of whom Georgia Mae married Arvel Lawrence Shamblen. Virdie registered for both world war drafts but no military service is proved. He died on 9 December 1963 at Mt. State Hospital in Charleston and was buried three days later at Fairplain Cemetery, where a shared headstone names him and Rhoda. No portrait of Virdie alone has been found; the photograph above shows him with Rhoda late in life.

Research Notes and Open Questions

  • Birth: three-way conflict, carried open — 25 February 1884 (headstone, 1939 genealogy, and both draft cards), 29 March 1882 (an old compiled account), 25 February 1881 (his death certificate's birth-year field). The certificate's own age of 79 years 9 months fits 1884 better than 1881. The working date on this page and every tree site is 1884.
  • Middle name: his death certificate's name field reads "Virdie Lee Fisher" in full, so "Lee" is documented, correcting an older folder note that the records showed only the initial "L."
  • Occupation: farmer on the death certificate and the 1918 draft card; an old account's blacksmith claim is contradicted by the certificate; Cliff Shamblen remembered him as a carpenter. He may have done more than one trade.
