Life Story

Eunice Edna Brewster was born on December 22, 1930, in Cincinnati, Ohio, to Homer William Brewster Sr. and Sarah Hazel Kelly. She was their first child, born four months after their wedding, and the elder of their two daughters. In 1940 she was eight years old and living with her parents, four brothers and little sister in Morgan County, Tennessee. The National Archives describes her enumeration district, ED 65-15, as Civil District 12, “Brewstertown, Rugby.” That district included both named places, so the census index alone does not establish which settlement held the family’s home. The connection is nevertheless significant: Eunice’s paternal great-grandfather was James “Thomas” Brewster, whose descendants formed the historic Brewstertown settlement, according to Tennessee House Joint Resolution 5003 (1996).

Eunice married Avery E. Whittle on July 23, 1947, in Covington, Kentucky. The Campbell County marriage certificate names her parents as Homer and Sarah Brewster and records both parties as single. It gives her age as twenty-one and includes their sworn statement that they were twenty-one; her birth date shown on her records makes her sixteen. That conflict remains unresolved. A U.S. Department of Labor summary of Kentucky law in force at the time states that an unmarried applicant under 21 required parental or guardian consent. Kentucky archival guidance says those permissions were often kept as loose papers separate from the certificate. Eunice’s consent/application packet has not yet been located, so the age discrepancy remains open. In April 1950, the census places Avery and Eunice in the same Cincinnati household as husband and wife.

Later records about Avery identify a separate household he built with Charlotte Ann (Mulvey) Whittle in New Castle, Indiana. Their daughter Theresa’s 2016 obituary names Avery as her father, Charlotte as her mother, and six surviving siblings; Charlotte’s 2025 obituary says she and Avery raised eight children, including Becky, who died in infancy. These later family records document Avery’s subsequent family, not children of Eunice. They do not establish when Avery and Eunice’s marriage legally ended or when Avery married Charlotte.

Eunice died suddenly on December 16, 1987, at 56, less than ten months after her mother. The Hodapp Funeral Home of Carthage handled her funeral, and services were held at the Rose Hill Burial Park Chapel in Hamilton.
