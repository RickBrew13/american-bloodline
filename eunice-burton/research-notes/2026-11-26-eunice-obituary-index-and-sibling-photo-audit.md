## Ohio obituary-index routes
- Ohio History Connection's newspaper research guide specifically identifies **Newsdex — the Public Library of Cincinnati and Hamilton County** as an Ohio obituary index. It also advises that an obituary search should cover several days after the death and that a reproducible citation should include article title, newspaper, date, page and column. Eunice's 16 December 1987 death notice therefore needs a Newsdex search across approximately 16–22 December 1987, including spelling/name variants, and the exact citation must be recorded from the issue rather than inferred from the family clipping.
- The R. B. Hayes Ohio Obituary Index is described by Ohio libraries as indexing millions of Ohio obituaries, death/marriage notices and related sources from the 1810s to the present; actual copies may be requested from partner libraries. This is a second distinct index to query for Eunice, with variants Brewster, Whittle, Burton and Taylor.
- Hamilton County Genealogical Society's public databases include a Master List of Hamilton County Burial Sites and a Marriage Banns Index for 1900–1949. Its members-only collections include Weil Funeral Home records and a master will index. These may help with the known Rose Hill burial and possible marriage/estate leads, but the website does not itself confirm an Eunice entry in any of them. The Weil records are not the same funeral home as the already suspected Hodapp arrangement file, so should not be treated as proof of her funeral provider.

- **1950 census detail, enumerated 10 April 1950, Hamilton County, Cincinnati:** Avery Whittle, head, age 23, born Kentucky, occupation gardener at a distillery; Eunice Whittle, wife, age 19, born Ohio, occupation candy packer at a wholesale grocery. This directly establishes the couple's residence and Eunice's employment at that date. It does not establish when their marriage ended.
- **1987 family death notice:** the clipping names Eunice E. (née Brewster), wife of Irvin Burton; children Demetria Sexton, Elaine Bishop and Anthony Q. Burton; grandchildren Michael and Melissa Sexton, Alicea and Brandy Bishop; parents Homer and late Sarah Brewster; siblings Aileen Gilpin, Elmer and Homer Brewster, and late Earl and Eugene Brewster. It gives death as 16 December 1987, age 56, residence Hamilton, and names Hodapp Funeral Home, 7401 Vine St., Carthage, with service at Rose Hill Burial Park Chapel, 2421 Princeton Park Chapel, Hamilton. This clipping itself is family-held primary evidence, but its newspaper title/date/page/column have not been identified. The listed 2421 address is retained as printed; do not silently substitute the current Rose Hill street address.
- The 1950 sheet's age of 19 in April 1950 and the death notice's age 56 in December 1987 are consistent with a birth in late 1930/early 1931. The certificate gives age 21 in July 1947, which is consistent with birth after July 1925 and before July 1926 only if the handwritten age were taken literally; that conflicts with the census and death notice. The 1947 certificate's recorded age is therefore a notable age discrepancy that needs a birth certificate/SS-5 to resolve. Do not calculate an exact birth date from any of these ages alone.


## Additional occupation, directory and obituary-index research — 26 November 2026

### Nursing occupation: historical context
The Ohio Board of Nursing's historical summary states:
- Ohio's first Nurse Practice Act was enacted in 1915; the first nurses were registered in January 1916.
- The State Nurses Board became autonomous in 1941.
- Practical-nurse licenses began in 1956.
- Ohio did not make licensure mandatory for registered and practical nurses until 1967.
This matters because a woman recorded as a registered nurse in 1987 may have trained and begun working before mandatory licensure. It is not safe to assume that a searchable modern license number or a single county registration certificate must exist. The Ohio History classification still lists OY N 77.15/2 “Nurse inventory” under the 1955–1988 Board of Nursing Education and Nurse Registration, but the description does not say whether it is a name-level roster or an administrative statistical inventory. Request a description and access check before treating it as a likely personal file.
