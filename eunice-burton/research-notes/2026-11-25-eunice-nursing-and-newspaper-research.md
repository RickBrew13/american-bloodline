## Ohio nursing-record series located
A further search of official Ohio archival descriptions identified a specific state-level source series that had not been named in the earlier notes:

- Ohio's State Board of Nursing became autonomous in 1941; the Board's name changed to the Nursing Education and Nurse Registration Board in 1955 and to the Ohio Board of Nursing in 1988.
- Ohio's first statewide nurse inventory was published in 1973.
- The Ohio state government records classification identifies the series **OY N 77.15/2, Nurse inventory**, under the Nursing Education and Nurse Registration (State) Board (1955–1988).
- The Board's licensure database became computerized in 1986, according to the Ohio Nurses Association's published history.
- Nursys states that licenses that expired before 1985 may not appear in its database and directs users to contact the state board for missing records.

These findings make two distinct record requests appropriate: (1) a search of the archived 1973 nurse inventory and later inventories for Eunice E. Brewster / Whittle / Burton / Taylor; and (2) a request to the Ohio Board of Nursing for the historical licensure registry/application file, including any original education-program, license-issue, renewal and name-change entries. The current eLicense lookup is not a reliable negative test for a deceased nurse whose license may have expired before 1985. No individual inventory entry or license record for Eunice has yet been located; the profession remains supported by the 1987 Ohio death-index occupation field, not by an inspected license record.

## 1947 marriage-age rule — legal context clarified
The original Kentucky marriage certificate is still the controlling record for what the clerk recorded: Eunice and Avery each stated they were 21 and single. Her independently supported birth date, 22 December 1930, makes her 16 on 23 July 1947.

The contemporaneous U.S. Department of Labor Women's Bureau report, *The Legal Status of Women in the United States: January 1, 1948 — Kentucky*, is based on the Kentucky Revised Statutes 1946 and explains the rule then in force: if either party was under 21 and not previously married, a license could not issue without the consent of the minor's father or guardian (or, under the stated circumstances, the mother's consent). The report separately says a female under 14 was below the statutory marriage minimum; Eunice, at 16, was above that minimum. Thus her age alone did not make the marriage automatically void, but parental/guardian consent was required if she was in fact 16 and previously unmarried. No consent document has been found in the archived certificate or the searches so far. Do not assume whether consent was filed, whether the clerk relied on the sworn age statement, or whether the marriage was legally invalid.

Source: U.S. Department of Labor, Women's Bureau Bulletin 157-16 (Revised), *The Legal Status of Women in the United States of America: January 1, 1948 — Report for Kentucky* (1949), section II, item 18, pp. 8–9: https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/files/docs/publications/women/b0157-16-rev_dolwb_1949.pdf


## Avery–Charlotte marriage and Avery–Eunice divorce: Indiana archive route
The follow-up on Avery's later family located a more precise county-level research route. The official Indiana Archives and Records Administration states that statewide marriage recording began in 1958; before that, records were kept at county level, with some county collections digitized and incomplete. The Indiana State Library's Henry County microfilm inventory specifically lists:
- Marriage Record, volumes 35–36, 1942–1945 (microfilm M-20 / GSU 1887832)
- Marriage Record, volumes 37–38, 1945–1947 (M-21 / GSU 1887833)
- Marriage Record, volumes 39–40, 1947–1949 (M-22 / GSU 1887834)
- Marriage Record, volumes 41–42, 1949–1951 (M-23 / GSU 1887835)

The Henry County Public Library Indiana Room holds New Castle city directories, a full Courier-Times card index for 1848–1989, local newspaper microfilm, and a marriage index (1850–1920). These are relevant because Charlotte's 2025 obituary says she and Avery built their home and raised eight children in New Castle, Indiana, but does not state their marriage date. No Avery–Charlotte marriage record or Avery–Eunice divorce decree was retrieved in this online pass. The Henry County clerk is the custodian for original county marriage/divorce records; Indiana has no single statewide divorce index for all years.
