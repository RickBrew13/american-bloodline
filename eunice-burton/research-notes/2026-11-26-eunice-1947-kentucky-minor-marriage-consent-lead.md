# Eunice Edna Brewster — 1947 Kentucky minor-marriage consent lead
**Research date:** 26 November 2026  
**Scope:** Eunice's 23 July 1947 marriage to Avery E. Whittle. Her own archive and original certificate were reviewed first.

## Direct evidence and age conflict
The original Campbell County marriage certificate (book 196, page 83; FamilySearch image 3:1:3Q9M-C91S-2QLP-2) states:
- Avery E. Whittle, age 22, single.
- Eunice Brewster, age 21, single.
- Both gave Cincinnati, Ohio, as their residence; Eunice's birthplace is written as Hamilton County, Ohio.
- The form states, “Both being duly sworn state they are twenty-one years of age,” and bears Eunice's and Avery's signatures.
- The marriage date is 23 July 1947; the place is Covington, Kentucky.

Eunice's Social Security NUMIDENT and SSDI index give her birth as 22 December 1930. On 23 July 1947 she was 16 years, 7 months old, not 21. Her 1950 census age (19 on 10 April 1950) is consistent with the 1930 birth date. The certificate's sworn age statement is therefore materially inconsistent with her birth record/index. The birth date is supported by federal SSA index records and the headstone, but the original birth certificate and original SS-5/Numident image have not yet been inspected.

## Why a separate consent record may exist
The U.S. Department of Labor Women's Bureau publication *The Legal Status of Women in the United States of America: January 1, 1948 — Report for Kentucky* states that if either party was under 21 and had not previously been married, a marriage license could not be issued without the consent of the minor's father or guardian (or, in the specified circumstances, the mother's consent). It cites Kentucky Statutes §402.210. This is near-contemporary legal evidence for the rule applicable to the 1947 marriage.

The original certificate records Eunice as single. If her 22 December 1930 birth date is correct, she was under 21 and the marriage record should be checked for a parental/guardian consent or related application papers. Kentucky marriage records can consist of separate bonds, consents/permissions, licenses, certificates, ministers' returns and register entries; the existence and survival of separate papers varies by county and year. Kentucky's retention schedule identifies parental permissions as a permanent county-clerk record series, but that schedule does not prove that this specific consent survives.

A second issue also needs examination: both parties' residence is recorded as Cincinnati, Ohio, while the license/certificate is in Campbell County, Kentucky. Kentucky's licensing-jurisdiction rules changed over time; the applicable 1947 version of KRS 402.080 should be compared to the original application before drawing any conclusion about why Campbell County issued the license. The age 21 sworn statement may have affected how the clerk treated the application, but that remains a hypothesis.

## Specific records to locate
1. Original marriage application/affidavit for Avery E. Whittle and Eunice Brewster, 23 July 1947.
2. Any parental or guardian consent/permission filed with the application, including a separate loose paper.
3. Any marriage bond, license stub, register entry, minister's return or clerk's application index associated with book 196, page 83.
4. The original county clerk's record image at a higher resolution, to confirm all handwriting and any reverse-side annotations.
5. The 1947 version of KRS 402.080 and KRS 402.210, including amendments effective by 23 July 1947.

The FamilySearch image currently archived in Eunice's folder is the certificate page only. No consent or separate application has yet been located in the archive.

## Evidence boundary
This is a high-priority record lead, not proof that a consent was filed or that the marriage was invalid. Do not characterize the event as a fraudulent marriage or state that her parents approved/refused until the original application/consent and the law effective in July 1947 are checked. No clerk or archive has been contacted and no record ordered.
