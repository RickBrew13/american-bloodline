## Ohio nursing registration — more precise record-request basis
- Rechecked the Ohio Board of Nursing's current public guidance. Ohio eLicense recommends searching with only the nurse's last name and first initial, selecting the Nursing Board; the guidance warns that adding more fields can prevent a match. The names to test remain Burton/E, Brewster/E, Whittle/E and Taylor/E. The interactive lookup did not expose a usable result in this session, so no positive or negative license result is claimed.
- Located the Board's official public-records request form. It asks for a description of records and provides the Board's Reynoldsburg address and phone. The Board's current contact page lists 8995 East Main Street, Reynoldsburg, OH 43068 and 614-466-3947.
- Rechecked the historical version of Ohio Administrative Code 4723-1-03 (2002). It specifies that the Board's registry included the applicant/licensee's name, nursing education program, method and date of licensure, and (where Ohio was the original state) examination results. The current 2026 rule still requires the Board to maintain records of applicants and license holders, though it no longer lists the fields in that paragraph. This gives a concrete request target: a name-variant search of the historical RN registry and any surviving application, education-program, initial-license, renewal and name-change records.
- These sources establish the record series and a request route, not that an individual file for Eunice survives or is publicly releasable. No request was submitted.

## 26 November 2026 — Ohio State Archives nurse-inventory series identified
A more specific archival lead has now been identified in the Ohio state-government classification schedule. It lists the State Board of Nursing Education and Nurse Registration (1955–1988) under record group **OY N 77**, and specifically lists **OY N 77.15/2 as “Nurse inventory.”** This is distinct from the general Board registry and the county probate certificate books. It may be a published or administrative inventory rather than a name-level license file; the catalog classification alone does not reveal its contents, years, or whether individual nurse names appear. It should not be assumed to be a searchable list of licensees until the finding aid or actual material is inspected.

The Ohio History Connection Archives & Library is the state archive custodian/research contact. Its current guidance says most state and local public records are paper or microfilm, not searchable online; researchers may need to inspect them on site. The current reading room is appointment-only, with contact voicemail 614-297-2510 at 800 E. 17th Avenue, Columbus, Ohio 43211. A precise inquiry should ask whether OY N 77.15/2 survives, its date coverage, whether it indexes individual nurses, and whether a name search for Eunice Edna Brewster / Eunice E. Whittle / Eunice Taylor / Eunice E. Burton can be conducted or whether the user must inspect it in person. This is a targeted next step, not evidence that Eunice appears in the inventory.
