Life Story

Permelia A. Whitaker was born about 1814–1815. Where she was born is not settled: the 1850 census says Virginia, the 1860 census says Kentucky, and the 1870 and 1880 censuses say Indiana. Her father was John Whitaker, who died by June 1825; the court orders that year call her his orphan. Her mother is unknown. In June 1825 the court named Alexander Whitaker her nearest friend, and in July she was bound to James Wood until she turned sixteen. James Wood had married Margaret Hunt, the sister of Gasham Davis's mother — so the man she was bound to was her future husband's uncle by marriage.

She married Gasham Davis about 1837; no marriage record has been found. They lived on the Muddy River, where Gasham farmed and kept the ferry. After his death in April 1847 she stayed on as a widow and farmer in her own name through four censuses, and never married again. In 1856 she was granted 45 acres carved from Gasham's 150-acre tract, held in trust for her two youngest children, Squire Harper and Lydia Margaret; in January 1890 she sold it, keeping the right to live on the place for the rest of her life.

She was last seen in the records in that 1890 deed, recorded in January 1891. No death record or burial has been found; she is carried as dying about 1891–92. Five of her children are documented or supported, and a sixth, Charity A., is listed with her parentage unproved. The middle initial "A." is never spelled out in any record.

Research Notes and Open Questions

  • How to read the grades: DOCUMENTED means a record was read directly. SUPPORTED means the evidence points that way but no single record proves it. FAMILY ACCOUNT means it comes from the family's own telling. UNPROVED means it is a lead only, with no record behind it yet.
  • Birthplace: not settled. The records say Virginia (1850 census), Kentucky (1860 census; John P.'s 1862 marriage; Squire's 1866 certificate, which says his mother was born in Muhlenberg), and Indiana (1870 and 1880 censuses) — Kentucky 3, Indiana 2, Virginia 1. No record before 1870 says Indiana; the Indiana reading rests on the elopement story and the two latest censuses.
  • Mother: unknown. Three names have been suggested and none is proved: "Nancy?" (chart only); Mary McCartey (chart, married 22 December 1780, Rowan County, North Carolina — the bond is unchecked); Caty Hargroves (one tree's say-so only, with no record behind it).
  • Bound to James Wood, not Isaac Wood: the 1825 court orders name James Wood; Anderson's "Isaac" is wrong on this point. James Wood married Margaret Hunt, Gasham's aunt. Rothert's 1913 county history says James Wood's wife was Susan — against the marriage register's Margaret Hunt; that wrinkle is unresolved.
  • Alexander Whitaker, "nearest friend": the 1825 order names no kinship. Whether he was an uncle, an older brother, or another relation is unproved.
  • Death: about 1891–92 is inference from the 6 January 1891 recording date only; no death record or burial has been found. Gardner (Davis) Cemetery is the likeliest unmarked resting place — a lead, not a finding.
  • Middle initial: "A." is never expanded in any record. "Ann" appears nowhere attached to her and is not carried even as a guess.
  • Children: five are documented or supported — John P., Ransom, Enos, Squire Harper, Lydia Margaret. Charity A. is listed with her parentage unproved: her marriage is documented, but no record names her parents, and she was born about 1835, before the 1837 wedding. Nancy J. is not a daughter: the 1848 marriage register names the bride Nancy J. Mairs. The "Susan Davis, 5" in the 1850 census is read as Squire, mis-recorded, not a separate child.
