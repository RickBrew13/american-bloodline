Life Story

Mathias Henry Novak was born about the 1830s — the year is an estimate only, inferred from his daughter Theresa's birth in 1863; no birth record exists, and no record names his parents or his birthplace beyond Perjamosch, the family's village in the Banat. He is known in America as Henry Novak: his daughter Theresa's Ohio death certificate names her father Henry Novak, while the family-book data behind Dreyer's ship-list annotation names him Mathias Novak. The two forms are one man, Henry the middle name, and are never split into two men — a working decision, not a read record. No photograph of Mathias has been found.

About 1862, presumably at Perjamosch, Mathias married Elisabeth Schisler, born about 1840 — the marriage is unproved, no record survives, and it falls in the gap after 1842 where the Perjamosch registers end. Their one child is Theresa, born at Perjamosch on 30 April 1863. She married Johann Lorch, emigrated to Cincinnati in 1907, and died there on 27 August 1937. Her daughter Margaret married Peter Palmer; she is the direct line.

No record shows Mathias ever leaving Hungary, and no death or burial record has been found for him. No occupation or military service is recorded for him. He was Roman Catholic, in the parish context of Perjamosch.

Research Notes and Open Questions

  • Grades on this page: DOCUMENTED means an original record was read; SUPPORTED means a claim attested by filed research — the FamilySearch indexes, Dreyer's ship-list extractions, the signed-in tree checks — but not by a register read here; UNPROVED means a claim carried from the research folder with no corroboration found.
  • Parentage: Theresa's link to Mathias Novak × Elisabeth Schisler is supported, not proved — it rests on ONE compiled annotation, Dreyer's ship-list entry for Theresia Lorch, whose bracketed note names her as the daughter of Mathias Novak and Elisabeth Schisler. The bracketed note is drawn from Krämer's Perjamosch family book, not from manifest text, and no register image has been read for her 1863 baptism — the Perjamosch registers on FamilySearch end in 1842.
  • One man: "Mathias" (Dreyer) and "Henry" (Theresa's death certificate) are one man, Henry the middle name, never split into two men. This is a working decision of the family research, not a read record — no record names a second Novak man, and no Henry or Heinrich Novak appears anywhere in the 911-record Perjamosch roster. The certificate image itself names the father "Henry Novak"; it does not name a Mathias.
  • The 10-02 claim, resolved: an earlier note said the ~1890–92 marriage register names Theresa's father as Henry Novak. That was a misattribution — a garbled paraphrase of two Dreyer brackets — and no Johann × Theresa marriage record exists. No marriage-register attestation for "Henry Novak" is carried as a fact.
  • The document's refuted claim: the couple's document in the research folder still carries "Elisabeth's parents were Nikolaus Schisler × Maria Anna Hus". That is refuted: the Perjamosch Nikolaus's children were born 1878–1886, a generation below Elisabeth (born about 1840) — he was born about 1845–55, roughly fifteen years after her. The document was read, not edited, and stays as written; these pages carry the corrected facts. The couple Nikolaus × Maria Anna Hus is itself compiled-documented; only the link to Elisabeth is refuted.
  • Slot #15 — Elisabeth's father: a candidate field, none of it clears the bar. The Tschanad Nikolaus Schisler, born 1826, is now very unlikely as her father — he would have been 14 for a daughter born about 1840 (and about 1840 is itself an estimate, so this is "too young", not a flat impossibility). Paul Schisler of Perjamosch × Maria Anna Arnold is a documented Schisler householder in Perjamosch in the parent generation, but their daughter Elisabeth, born 1841, died in 1845 — a second surviving Elisabeth by the same parents is speculation. Adam Schiszler, born 1825 at Tschanad, has the same generation problem (he would have been about 15 for a daughter born about 1840). The NEW lead is Nicolaus Schisler × Agnes Schuhmacher ("Agn. Schuman") of Perjamosch, whose children were born 1825–1831 — their daughter Margareth's baptism is documented (FamilySearch film 858461, image 674 of 959). They are UNPROVED as Elisabeth's parents: a child born about 1840 falls after the 1835 baptism cutoff, so no baptism for Elisabeth herself can be checked in that film.
  • Slot #14 — Mathias's parents: unknown. No candidate couple exists in any source held. The Glogon family (Petrus Novak × Eva Schubert, whose son Mathias was baptized 9 February 1809) is unlikely, not excluded — the 17 February 1809 death carried for him has no source at all, and a pattern of infant mortality is not a record. He would also have been about 54 at Theresa's birth in 1863, and Glogon is a different parish from Perjamosch. Decider: the Glogon burial register for February 1809, or the Glogon family book.
  • The 61st Infantry Regiment book: the ~1862 marriage is not in it (FamilySearch film 1454300, images 500–503 read — no 1860 or 1862 marriages at all). Genuine negative. The compilation's abstracts contain no Schisler groom or bride either; the original regimental films remain a possible route.
